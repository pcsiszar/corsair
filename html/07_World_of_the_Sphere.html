Worlds of the Sphere

The Setting at a Glance

Corsair is a tabletop roleplaying game of high-stakes frontier adventure, mystery, and intrigue, blending the mythic wonder of Space Fantasy with the grounded weight of Hard Science Fiction. Set within the Sphere—a breathtaking, macro-engineered solar system featuring more than a hundred habitable worlds locked in tight binary orbits around colossal gas giants—the setting pairs ancient cosmic dragons, forgotten alien vaults, and diverse lineages with the tangible reality of sub-light orbital mechanics and kinetic weaponry.

You and your crew play a Corsair Cell: licensed, independent operatives flying a nimble starship with a cross-jurisdictional charter. When planetary law ends at the clouds and the naval peacekeepers are light-minutes away, your sponsors call on you. Whether investigating corporate sabotage in an orbital arcology, braving hazardous Precursor ruins in search of lost technology, unmasking political conspiracies, or negotiating tense standoffs across a fraying frontier, your crew stands as the thin line holding a young civilization together.

Five hundred years ago, automated seed arks brought the survivors of four dying worlds into the Sphere. Rather than a naturally accreted stellar system, the Sphere is a marvel of celestial engineering: concentric rings of massive gas giants locked in gravitational harmony around a quiet Orange Dwarf star.

Within the magnetic shelters of these gas giants, terrestrial worlds orbit in close-knit binary pairs. Separated by mere tens of thousands of kilometers, neighboring moons hang like colossal jewels in each other's skies. Yet for all its mythic grandeur, everyday survival in the Sphere is governed by strict, tangible physical laws:

  • No Faster-Than-Light Travel: Interplanetary transit relies entirely on continuous-thrust fusion engines burning along calculated Brachistochrone trajectories. Hopping between twin binary moons takes mere hours, while journeys across the deep void between neighboring gas giants take only a few days.
  • No Artificial Gravity: Stations and ships obey Newtonian physics. Gravity exists only when an engine is under thrust or when an orbital station's habitat rings are spinning. Everywhere else, life is defined by magnetic boots, safety tethers, and the silent float of microgravity.
  • Kinetic and Thermal Realities: In place of energy shields or disintegrator beams, ships and ground operatives rely on layered composite armor, point-defense lasers, and ballistic firearms. Every burning engine and fired weapon generates waste heat that must be carefully radiated into the vacuum through glowing thermal arrays.
  • The Living Dragons: The titans of the void are not mere myths, but colossal, ancient biological entities that navigate the vacuum using internal bio-fusion dynamos. From gentle Goldheart watching over Landfall to the thunderous Stormdrinker circling the electrical storm-wells of Nimbus, dragons are sovereign beings whose presence and alliances shape the fate of entire worlds.

Ten Lineages, Two Philosophies

Newcomer civilization is composed of ten distinct lineages, reimagining classic fantasy archetypes through an evolutionary and biological lens. All trace their ancestry back to the four original seed arks: the adaptable Humans, the crest-bearing and cold-blooded Naga, the dense-skinned and crystalline-eyed Craggen, and the tall, chlorophyllic Sylvan.

Over five centuries of shared survival on Landfall and across the moons, six natural hybrid lineages took root: the draconic Drakelings, graceful Elves, ingenious Gnomes, stout and enduring Dwarves, nimble Kobolds, and towering Trolls. Yet, as the species intermingled, a profound philosophical rift divided Newcomer society:

  • Integrationism: The dominant cultural current, championed in metropolitan melting pots like Crossroads and Cloudspire. Integrationists believe that cultural blending, shared destiny, and inter-lineage solidarity represent the true future of the Sphere.
  • Preservationism: A traditionalist movement born from filial reverence for lost homeworlds. Preservationists maintain insular, single-lineage settlements on the outward frontiers, dedicated to keeping ancestral bloodlines, cultural rituals, and ancient languages pure to honor their forgotten ancestors.

The Law of the Void

Order across the populated moons is governed by the Skyline Accords, a historic treaty ratified in 335 AA that divided civilization into two distinct realms of legal authority:

  1. The Sovereign Moons: Planetary governments hold total jurisdiction within their atmospheric gravity wells, enforcing their own local laws, taxation, and colonial charters.
  2. The Flotilla: A massive, neutral peacekeeping fleet that holds supreme jurisdiction over open space, orbital transfer corridors, Lagrange stations, and void shipping lanes.

While this separation prevented civil war, it created a dangerous legal seam. Smugglers, corporate raiders, rogue mercenaries, and political conspirators routinely commit crimes in planetary skies and burn into orbit, daring local authorities to chase them past their legal boundaries.

The Skyline Seam: Why Dual Jurisdiction Exists

The division was not born of idealism, but exhaustion. Following the Drift Wars in the Fume rings, colonial leaders refused to surrender orbital sovereignty to the Flotilla, while the Admiralty refused to police squabbling planetary cities. The resulting boundary—the "Karman Verge"—became both a peace line and a haven for organized crime.

The New Wave: Your Role as Corsairs

To bridge this jurisdictional void, the Flotilla and colonial administrations established the Corsairs—autonomous specialist crews granted cross-jurisdictional mandates to operate seamlessly from planetary mud to the cold vacuum of deep orbit.

The year is 518 AA. Exactly one decade ago, the apocalyptic Frontier War against the Orkish Empire and the catastrophic fall of the ancient alien super-dreadnought Evergaol shattered the Flotilla's veteran battle fleet. With naval hulls in short supply and the frontier fraying under piracy, corporate land-grabs, and border skirmishes, the High Council has issued emergency charters to recruit a New Wave of Corsairs.

The Corsair Cell

As players, you form a Corsair Cell—a tight-knit crew of 3 to 5 specialists operating aboard an agile, multi-role starship. Every cell is unique: backed by multiple Sponsors (such as a naval officer, a governor, a corporation, or an independent union) to preserve operational neutrality. Supervised by an Oversight Committee of retired veteran Corsairs and civilian delegates, your cell enjoys total freedom of approach across the void.

The Corsairs of the Sphere

The Corsair system was established to manage jurisdictional friction between the Flotilla and the Sovereign Moons. It provides a mechanism for cross-jurisdictional operations in cases where local laws and orbital regulations overlap. Corsairs are operatives granted a cross-jurisdictional mandate to operate in both planetary and orbital environments.

Background and Origins

The Corsairs originated as a specialized division within the Patrol Wing following a landmark legal decision that established their jurisdiction to operate seamlessly across both orbital space and planetary surfaces. Initially, their mandate was focused on cross-jurisdictional investigations and bounty hunting. However, as the system proved its utility, both the Flotilla and the planetary colonies began offloading other critical duties. Today, Corsair cells are regularly tasked with localized law enforcement, reconnaissance, search and rescue, and recovery operations—missions ideally suited for a small, versatile crew.

The Sponsors

Behind every Corsair, there is a major power in the Sphere that sponsored their entry into the organization. The system could not exist without the sponsors who grant the means for it to operate, both in terms of granting them legal jurisdiction and in terms of funding their existence.

Being a sponsor grants you the right to bring matters to the attention of the Corsair Cells. When you need a perceived injustice or a threat investigated or handled, you can directly petition the organization to act. In exchange, sponsors are required to fund the entry of at least one member into the Corsairs, the majority of which is for the ship they will be operating from. Sponsors handpick the individuals who become Corsair agents and provide their initial funding.

Furthermore, sponsors gain a voice in the organization's governance. Representatives from each sponsor sit on the Sponsor Board, allowing them to vote on broader decisions that concern the organization's overall policies, budget, and leadership.

The Oversight Committee

To prevent sponsors from wielding the Corsairs as personal weapons or using them to advance partisan agendas, the organization is supervised by the Oversight Committee. This body is composed of former Corsair agents with exemplary service records, alongside civilian officials approved by the Sponsor Board. The Committee's role is twofold:

  • Contract Review: They evaluate all contracts submitted by sponsors to ensure they serve the public interest and the common good, filtering out requests that are purely self-serving or malicious.
  • Internal Investigations: They investigate allegations of misconduct or inappropriate behavior by active Corsair agents, holding the authority to hand down disciplinary actions or strip agents of their mandate if found guilty.
  • Audit of Arms & Munitions: They maintain continuous remote monitoring of high-yield ordnance, boarding charges, and restricted telemetry arrays deployed to licensed hulls.
The Charter Token & Verification Protocol

Every licensed Corsair vessel carries a cryptographic Charter Token hardwired into its primary comms transponder. When challenged by local planetary militia or Flotilla patrol cruisers, broadcasting the token initiates an automated handshake with the nearest relay beacon, confirming the crew's legal clearance to transit airspace, carry combat armor, and execute warrants without interference.

The Corsair Cell: The Fundamental Unit

The Corsair system is decentralized and operates through the Corsair Cell. Each cell is a small, autonomous group of 3 to 5 specialists based on a single, high-performance vessel.

This small-scale structure is a strategic choice: a single vessel can operate in crowded spaceports like Pelagia, dock at Nimbus refineries, or land at fringe outposts on Kazahn without the political implications of a military deployment. This versatility allows cells to handle investigations and tactical operations that would otherwise be complicated by interplanetary bureaucracy.

To ensure neutrality, every cell is a mandatory collaboration between different Sponsors. Candidates are typically placed into cells with mixed sponsorships. This structure provides a self-policing environment; because members represent different interests, the cell is discouraged from serving any single power's exclusive agenda, enforcing their impartiality.

Cell Operational Roles

While every cell tailors its operational dynamic to its members' talents, most balanced crews organize into five key operational functions:

  • Commander / Negotiator: Manages legal warrants, charter compliance, sponsor negotiations, and diplomatic de-escalation under pressure.
  • Helm / Astrogator: Plots Brachistochrone burns, calculates orbital slingshots, manages propellant reserves, and maneuvers in tight dockings.
  • Tactical / Point Defense: Operates fire-control computers, manages kinetic coilguns, and coordinates intercept geometry against hostile bogeys.
  • Breaching Specialist: Wields MANTLE rigs in zero-g boarding actions, CQB clearing, structural cutting, and physical site security.
  • Systems Engineer / Tech: Calibrates drive plumes, maintains life support scrubbers, radiates reactor heat, and overrides security locks.

Recruitment and Selection

Becoming a Corsair agent is a prestigious but difficult path. There are two primary avenues for entering the organization:

The Institutional Path

Candidates can enlist directly with a major sponsor—such as the Flotilla or a colonial law enforcement agency—and work their way through the ranks. Outstanding performance, dedication, and recommendations from superiors within these organizations can eventually lead to a nomination for sponsorship as a Corsair agent.

The Freelance Path

Alternatively, individuals can build a reputation through bounty hunting, security consulting, or other freelance work across the Sphere. When a sponsor cannot find a suitable nominee within their own ranks, they often turn to established freelancers. To facilitate this, retired Corsairs run several smaller organizations and networks dedicated to mentoring promising freelancers; recommendations from these veterans carry significant weight with the Sponsor Board.

Operational Variations

While the standard mandate establishes chartered cells sponsored by major powers and equipped with dedicated starships, two distinct operational variations operate across the Sphere:

Freelancer Crews (Aspirants)

Not all who seek to become Corsairs have secured a charter or a ship. Freelancer crews operate on the ground level, taking contract security, planetary tracking, and low-level salvage to prove their capabilities to watching sponsor scouts. Without a personal vessel, they rely on commercial transport, cargo shuttles, and station transit across the Sovereign Moons, striving to build the reputation needed to earn a sponsor's backing.

Rogue Corsairs (Outlaws)

At the opposite end of the spectrum are Rogue Corsairs—operatives who were stripped of their mandate by the Oversight Committee, broke strict neutrality protocols, or walked away from corrupt superiors. Operating unregistered, jury-rigged, or re-flagged starships, rogue cells take on high-risk, legally questionable contracts in the grey and black markets. Though officially hunted as outlaws, many maintain precarious backchannel contacts, mutual blackmail arrangements, or clandestine pipelines with their former sponsors.

MANTLE Chassis & The Freelancer Network

MANTLE rigs (Modular Adaptive Neural Transmission Linked Equipment) are classified as Tier-1 Restricted Military Hardware, legally manufactured and controlled by the Flotilla for specialist strike corps. While commissioned Corsairs receive officially sanctioned frames underwritten by their sponsors, Freelancers acquire their required starting MANTLE either through competitive Sponsor Candidate Pipelines (training programs pending formal charter confirmation) or via The Freelancer Network—an underground fraternity of retired Corsair veterans, mentors, and dockside armorers who distribute repaired, surplused, or salvaged frames to promising aspirants.

The Black Market Economy & Transponder Spoofing

Rogue crews survive by disabling their vessel's primary transponders, adopting forged ID beacons burned into civilian comm-chips by syndicate slicers. However, operating without a clean charter carries severe risks: unflagged vessels have zero legal protections in open space and may be targeted on sight by Flotilla patrol cutters or colonial defense craft without warning.

The Candidate Pipeline & Evaluation

Before any charter token is minted, prospective candidates undergo exhaustive evaluation. Flotilla intelligence and corporate sponsors run applicants through high-fidelity flight simulators, stress-tolerance tanks, and live-fire tactical drills. The attrition rate exceeds eighty percent; those who wash out either return to commercial transport ranks or drift toward the freelance underground, carrying half-completed training and bruised pride.

Sanctioned vs. Outlawed Ordnance

Under the Skyline Accords, chartered Corsair cells are authorized to carry military-grade railguns, point-defense lasers, and shaped-charge boarding torpedoes. However, strict naval conventions prohibit high-yield nuclear warheads, unguided kinetic swarms in commercial orbits, and biological agents. Possessing such outlawed ordnance triggers instant charter revocation and an unconditional Flotilla strike warrant.

The Contract Registry & Escrow

All sanctioned Corsair contracts post their performance bonds into escrow accounts managed by the Flotilla Bank of Fume. Upon verifiable completion of operational objectives, funds are released directly to the cell's ship ledger, ensuring that crews operating across hostile borders never have to chase delinquent sponsors for promised payouts.

Locations: The Worlds & Corridors

If you ask an astrophysicist at the Sphere Research Center to describe where you live, they will lean back, clean their spectacles with a microfiber cloth, and sketch a cosmic masterpiece: an amber dwarf star ringed by twenty-six colossal gas giants locked in a mathematical hymn called the 4:2:1 Laplace resonance.

If you ask an orbital shuttle pilot hauling fifty tons of fertilizer from Crossroads to Pelagia, however, they will tell you that the Sphere is a cosmic nuisance invented by someone who had clearly never smelled raw fish in microgravity. To the pilot, the Sphere is the thirty-minute panic window when your sister planet blotted out the sun and your solar arrays dropped seventy percent efficiency while you were trying to line up with a docking collar. It is the permanent, dull headache caused by switching between the 0.98g of Landfall's continental dirt and the zero-g spin-lock of an L1 cargo hub.

Civilization in the Sphere does not cling to a single cradle. It is scattered across more than a hundred habitable worlds suspended in the magnetic hollows of twenty-six titans. To navigate this clockwork expanse, one must understand the ground beneath their magnetic boots, the sister world hanging overhead, and the orbital corridors that link them.

I. The Anatomy of the Clockwork System

The Sphere is not a solar system in the traditional, chaotic sense; it is a meticulously calibrated machine running on geological timescales. The entire architecture rests on three foundational structural invariants:

1. The Amber Anchor & Resonant Rings

At the heart of the system sits an Orange Dwarf star (K-type). Cooler, steadier, and far less prone to violent coronal mass ejections than ancient Earth's yellow sun, its amber-tinted light bathes the entire system in warm, long-wavelength illumination with significantly reduced ultraviolet radiation, mitigating genetic damage to surface biospheres while delivering stable solar energy across millenia.

Around this gentle forge orbit twenty-six gas giants arranged into three concentric rings (six in the Inner Ring, eight in the Middle, twelve in the Outer). Because they are locked into an immutable 4:2:1 Laplace orbital resonance, their gravitational pulls continuously cancel out systemic drift: for every single orbit completed by an Outer giant, the Middle completes two, and the Inner completes four. This mathematical synchronization functions as a gravitational dampener, preventing chaotic orbital drifts and ensuring that the densest collection of terrestrial biospheres in the galaxy never wobbles out of alignment.

2. The 800,000-Kilometer Sanctuary

Naturally formed gas giants are cosmic meat grinders, surrounding themselves with lethal radiation belts generated by violent metallic hydrogen cores. The builders of the Sphere bypassed this by altering the giants' cores to dampen high-energy dynamos and installing faint inner ring systems that continuously sweep trapped particles from the void.

The primary habitable track sits precisely 800,000 kilometers from each gas giant's center—far enough out to escape the inner radiation belts, yet comfortably shielded inside the giant's vast magnetospheric bubble, which effortlessly deflects the stellar wind.

3. Trojan Co-Orbital Configurations

Within this 800,000-kilometer track, orbital capacity is maximized via the utilization of L4 and L5 Lagrange points. Because the central gas giant easily exceeds the 24.96 mass ratio required to maintain stable "tadpole" orbits, a single orbital track hosts three distinct positions: a Primary Binary Pair, a leading Trojan Pair 60 degrees ahead, and a trailing Trojan Pair 60 degrees behind.

The Laplace Harmonic: Gravitational Dampening

In chaotic stellar accretion, packing twenty-six giants into close orbits would inevitably fling worlds into deep space within a million years. The 4:2:1 resonance ensures that conjunctions occur only at opposite orbital nodes, turning potential gravitational disruptions into self-correcting nudges that preserve perpetual orbital harmony.

II. Binary Barycentric Mechanics

The standard habitable configuration within the Sphere is the Binary Pair—two Earth-mass planets orbiting a shared center of gravity (the barycenter). To achieve a strict 30-hour orbital period while preventing destructive tidal forces (remaining outside the Roche limit), these sister worlds are separated by exactly 61,000 kilometers.

This close proximity dictates the fundamental geophysics of both worlds:

  • Tidal Locking: The planets are tidally locked to one another, perpetually presenting the exact same hemisphere to their twin across the void.
  • The Permanent Tidal Bulge: Gravitational attraction across the 61,000-kilometer gap pulls the hydrosphere and lithosphere toward the barycenter. The Inner Face facing the sister world is characterized by elevated sea levels, flooded river deltas, and dense archipelagos. The Outer Face, directed toward the void, features significantly lower sea levels, exposing vast continental basins, receding shorelines, and arid plateaus.
  • Internal Dynamo Generation: Gravitational friction generated by this tight binary orbit ensures the planetary cores remain active and molten. This geological activity allows twin planets to generate their own overlapping magnetic fields, creating a localized magnetospheric bubble that repels any ambient radiation from the gas giant.

The Roche Margin & Tidal Heating

For two Earth-mass terrestrial worlds, the fluid Roche limit sits at roughly 21,500 kilometers. The 61,000-kilometer separation provides an engineered safety buffer of nearly three-to-one, preventing catastrophic lithospheric shredding while generating just enough tidal flex to keep sub-surface tectonic heat pipes active. This continuous tidal kneading drives geothermal sea-vents, mineral-rich ocean thermal columns, and perpetual geothermal tap points that supply limitless clean baseload power to colonial settlements.

III. Surface Conditions & Orbital Eclipses

Life on the surface is governed by the 30-hour rotational cycle and the complex lighting conditions created by multiple celestial bodies.

The Systemic 1-Hour Eclipse ("The Pause")

Because the binary pairs orbit on the same ecliptic plane as the central star, a total solar eclipse is a mathematical certainty during every cycle. Once every 30 hours, the sister planet eclipses the Orange Dwarf for approximately one hour.

This results in a rapid, predictable drop in surface temperature and a temporary shift in global wind patterns as the atmosphere cools. Biospheres and civil infrastructures are tightly synchronized to this rhythmic thermal cycling, utilizing "The Pause" as a universal period for systemic recalibration, shift turnover, and biological rest.

Illumination & The Skybox

The visual environment of the Sphere is dynamic and spectacular. The central Orange Dwarf provides a warm, amber-shifted baseline illumination. At the poles, the interaction between the binary worlds' magnetic bubble and the gas giant's magnetosphere produces permanent, brilliant crimson and gold auroras.

From the Inner Face, the sister planet dominates the heavens. At a distance of only 61,000 kilometers, it appears immensely larger than ancient Earth's moon, transitioning through a complete cycle of phases every 30 hours and providing substantial reflected "planetshine" during the solar night. Beyond the sister world, the host gas giant looms as a massive, ever-present backdrop at an 800,000-kilometer distance, tracking across the sky over its longer orbital period.

The Inner vs. Outer Hemispheres

The tidal bulge fundamentally divides civilization: the Inner Face enjoys brilliant night-sky planetshine, flooded river shipping lanes, and dense metropolitan capitals (Crossroads, Cloudspire). The Outer Face looks into the dark void, housing deep-core mines, corporate launch facilities, and insular Preservationist enclaves.

The 30-Hour Work Shift & Civil Recalibration

Across all Sovereign Moons, society operates on 10-hour work blocks separated by the 1-hour Eclipse. During the Eclipse, public transit halts, orbital power grids switch to local capacitors, and markets close. Spacers call it the "Shadow Breath"—the quiet hour when the void stands still.

IV. The Spacer's Horizon: Transit & Orbital Geography

Because the system was engineered rather than formed by cosmic accident, interplanetary logistics do not require centuries of cryogenic sleep or fictional faster-than-light engines. Instead, society operates on cold, predictable Newtonian physics and continuous-thrust Brachistochrone burns.

Hopping the Binary Gap (61,000 km)

Jumping between sister worlds is a commuter run. A modest courier vessel burning at 1g reaches the midpoint in just over an hour, flips its main drive plume 180 degrees, and decelerates into the sister world's gravity well. Total transit time is roughly 3 hours, with a communication delay of less than a quarter of a second. The two worlds effectively share a single media ecosystem, financial exchange, and criminal docket.

Traversing the Trojan Expanse (800,000 km)

Traveling across the 800,000-kilometer straight line between the Primary Pair and the L4 or L5 Lagrange points at a standard 1g burn takes approximately 3.5 hours, with a light-speed communication lag of only 2.6 seconds. The Primary and Trojan worlds form a tightly integrated economic triangle where freight, agricultural produce, and naval patrols move with the ease of an oceanic shipping line.

Crossing the Deep Void (90,000,000 km)

Journeys between neighboring gas giant clusters—such as the run from the Newcomer cradle of Fume to the Ancestral bastion of X'ara—cross deep interplanetary space. Under continuous 1g acceleration and deceleration, this run takes approximately 2.2 days, with a communication lag peaking around five minutes. While requiring robust reaction-mass reserves, radiation shelters, and careful course calculations, the void between clusters is a highway, not a lifetime barrier.

The High Ground: L1 Anchorages & Bastion Yards

Between every binary pair sits the L1 Lagrange point, a gravitational null zone suspended precisely 30,500 kilometers between the sister moons. Standard geostationary orbits are impossible, as they would intersect with the sister planet's pull. Consequently, heavy orbital infrastructure is anchored at L1: sprawling transfer hubs like Waypoint Station, high-bandwidth comm relays, and orbital bastion docks like the Port Zenith Bastion Yards above Landfall, where capital hulls are overhauled and the first combat MANTLE frames were forged. Communication and surveillance satellites are confined to tight Low Moon Orbits (LMO) beneath the localized radiation belts.

Atmospheric Re-entry Dynamics

Re-entering a sister moon's atmosphere from high orbit demands precise angle management. Ships employ lifting-body hull geometry and ablative ceramic heat shields to bleed off velocity during atmospheric capture. A steep vector risks incinerating the ship in atmospheric friction, while a shallow burn skips off the mesosphere into deep interplanetary space.

Why the Corridors Belong to Corsairs

Under the Skyline Accords of 335 AA, planetary police authority terminates sixty kilometers up, while Flotilla capital dreadnoughts cannot fire into planetary atmospheres without triggering civil war. Between the clouds and high orbit lies the jurisdictional seam—where smugglers, corporate raiders, and fugitive cartels thrive. Corsairs alone possess the cross-jurisdictional warrants and agile multi-environment ships to hunt across this boundary.

Microgravity Health & Radiation Shelters

Life beyond the magnetic shelter of planetary atmospheres requires strict biological safeguards. Spacers endure continuous microgravity during drift phases, mitigating muscular atrophy and osteopenia through high-resistance pneumatic exercise rigs, daily compression wraps, and magnetic boots. To combat cosmic radiation during deep interplanetary runs, crew quarters and bridges are encased within hydrogenous polyethylene composite shielding, while storm shelters are ringed by the vessel's potable water and reaction mass tanks.

Propellant Economics: Water & He-3 Logistics

Brachistochrone flight demands vast quantities of reaction mass (purified water) and fusion fuel (Helium-3 skimmed from gas giants). Stations at L1 act as municipal filling depots; a crew that miscalculates its delta-V budget can find itself stranded in high orbit, facing predatory corporate towing rates.

The Brachistochrone Flight Profile

In hard-physics spaceflight, there is no coasting on long leisurely orbits if time matters. Starships perform Brachistochrone burns: accelerating continuously toward the target under 1g thrust (simulating comfortable Earth-gravity for the crew), performing a 180-degree Turnover Maneuver precisely at the mathematical midpoint, and decelerating under equal 1g reverse thrust into the destination's orbital envelope.

This continuous-thrust profile requires high-efficiency Draco fusion drives and disciplined reaction-mass accounting. If an engine fails before flip, or if fuel runs dry mid-burn, the vessel becomes a ballistic missile screaming into the outer void at hundreds of kilometers per second.

Thermal Radiation Realities

Vacuum does not conduct heat; it insulates. Every burning fusion reactor, fired kinetic railgun, and energized MANTLE chassis generates waste heat that must be pumped through internal ammonia loops into massive, glowing thermal radiator arrays extending from the hull.

In combat, retracting radiators protects them from enemy shrapnel but locks thermal heat into the ship's internal heat sinks. If a duel drags on too long, the crew risks boiling in their own pressure suits.

Navigation in Microgravity: The Pilot's Axiom

"Speed is an illusion; vector is reality. When you cut your burn at the midpoint, you are not slowing down—you are falling backward into the other world's arms at sixty kilometers per second. Keep your RCS thrusters clear of frost and trust the computer's burn timer."

Communications & Packet Networks

While binary sister worlds enjoy instantaneous voice and data channels (<0.25s), cross-cluster communications operate via store-and-forward packet buoys. Relay drones accelerate along fast trajectories, transmitting encrypted batch ledgers and news feeds to avoid the multi-minute speed-of-light lag.

Orbital Slingshots: The Oberth Maneuver

In tactical intercepts or emergency getaways, skilled helmsmen plunge deep into a gas giant's gravity well, firing main drives at closest approach (periapsis). The Oberth effect multiplies effective kinetic delta-V, allowing an agile corsair hull to outrun heavier military pursuit. Navigators refer to this high-g trajectory as "skimming the furnace," where radiator shields are locked flat and structural stress gauges shriek in redline telemetry.

The Fume Cluster

The Fume cluster is a hyper-dense, engineered orbital node within the Sphere. Centered on the colossal gas giant "Fume," this cluster hosts three distinct binary pairs—a total of six terrestrial worlds—within a single radiation-shielded orbital track at 800,000 kilometers. Each pair is locked in a 30-hour cycle, resulting in a system defined by extreme tidal stress, consistent eclipses, and profound atmospheric diversity.

I. The Central Anchor: Fume

Fume is a massive, ringed gas giant characterized by an artificially altered metallic hydrogen core that suppresses violent magnetic dynamos, minimizing lethal high-energy radiation output. Its atmosphere is a turbulent expanse of deep amber and violet clouds, illuminated by the internal heat of its primordial formation and the reflected light of its sibling gas giants.

Fume's vast magnetosphere is the vital life-support mechanism for the six worlds in its local track, acting as a massive cosmic umbrella against stellar wind while producing spectacular, permanent aurorae that wash across the polar skies of its orbiting moons in ribbons of emerald and gold.

Atmospheric Skimming & He-3 Harvesters

The upper cloud decks of Fume are rich in Helium-3 and deuterium. Automated orbital skimmers and daring corporate scoop-ships dive into the mesosphere along steep aerocapture arcs, compressing raw fusion fuels into cryogenic tanks before slingshotting back to high orbit.

II. The Binary Pairs

1. The Primary Pair (Barycenter): Landfall & Nimbus

Situated at the base of the orbital track, these two worlds provide the system's baseline terrestrial conditions and represent the political and economic heart of Newcomer civilization.

Landfall: Landfall is the most Earth-like world of the cluster. It features a balanced distribution of continents and oceans, with moderate plate tectonics driven by the tidal pull of its twin. The geography consists of temperate forests, mountain ranges, and large, stable basins. Its climate is the most temperate in the Fume cluster, with the hourly eclipse serving as a mild seasonal shift. The planet's geopolitical landscape is anchored by Crossroads, the rustic capital founded five centuries ago, and the scarred, radiation-swept Evergaol Crash Site exclusion zone.

Nimbus: Landfall's twin is a vertical world of high-altitude plateaus separated by dizzying, vertical chasms. Because of the planet's unique atmospheric density, heavy inert gases collect in these low-lying ravines, creating "atmospheric lakes" that possess a liquid-like viscosity and swallow sunlight. The high-altitude plateaus are relatively thin-aired and arid, while the ravines are sheltered, pressurized micro-environments choked with heavy, luminous fog.

Perched across the high plateaus is Cloudspire, a massive, hyper-dense metropolis of soaring arcologies washed in neon and plasma advertisements. Its five vertical tiers house the elite financial houses and biotech laboratories of the Sphere. Beneath this affluent, brightly lit surface layer lies a volatile criminal underworld: the dense, fog-ridden ravines below the city lines are completely unpoliced, serving as decentralized hideouts for heavily armed syndicates, rogue slicers, and illegal bio-synthesis labs known collectively as the Ravine Cartels.

Aerostats & Sky-Ranches

Between the thin highlands and the dense gas ravines sits Nimbus's habitable middle troposphere. Here, wealthy agricultural consortiums float massive solar aerostats—colossal buoyant domes kilometers wide where genetically engineered grain and citrus crops bask in uninterrupted amber sunlight, tended by airborne migrant crews.

These floating plantations are connected by pressurized cargo cable-cars and vertical lift shuttles. During heavy cyclonic seasons, dockworkers tether the aerostats to reinforced granite pylons sunk into the cliff walls to prevent the domes from drifting into the toxic smog-wells below.

The Atmospheric Lakes of Nimbus

The gas pooling in Nimbus's ravines is so dense that specially hydro-foiled skimmers can float on its surface without airfoils. Crossing these chemical mists requires rebreathers and pressurized suits; breathing raw ravine gas causes rapid asphyxiation and lung calcification.

2. The Leading Pair (L4 Trojans): Pelagia & Suncatcher

These worlds occupy the leading Lagrangian point, forming the most climatically polarized binary in the cluster.

Pelagia: Pelagia is a world composed almost entirely of water. Shallow, interconnected island chains span the equator, but the poles are marked by massive, abyssal deep-sea trenches. Societally, Pelagia is defined by a rigid class divide. The planet's elite reside on massive, floating megastructures—luxurious, self-sustaining pontoon cities that glide gracefully across the open ocean, operating with an insulated, aristocratic elegance. The common working populace is restricted to the natural equatorial island chains, which are heavily urbanized, densely packed, and largely dedicated to harvesting chemical kelp and processing marine pharmaceuticals for the upper tiers under the watchful, hidden gaze of the deep-sea Leviathan.

Suncatcher: Pelagia's twin is a brutal contrast: a planet of low albedo, dominated by vast, heat-absorbing desert basins and savannah-like equatorial plateaus. Its surface features very low thermal inertia, experiencing rapid thermal spikes during solar exposure and steep cooling during the eclipse. Its landscape is defined by vast, wind-scoured plains, glassed dunes, and permanent dust storms.

While surface agriculture is nearly impossible, the arid crust is rich in highly concentrated heavy mineral and crystalline deposits, driving a massive, volatile mining industry. The primary civil hub is Dustwall, a heavily fortified industrial frontier settlement built directly around a massive crater mine. Beyond the hub lies the Badlands, a lawless expanse of wind-carved canyons where corporate law enforcement cannot penetrate, leaving the territory to cutthroat outlaws, prospectors, and bounty hunters.

3. The Trailing Pair (L5 Trojans): Karst & Rime

This pair showcases the extreme effects of gravitational tidal friction on planetary crusts.

Karst: Karst's surface is a landscape of collapse. Centuries of tidal flexing have shattered the crust into a network of massive cenotes and sinkholes. The surface is essentially a barren, razor-sharp plateau. However, the true geography of Karst lies beneath; the planet's interior is hollowed into massive, cavernous networks filled with geothermally heated inland seas and bioluminescent fungal forests.

This subterranean ecosystem is home to the Lithari, a primitive intelligent species of tribal hunters. Following their discovery, the Flotilla enacted the Non-Interference Protocol, declaring Karst a quarantined zone. All Newcomer presence is strictly prohibited, and the Flotilla maintains a permanent orbital blockade to ensure the Lithari remain undisturbed until they achieve spaceflight capability.

Rime: Rime is defined by its extreme albedo. The Outer Face is locked under a permanent, kilometers-thick ice sheet that reflects incoming stellar radiation. The planet's geography is a gradient of temperature: the outer hemisphere is a frozen wasteland, while the Inner Face features a single, geothermally maintained circular ocean. This coastal ring is the only habitable region, pinned between encroaching glacial walls and the tidal-warmed sea.

The Leviathan's Shadow in the Abyss

In Pelagia's deepest trenches lies the slumbering Leviathan, an ancient dragon entity whose psychic hum whispers into the minds of the floating city Barons. Those who dredge the abyssal shelves report missing submersibles and unmapped magnetic pulses rising from the deep floor.

Landfall: Cradle of Newcomers

Landfall was the very first moon in the Sphere that the Newcomers touched down upon circa 500 years ago. The original four races—Humans, Naga, Craggen, and Sylvan—woke from cryo-sleep aboard their crash-landed seed arks in Crossroads Valley. The monuments of those founding days remain central to planetary life, chiefly Crossroads itself, the very first settlement built by Newcomer hands and today the cultural and demographic capital of the known Sphere.

Planetary Geography & Continents

Landfall is the most Earth-like of the colonized moons in climate and biome, though with slightly more clustered continental landmasses than ancient Earth. Crossroads sits at the confluence of two wide freshwater rivers on the near-side of the moon, facing directly toward its sister world Nimbus and situated within sight of the historic Exodus Arks.

The surrounding landscape is defined by fertile continental plains, rolling fields, and ancient purple-leafed forests. Traveling east beyond the continental shelf and across the Sun Canal—a narrow strait dividing the northeastern and southeastern continents—savannahs give way to dense equatorial rainforests. Following the river valleys north toward the pole leads into the colossal granite spines of the Crown Mountains, where early Craggen prospectors established the first deep-crust geothermal shafts.

The western continent lies on the far side of the moon, facing the cold void. Slightly smaller and offset to the south, it is anchored by Settler's Bay, established in 60 AA as the primary marine port of the western hemisphere. Broad swaths of both continents are legally protected wildlife reserves, preserving the virgin ecosystems that greeted the first generation of pioneers.

The Geothermal Spines of the Crown Mountains

High above the continental plains, the Crown Mountains house the deepest mining shafts in the Newcomer territories. Craggen tunneling clans operate massive geothermal boreholes reaching twenty kilometers into the mantle, tapping super-critical magma vents to smelt heavy construction alloys and composite hull plating. In the high passes, mountain outposts maintain sensor listening posts tracking orbital conjunctions.

Quirks of Native Flora & Fauna

Landfall's native biosphere evolved under the gentle amber spectrum of an Orange Dwarf. To absorb long-wavelength radiation efficiently, native plants developed leaves and stems rich in purple, crimson, and deep indigo pigments; true green foliage is a botanical rarity confined largely to flowering petals.

Native fauna is equally distinctive: even apex predators display a remarkable neutrality toward Newcomers, avoiding populated settlements unless their nesting grounds or migratory corridors are directly threatened. Biologists credit this calm demeanor to the subtle pheromonal harmonizing fields seeded by the ancient Precursors before the seed arks touched down.

Crossroads: The Capital City

Crossroads is deliberately low-slung, eschewing the gleaming vertical megastructures of Cloudspire in favor of an open, sprawling footprint. Aside from monumental structures like the Crossroads Council Hall, the Sphere Research Center (SRC), and the orbital spaceport, the city is a harmonious blend of high-tech modular prefabs, carved local granite, and reclaimed ark timbers. Paved streets are lined with flowerbeds, communal verandas, and festive banners strung between rooftops. Underground tram networks handle heavy passenger transit, preserving the quiet, garden-like tranquility of the surface.

Five centuries of shared survival have forged Crossroads into a cultural melting pot. This unity is reinforced by Unitarianism—a prevailing cultural and philosophical movement that views the four founding lineages as kin shaped by ancient design. Crossroads hosts more cultural festivals than any other city in the Sphere, celebrating the traditions of all ten lineages with open-air feasts, music, and artistic galas.

Megacorporations on Landfall

Nearly all major corporate conglomerates maintain headquarters or flagship offices in Crossroads, rooted in their historical origins. Vandal's Workshop and Redfeather Aerospace maintain expansive campuses here. To prevent corporate enclaves from monopolizing civic life, the Crossroads Directorate strictly confines heavy industry to zoned outer perimeters, actively penalizing companies that attempt to construct isolated corporate arcologies.

The Anti-Arcology Acts of Crossroads

To preserve public solidarity and democratic cohesion, the Crossroads Charter strictly prohibits private corporations from establishing fully enclosed employee arcologies. Every worker is guaranteed private civilian housing within municipal wards, preventing the corporate serfdom seen in the frontier mining hubs.

The Landing Site Memorial

Located just outside Crossroads, the Landing Site is preserved as a revered historical sanctuary. The four original Exodus Arks stand where they touched down in -4 BA. Generations of citizens visit the cavernous cargo bays and preserved autotutor crèches on civic pilgrimages, reflecting on the mysteries of their forgotten homeworlds and the journey across the stars.

The Evergaol Exclusion Zone

In stark contrast to Crossroads lies the Evergaol Exclusion Zone—the scarred impact trench where the disabled alien super-dreadnought crashed during the climax of the Frontier War in 508 AA. An automated Monolith war engine dispatched from the Praetor's arsenal, the dreadnought was brought down only when Flotilla warships, Orkish heavy rams, and the dragons Goldheart and Stormdrinker united to drag it into the crust.

A decade later in 518 AA, the site remains a lethal, cordoned exclusion zone. Automated defense pods, leaking radiation sinks, and rogue scavenger cartels known as the Jailers infest the wreckage, stripping dark matter conduits and Precursor alloys. Corsairs are routinely contracted by colonial magistrates to intercept rogue salvage teams and clear lethal automata wandering beyond the perimeter.

The Scavengers of the Scar

The Sphere Research Center (SRC)

The SRC is the premier academic institution of Newcomer civilization. Its massive subterranean sensor arrays continuously monitor the 4:2:1 Laplace resonance and track cosmic anomalies across the outer rings, providing crucial navigational charts for Flotilla navigators and Corsair pilots.

Crossroads Spaceport & Customs Gate

While orbital beanstalks were debated, Landfall's dual-jurisdiction laws favor aerospace shuttles. The Crossroads Spaceport handles over two thousand sub-orbital and interplanetary launches every ten-hour cycle, linking the surface directly with Waypoint Station at L1. Customs inspection gates run biometric checks on every inbound spacer to prevent contraband bio-shunts from entering civilian markets.

The X'ara Cluster

The X'ara cluster is an engineered orbital node located across a 2.2-day transit corridor from Fume. In macro-engineering, radiation shielding, and orbital geometry, X'ara closely mirrors Fume: three binary pairs—six Earth-mass worlds in total—orbiting at 800,000 kilometers within a synchronized 30-hour cycle.

Unlike Fume's radical biospheric extremes, X'ara's six worlds are remarkably uniform, featuring temperate continental distributions that closely match Landfall. Variations arise from localized soil chemistry, ancient Precursor installations, and the solemn architecture of the Ancestrals. In the Ancestral tongue, the suffixes -ria/-lia and -oa designate the first and second worlds of each binary pair.

I. The Central Anchor: X'ara

X'ara is a massive, ringed gas giant identical in structural physics to Fume. Its subdued metallic hydrogen core minimizes baseline radiation while generating a dense magnetospheric sheath that protects its six moons from cosmic rays. Its cloud bands swirl in deep amethyst and amber, casting ethereal twilight across the sister worlds.

II. The Binary Pairs

1. The Primary Pair: Irul'lia & Irul'oa (Irul 1 & 2)

Situated at the base of the orbital track, these twin Earth-mass worlds serve as the sovereign, heavily fortified home territory of the Ancestral civilization.

Irul'lia (Irul 1): The first world of the primary pair mirrors Landfall's baseline geography, though high atmospheric concentrations of xenon and iodine give its massive northern taiga forests a dark purple hue. The planet is the administrative core of Ancestral society. Its Inner Face is dominated by soaring lithium-sheathed spires that tap directly into deep tectonic heat dynamos. While closed to outside commerce, the Flotilla maintains a single heavily fortified diplomatic consulate on the surface—the only port where Newcomers may legally clear transit protocols and interface with Ancestral archons.

Irul'oa (Irul 2): The second world is an industrial and martial redoubt. High surface silicate concentrations cause its equatorial savannahs to grow stiff, glassy, golden-orange grasses. Its Outer Face is a barren, scorched desert exposing ancient automated cloning vaults and subterranean manufacturing bays dating back millions of years—sacred assets fiercely guarded by the Ancestral military elite.

The Shrouded Genesis Vaults

Deep beneath the glassy savannahs of Irul'oa lie automated genesis vaults. Maintained by mechanical servitors since before the First Arrival, these subterranean cryo-catacombs store the frozen genetic codices of billions of extinct biospheres, guarded under eternal vigil by elite Ancestral phalanxes. The genetic lines of the Ancestrals themselves have remained unchanged for over fifty millennia, replicated with zero drift.

Diplomatic Enclaves & Transit Visas

The Flotilla Consulate on Irul'lia is a sovereign walled enclave. Foreign spacers, merchants, and Corsairs must register their biometric tokens and post diplomatic bonds before taking a single step outside the port gantry. Unregistered visitors face immediate detention by Ancestral wardens, who escort trespassers to orbital holding rings without appeal.

The Sacred Vigil: Ancestral Dogma

Ruled by the immortal Archon and guided by the theological dogma of the Sacred Vigil, the Ancestrals maintain strict biological uniformity and reject genetic intermingling. They view Newcomer dynamism with a mix of bemused condescension and deep existential unease.

The Lithium Spires of Irul'lia

Soaring five kilometers above Irul'lia's purple taiga, the Spires are solid monobloc structures of Precursor lithium-alloy. Acting as massive solid-state heat exchangers, they channel planetary magma currents into microwave power beams that illuminate the orbital shipyards above. Navigators describe seeing these brilliant vertical beacons from across the binary gap, piercing the planetary shadow during the 30-hour eclipse like pillars of incandescent violet fire.

Timeline of Newcomer Civilization

This chronicle documents the recorded history of Newcomer civilization across the Sphere, from the arrival of the four ancestral seed arks on Landfall to the present day in 518 AA.

Era I: The Era of Genesis & The Primary Pair (0 AA – 60 AA)

? BA — The Great Exodus

According to curated ship archives and sacred oral tradition, the four ancestral lineages—Humanity, Naga, Craggen, and Sylvan—fled catastrophic stellar collapse in their home systems aboard four colossal automated seed arks. Guided across the lightless interstellar void by automated navigational systems, these massive colony vessels safeguarded the genetic repositories, cultural libraries, philosophies, and scientific knowledge of their dying homeworlds, seeking a habitable system where civilization could begin anew.

-4 BA — Silent Touchdown in Crossroads Valley

Under sub-light automated navigation, the four arks entered the atmosphere of the temperate moon Landfall and touched down silently within a single verdant, sheltered valley. While the hulls rested on alien soil, the ships' automated nurseries remained sealed; the onboard autotutors were programmed to keep their adolescent charges sequestered within simulated biodomes until they reached full biological and social maturity at age twenty.

-3 to -1 BA — The Clandestine Breakouts

Confinement proved impossible against restless, inquisitive youth. Bored by looping historical lectures, synthetic nutrition paste, and sterile nursery corridors, teenage troublemakers (aged 14–16) began prying open ventilation shafts, overriding maintenance bulkheads, and slipping out into the misty valley under the cover of night.

In the untamed alien ferns of the valley floor, runaway humans, nagas, craggens, and sylvans collided. Although their databanks had never disclosed the existence of the other three hulls resting mere kilometers away, they discovered to their shock that their autotutors had taught them all the same engineered Common Tongue.

Waiting for them in the valley was Goldheart, a gentle, solitary young dragon who nested among the crags. Rather than alarming the ships' security sensors or herding the runaways back into confinement, Goldheart became their secret guardian, protector, and older brother. He shielded the teenagers from Landfall's native predators, taught them to avoid natural hazards, and concealed their illicit excursions from the arks' external scanners. Around clandestine campfires beneath the dragon's folded wings, the teenagers traded smuggled rations, shared homeworld myths, and forged a fierce cross-species camaraderie that transcended the sterile boundaries of their individual ships.

0 AA — Graduation Day

The automated gestation and education cycles reached scheduled completion. Ship sirens wailed, floodlights activated, and the primary nursery airlocks swung wide to formally release Cohort Zero—an initial generation of young adults (aged 18–20) meant to step onto an alien moon for the first time.

The ship autotutors anticipated bewildered, solemn survivors clutching ancestral archives. Instead, the breakout veterans burst back into their respective arks, laughing and dragging their wide-eyed, obedient peers out into the sunlight. Rather than four frightened alien factions encountering each other in defensive isolation, the cohorts stepped into a valley where shared jokes, deep friendships, rivalries, and first loves had already been quietly flourishing for three years. Goldheart greeted the assembled multitudes, playfully reinforcing the beloved cultural belief that he had pulled their drifting ships from the void and watched over them.

1 AA — Foundation of Crossroads

Cohort Zero began building their first permanent settlement in the valley where the four arks landed. Naming it Crossroads, they constructed low-slung, rustic dwellings from local stone, timber, and salvaged ark hulls. Influenced by Goldheart's gentle warnings to avoid high-altitude electromagnetic emissions that might draw unknown dangers from the void, the settlement established an intentionally horizontal footprint that eschewed massive vertical spires. The four lineages formed a provisional council, electing one representative from each ark.

6 AA — Birth of Integrationism

The birth of the first cross-lineage child sparked a profound cultural transformation. Natural romances, having taken root during the secret midnight gatherings of the breakout years, blossomed openly across Crossroads. This gave rise to Integrationism—a socio-political movement advocating total cultural synthesis, mutual adaptation, and shared destiny as the moral core of the Newcomer civilization.

14 AA — The First Vault & The Covenant

Expeditions venturing into Landfall’s crags discovered the First Precursor Vault—a humming subterranean complex of crystalline lattices. The anomaly attracted Stormdrinker, a massive dragon roosting in the violent electrical storms of Landfall's binary twin, Nimbus. Driven by an insatiable curiosity for ancient technology and energy phenomena, Stormdrinker descended upon the site.

Goldheart stepped in to mediate, forging an unprecedented alliance between mortals and dragons: the Newcomers provided the nimble hands and fine tools to navigate human-sized maintenance conduits, while Stormdrinker and Goldheart provided the sensory depth and brute energy to crack heavy exterior bulkheads.

Inside the Vault, the expedition leaders encountered active automated defenses and energetic systems of incomprehensible power. Recognizing that reckless tampering with Precursor ruins or broadcasting energetic distress signals into open space could trigger devastating planetary catastrophes, the expedition leaders, council elders, Goldheart, and Stormdrinker swore an eternal oath. They formed The Covenant: a classified circle dedicated to containing dangerous technologies, sanitizing active Vault sites, and shielding the young civilization from cosmic hazards.

15 AA — The Preservationist Counter-Movement

As Integrationism swept the urban heart of Crossroads, an ideological counter-current emerged. Led primarily by the "dutiful students" of Cohort Zero—those who had remained inside the arks until Graduation Day, absorbing the unadulterated poetry, tragedy, and philosophies of Earth, the Naga swarms, the Craggen deep-hives, and the Sylvan groves—these traditionalists viewed the freewheeling hybrid culture as an irreverent betrayal of their sacred ancestral heritage. Adopting the philosophy of Preservationism, they began migrating toward Landfall's outward-facing hemisphere to establish insular, single-lineage settlements dedicated to keeping their bloodlines and traditions pure.

35 AA — The Nimbus Rendezvous

Guided by data shared through The Covenant, researchers at the Sphere Research Center (SRC) constructed Landfall’s first deep-space manned rocket. Commanded by a daring multi-species crew, the vessel braved the turbulent electrical storm-wells of Nimbus to rendezvous with Stormdrinker at his high-altitude roost. In exchange for historical texts, mathematical proofs, and cultural data, Stormdrinker allowed SRC physicists to study his unique bio-fusion biology and gravitic flight mechanics.

38 AA — The Draco Fusion Breakthrough

Synthesizing telemetry from Stormdrinker’s biological dynamo with Precursor magnetic shielding principles, the SRC developed the Draco Fusion Drive. Operating on continuous-thrust fusion physics, the drive made rapid sub-light interplanetary transit safe, reliable, and commercially viable.

39 AA — Maiden Flight of the Birdy

The transport vessel Birdy, equipped with the prototype Draco Drive, successfully crossed the 61,000-kilometer binary gap between Landfall and Nimbus in under two hours, formally opening the space age for Newcomer civilization.

45 AA — The Rise of Cloudspire

A permanent research outpost on the high-altitude arid plateaus of Nimbus rapidly expanded into a metropolis of gleaming towers, neon arrays, and lightning catchers: Cloudspire. Stormdrinker became the public patron of the city, revered by citizens as the "Lightning Sage," circling the upper atmosphere above the clouds while maintaining quiet council with Covenant leaders in restricted subterranean bays.

170 AA — The Shadow of the Abyss

Factions: Powers of the Sphere

To the celestial cartographers and ivory-tower physicists of the Sphere Research Center, the Sphere is a marvel of cosmic clockwork: twenty-six ringed gas giants suspended in a flawless 4:2:1 Laplace resonance around an amber dwarf star. It is clean, elegant, and mathematically sublime.

To the eight billion souls actually living in it, the Sphere is mostly an alarm clock that goes off every thirty hours, a persistent argument over communal water filters, and the stubborn realization that no matter how far your ancestors traveled across the lightless void, someone in middle management will still find a way to charge you for parking.

To the orbital admirals of the Flotilla and corporate executives aboard pressurized stations, planetary bodies are often treated as mere gravitational wells—convenient heat sinks with high atmospheric drag and an irritating habit of requiring reaction mass to leave. But to the billions who dwell at the bottom of those wells, the planet is everything. It is the only place where an accidental drop of your morning coffee results in a broken mug on the floor rather than a scalded eye in microgravity. It is the soil beneath your work boots, the air you do not pay for by the cubic meter, and the municipal hall whose doors you can march through when a sewer pipe bursts.

Anatomy of a Cross-Jurisdictional Crisis

On an ordinary afternoon in Crossroads, an unregistered scrap-hopper screams through the upper atmosphere, clips a municipal tram line, and crashes into the stained-glass roof of the Council Hall. A terrified Jailer courier scrambles out, clutching a humming Precursor coil stolen from the Evergaol exclusion zone. Behind him, a Flotilla gunboat hovers at flight ceiling, legally forbidden from firing into municipal skies, while corporate lobbyists and municipal guards draw weapons in the gallery.

In less than four minutes of shattered glass and procedural indignation, the Crossroads Council Hall becomes an exact microcosm of the forces that govern the Sphere:

  • The Colonial Administrations (The Soil): Exercising territorial sovereignty within their atmospheric gravity well under the Skyline Accords, balancing agriculture, taxes, and municipal laws below cloud ceiling.
  • The Megacorporations (The Forge): Lobbying for transit tariffs and selling the structural titanium, Draco drive components, and life-support patents that keep civilization breathing.
  • The Underworld (The Shadow): Hawking unstable alien technology from the Evergaol to illicit cartels to bypass corporate monopolies.

  • The Alien & Cosmic: The humming Precursor coil, and Goldheart, the gentle living dragon lounging on the council terrace.

1. The Crossroads High Council (Landfall)

Lived Experience & Civic Philosophy

Life in Crossroads is an unhurried, deeply civic affair governed by thirty-hour cycles, the reassuring chime of municipal tram bells, and the enduring cultural conviction that any disaster in the cosmos can be resolved if five sensible people sit down around a sturdy cedar table with a pot of hot tea and a well-sharpened pencil. Founded in 1 AA by Cohort Zero around the original landing sites of the four exodus arks, Crossroads is the oldest continuously inhabited settlement in the Sphere and the undisputed heart of Integrationism.

Its architecture is deliberately horizontal: rustic two-story townhouses and communal cottages constructed from local granite, river timber, and lovingly burnished hull plating salvaged from the arks. Insulated with modern aerogel composites and draped in purple-leafed native ivy, blooming window boxes, and colorful lineage banners, the town eschews vertical spires. This low profile is partly a historic courtesy to the skies—honoring the ancient counsel of Goldheart, the gentle void dragon who has lounged on the council terrace for five centuries—and partly a reflection of the town's stubborn refusal to live in a filing cabinet.

Because of Goldheart's calming presence, the citizens possess an almost unflappable temperament. When an unregistered cargo hopper falls screaming through the clouds with a blown maneuvering thruster, the townsfolk do not panic; they step onto their front porches, check their pocket watches, adjust their watering cans, and begin calculating property damage to their prize hydrangeas.

Leadership & Internal Power Structure

Crossroads is administered by the High Council, presided over by the Speaker (currently Jesse Haddon, an unshakeable human carpenter-turned-statesman whose patience with bureaucratic filibustering is legendary across three moons). The Council comprises four permanent Lineage Seats—representing Humans, Nagas, Craggens, and Sylvans—alongside rotating delegates elected from municipal labor guilds: Stone-Carvers, River Dredgers, Orchardists, Rail Workers, and Scribes.

Council procedure is notoriously deliberative: every civic ordinance must undergo three public readings, an environmental impact study, and a floor debate. Goldheart holds no official ballot, but the Chair unfailingly recognizes "the dragon's remark" from the terrace. The dragon's warm, resonant commentary—often concerning the crisp scent of line-dried sheets, the sweetness of roasted autumn pears, or the comedic futility of mortal hurry—regularly shames bickering councilors into unanimous, sheepish compromises.

Territory, Assets & Relationship with Corsairs

Signature civic assets include the Sphere Research Center (SRC), where physicists study celestial mechanics; the Landing Site Memorial Arks, preserving the four exodus hulls as national archives; the Crossroads Council Hall with its historic stained-glass dome; and the whisper-quiet Subterranean Tramway keeping surface streets uncluttered.

The Council views Corsairs as an essential municipal constabulary with sub-light wings, regularly issuing official Writs of Recovery for fugitives fleeing into the upper atmosphere and hazardous Precursor salvage smuggled from the Evergaol. The Council pays promptly in Flotilla Credits, though municipal surveyors rigorously itemize and deduct any collateral property damage from the cell's reward.

1. The Trade Alliance

Commercial Profile & Corporate Ethos

The Trade Alliance is the oldest and most politically entrenched commercial coalition in Newcomer civilization. Founded in 180 AA during the catastrophic Inter-Binary Famine—when the destruction of the Pelagia-Suncatcher water railguns threatened to starve the young colonies—the Alliance was formed by a desperate syndicate of independent freighter captains, warehouse syndicates, and bulk grain brokers who pooled their ships to run emergency relief convoys across the void.

That humanitarian origin story has long since calcified into an all-encompassing, bureaucratic freight cartel. Their philosophy is unyielding: Civilization is a supply chain, and anyone who disrupts the schedule is an enemy of progress. They view planetary borders as irritating friction, naval blockades as supply-chain inefficiencies, and custom duties as an unprovoked assault on the natural right of cargo to move from point A to point B without someone opening the crate.

Leadership, Assets & Relationship with Corsairs

The Underworld & Syndicates

JURISDICTION: Unpoliced Ravines, Crash Exclusion Zones, Badlands, The Deep Shadow
AUTHORITY: Raw Physical Force, Syndicate Ledgers, The Black-Market Exchange
PRIMARY FACTIONS: The Jailers of Evergaol, The Cloudsurfers, The Iron Fang, The Desert Wraiths, The Children of the Leviathan

Wherever formal law draws a boundary, human—and non-human—ingenuity immediately invents a way to crawl beneath it. In the Sphere, the "Underworld" is not a single, tidy criminal syndicate with a dark-suited godfather sitting behind a mahogany desk. It is a sprawling, pragmatic, and remarkably stubborn ecosystem of people who looked at the high price of legal air, legal food, and legal docking permits, decided the mathematics were unacceptable, and resolved to make their own arrangements.

Crime in the Sphere is rarely ideological. It is driven by the brutal thermodynamics of the frontier: if the corporate monopoly on Nimbus charges twenty credits for an atmospheric filter that costs two credits to stamp from scrap sheet, someone in the foggy lower ravines will inevitably figure out how to steal the stamping die. And if the Flotilla outlaws the trafficking of un-sanitized Precursor tech under naval quarantine and colonial safety edicts, someone on Landfall will happily risk catastrophic cellular radiation to haul an alien focusing coil out of a smoldering impact crater, because that single humming cylinder of superconducting circuitry can buy a family fresh groceries and a decommissioned hopper for ten years.

The underworld exists in the cracks between the great powers: in the three kilometers of toxic, unpoliced fog beneath Cloudspire's gleaming skywalks; in the trackless, red-dust canyons of Suncatcher's Badlands where corporate crawlers fear to tread; in the creaking shantytown reef-barges of Pelagia; and in the radioactive exclusion zones of Landfall where automated alien kill-nodes still hunt the unwary.

1. The Jailers of Evergaol

Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos

If you ask an honest citizen in Crossroads about the Evergaol Crash Site, they will cross their fingers, murmur a quiet prayer of thanks to Goldheart, and change the subject. The crash site is a continent-scale scar on Landfall’s crust where the ancient automated dreadnought was brought down in 508 AA. It is a restricted exclusion zone of jagged black metal, active alien defense drones, and bizarre energy leaks. The Jailers are the only people in the Sphere insane enough to call it home.

Composed of eccentric salvagers, renegade scientists, and mutated survivors who refused to abandon the crash perimeter, the Jailers dwell inside the hollowed-out titanium ribbing and cavernous cooling decks of the fallen dreadnought. Their cultural philosophy blends scavenger pragmatism with pseudo-religious reverence for alien technology, viewing the wreck not as an apocalyptic war grave, but as an infinite, miraculous junkyard sent by the gods to provide for anyone brave enough to pry open a bulkhead. They paint crude warning runes with engine grease, wear patchwork hazard suits adorned with pulsing Precursor fiber-optics, and speak a rapid, clipped dialect filled with garbled machine-code terms.

Leadership & The "Bloodmages"

The Jailers have no central council; they operate as autonomous salvage clans that convene around shared barter-hearths deep inside the wreck. Authority within the clans belongs unconditionally to the Jailer Bloodmages. Despite their terrifying, occult-sounding title, Bloodmages possess no mystical magic: they are veteran cyberneticists and scavengers who perform reckless, illegal home-brew surgery upon their own bodies, grafting active Precursor neural shunts and salvaged thermal conduits directly into their nervous systems and circulatory tracks.

By siphoning electrical energy from their own biological metabolic heat and directing it through subcutaneous alien filaments, a Bloodmage projects short-range kinetic displacement barriers (the "Crimson Shield") or vents concentrated, high-velocity thermal arcs from their hands (the "Piercing Blood"). This biological interface takes a horrific physical toll: bulging dark veins, ash-pale skin, and violent auditory hallucinations caused by the humming coils. To outsiders, they look like terrifying warlocks; to their clans, they are living gods who can walk past the dreadnought’s automated point-defense nodes without being vaporized.

Territory, Assets & Corsair Contracts

The Jailers control the primary interior pathways of the Evergaol Exclusion Zone, fiercely guarding their private tunnels against corporate reclamation units and Flotilla research teams. Signature assets include illicit Precursor relics (superconducting coils, alien sensor arrays), the subterranean Smuggler Trenches bored beneath the crater's sensor perimeter, and heavy tracked Jailer Scrap-Rigs equipped with cutting torches.

While the Jailers view Corsairs with intense wariness as armed lapdogs of the Council, they are above all merchants. When a salvage clan unearths a device that is too dangerous or unstable to keep—such as an active alien beacon heating up bedrock—they quietly contact an independent Corsair cell to buy it off them before corporate death squads arrive.

2. The Cloudsurfers

Two hundred levels beneath the gleaming, rain-washed penthouses of Cloudspire on Nimbus, where corporate directors sip imported wine, the city’s glittering architecture gives way to the "Non-Serviced Sub-Deck Concessions"—a polite bureaucratic term for the dark, damp, and neglected industrial ravines where the air is thick with chemical fog and neon signs flicker with electrical faults. The Flotilla & Law of the Void

JURISDICTION: The High Void, Transit Lanes, & Lagrange Hubs ("The Law of the Void")
AUTHORITY: Supreme Naval Command & Interplanetary Peacekeeping (Skyline Accords)
PRIMARY SEATS: Redmane Station (HQ), Port Zenith Bastion Yards, Waypoint Station (L1)

To the civilian walking the paved streets of Crossroads or gazing out the panoramic windows of Cloudspire, space is an awe-inspiring, silent canvas of amber starlight and swirling gas giant rings. To those wearing the slate-grey uniform of the Flotilla, space is mostly the smell of ozone and machine oil, the taste of lukewarm instant chicory brewed from recycled greywater, and the unyielding magnetic clack-clack of standard-issue steel-toed boots locking onto steel deck plates thirty thousand times a day.

The Flotilla is the sovereign peacekeeper of the void, custodian of the navigational beacon network, and ultimate arbiter of the "Law of the Void." Consecrated by the Skyline Accords of 335 AA, the fleet holds absolute legal authority over open space, orbital lanes, and Lagrange customs hubs. Yet, under constitutional treaty, Flotilla warships are strictly barred from firing into planetary atmospheres or deploying boots onto planetary soil without unanimous consent from the High Council. Down in the mud, planetary governors hold the reins; up in the dark, the Flotilla rules alone.

1. Lived Experience & The One-Year Tour

The Flotilla belongs directly to the civilian population through the One-Year Tour. Every single eighteen-year-old in the system—pampered heirs from Pelagia, red-dust prospectors from Suncatcher, or apprentice carpenters from Crossroads—is drafted for twelve months of mandatory service. Scraping carbon burns off thruster manifolds in zero-g strips away planetary parochialism in weeks: recruits quickly learn that lineage and class mean nothing compared to keeping your tether clipped and remembering which valve vents the secondary airlock. In the crowded bunk modules of Waypoint Station, lifelong bonds are forged between kids from rival moons who otherwise would never have met.

The prevailing naval philosophy is The Discipline of the Hull: space is not cruel, because cruelty requires malice, and the universe is far too busy adhering to orbital mechanics to care about mortal feelings. Survival requires constant vigilance, routine maintenance, absolute honesty regarding fuel reserves, and respect for paperwork filed in triplicate. A leaky gasket does not care about your family name; it simply vents your air into the void until you tighten the bolts.

2. Governance & The Matrix Organization

Daily military command is held by the Admiralty Board at Redmane Station, answering directly to the civilian High Council of colonial delegates who hold the purse strings. Every sailor belongs to both an operational Wing and a technical Corps (Marine, Pilot, Engineering, Medical, Officer, Sailor, Admin).

3. Fleet Doctrine & Key Bases

Dictated by the hard physics of sub-light space travel, with no faster-than-light travel and no artificial gravity, space combat is an unforgiving duel of thermodynamics, vector management, and kinetic geometry. Warships fight while conducting brutal multi-g Brachistochrone burns, launching kinetic railgun slugs across thousands of kilometers while rotary PDCs shred incoming torpedoes before impact.

Key strongholds include Redmane Station (the colossal rotating spindle habitat serving as administrative HQ in high Fume orbit), Port Zenith Bastion Yards (Landfall's orbital drydocks where Fleet-Engineer Kira Abe developed the first prototype MANTLE combat chassis during the Scavenger Wars), and Waypoint Station (L1), processing millions of tons of inter-moon transit. Flagships like the battlecruiser FBS Verdant Dawn anchor system defense.

4. The Present Crisis (518 AA)

In 518 AA, exactly ten years after the Frontier War against the Orkish Empire and the catastrophic fall of the ancient alien dreadnought Evergaol, the Flotilla is stretched razor-thin. Dozens of capital ships were vaporized or crashed onto Landfall; veteran crews were decimated. Today, scarred hulls face deferred maintenance across three systems (Fume, X'ara, Wanabo) while colonial politicians bicker over defense budgets.

5. Relationship with the Corsairs

Born out of the Patrol Wing after the 335 AA Skyline Accords, the Corsair institution was created to bridge the legal seam between planetary atmosphere and open vacuum. With capital fleets depleted in 518 AA, the Flotilla relies on the New Wave of Corsairs more than ever as flexible troubleshooters. Independent crews receive Naval Warrants, universal credit bounties, and ordnance subsidies at Bastion depots. In return, the navy demands strict adherence to rules of engagement: rogue cells face ruthless interdiction under the Law of the Void.

Lex Aetheria: The Law of the Void

Under Article IV of the Skyline Accords, any vessel navigating beyond 100 kilometers altitude enters Flotilla maritime jurisdiction. Transponders must broadcast continuous trajectory telemetry. Tampering with distress beacons, venting cargo without vector authorization, or failing to heave-to upon receiving a naval interlock signal constitutes piracy, carrying immediate forfeit of hull and warrant rights.

Alien Powers & Foreign Empires

JURISDICTION: Neighboring Star Systems, Demilitarized Zones, & Quarantined Moons
AUTHORITY: The Sacred Vigil, Imperial Decrees, The Great Tithe, & Naval Treaties
PRIMARY POWERS: Ancestrals of X'ara, Orkish Empire, Scavenger Clans, Lithari of Karst

To the average citizen living on Landfall or Nimbus, the universe ends at the edge of the Fume cluster. They know twenty-six gas giants turn in the celestial clockwork of the Sphere, but the sixty-million-kilometer void between planetary tracks is so vast that foreign civilizations feel like distant myths—until an Orkish battlecruiser's transponder lights up on an outer sensor buoy, or an Ancestral diplomatic barge slides into a docking ring like an ivory ghost.

The Newcomers are not alone in the Sphere. They were merely the last to arrive. Foreign powers do not operate by Newcomer rules: they dismiss municipal ordinances, treat corporate patent law with contempt, and regard the Flotilla’s Skyline Accords as a quaint local custom.

1. The Ancestrals of X'ara

The oldest surviving civilization in the mapped Sphere resides across the engineered binary worlds of the neighboring X'ara cluster. Ascetic and humanoid with opalescent skin and dark eyes adapted to amber starlight, they live under the rigid theological dogma of the Sacred Vigil, teaching that the universe was perfected by the ancient "Makers" and that mortal expansion, reckless curiosity, and technological tinkering are grotesque sins of hubris. Their capital spires on Irul'lia produce no industrial waste, tapping directly into geothermal dynamos.

Ruled by the immortal Archon, contact in 425 AA sparked the Great Avon Schism across the binary moons Avon 1 and Avon 2: youthful reformers of the Gilded Movement discard traditional robes for colorful vests to visit Fume as enthusiastic "Space Tourists," while militant temple inquisitors of the Pure Blade view mortals as a viral contagion and lobby for a holy crusade to cleanse the noise.

2. The Orkish Empire

Emerging from the outer void beyond the buffer moons of Wanabo, the Orkish Empire is a disciplined, martial autocracy of muscular humanoids with dual circulatory systems and immense physical resilience. Ruled with an iron fist by Emperor Vrakketh, Orkish society is organized around absolute hierarchy, institutional slavery, and normalized martial violence. Clan Warlords oversee regional fleets, settling command disputes through ritual melee before their assembled troops.

Orkish doctrine rejects delicate skirmishing: their heavy-displacement warships feature solid-tungsten Forward Ram-Prows and armored boarding torpedoes designed to smash through hulls, while planetary armies deploy bipedal diesel-fusion combat walkers with pneumatic siege hammers. Following the joint battle against the ancient dreadnought Evergaol in 508 AA, Vrakketh signed the Great Truce, though border tensions in 518 AA remain at a fever pitch across the Wanabo DMZ.

3. The Scavs (Scavenger Raider Clans)

Drifting through the deep void in scrap-welded hulls, the Scavs are a nomadic, cloned raider species biologically adapted to hard vacuum: two meters tall, sinewy, with multi-faceted optical clusters and four fully functional arms capable of enduring minutes of explosive decompression. Governed by the fanatical dogma of The Great Tithe, they believe life is a spiritual debt owed to a mythical master called The Collector. Led by cybernetic Warmasters and obsessive Tithe-Counters, Scav boarding parties crawl through zero-g maintenance chases to strip copper wiring, fusion coils, and Precursor relics from living ships.

4. The Lithari of Karst

Discovered in 365 AA on the sinkhole moon Karst, the Lithari are an indigenous stone-age species of slate-skinned tribal hunters dwelling around subterranean freshwater seas illuminated by bioluminescent fungi. With sensitive acoustic antennae detecting seismic vibrations through stone, they fashion obsidian spears and weave waterproof garments from fungal fibers.

Championed by Goldheart, the Flotilla ratified the Non-Interference Protocol of 368 AA, declaring Karst a Total Quarantine Zone enforced by the Karst L1 Picket Station. Corsairs are contracted under Quarantine Interdiction Writs to extract poachers and bioprospectors without exposing the primitives to starships or modern firearms.

Epilogue: The Three Layers of Reality

To survive in the Sphere, a Corsair must master the tension between three distinct layers of reality that coexist across every moon, station, and derelict hull.

Layer 1: The Everyday Struggle (518 AA). This is the tangible world of the Flotilla, the colonial magistrates, the corporate ledgers, and the ravine gangs. It is measured in credit chits, reaction-mass liters, pressure gaskets, and court warrants. To the average spacer, this is the only world that matters: paying off the ship's mortgage, securing fresh provisions, and returning home alive from the dark.

Layer 2: The Five-Hundred-Year History. This is the cultural memory of the Great Arrival, the Inter-Binary Famine, the Skyline Accords, the emergence of the Draco fusion drive, and the bloody scar of the Frontier War. It is the realization that Newcomer civilization is young, fragile, and standing upon the shoulders of dragons who sacrificed their own celestial kin to preserve mortal life in an alien system.

Layer 3: The Cosmic Singularity & The Monoliths. Deep beneath the crusts and hidden within quarantined exclusion zones lies the ultimate truth: the Sphere was not built for mortals. It is an astronomical computational apparatus designed by ancient Precursors to survive an inevitable cosmic collapse. When ancient dreadnoughts wake and non-baryonic relics hum in the dark, mortal laws dissolve into irrelevance.

The Role of the New Wave. In the fragile peace of 518 AA, the Sphere teeters upon a knife-edge. The Flotilla cannot police the moons; colonial councils cannot secure the high drift; corporations pursue profit over stability; and foreign empires test the borders. Into this vacuum steps the Corsair: independent, sworn to the Charter, and armed with MANTLE chassis and sub-light corvettes to stitch the seams of civilization together before the dark claims them all.

The Shepherd's Legacy. Five centuries ago, the Dragon Children made landfall alongside the mortal arks. Goldheart chose the soil of Landfall, Stormdrinker the clouds of Nimbus, Windreaver the ice shelves of Rime. They guide mortal civilization not as conquering tyrants or distant gods, but as patient elders who understand the ancient astronomical machinery ticking beneath the crust. Their guidance was a solemn commitment—the Shepherd's Gambit: nurturing mortals into self-sufficiency so that one day they might unlock the true purpose of the Sphere.

As Speaker Jesse Haddon once observed during a turbulent council session in Crossroads: "The Flotilla watches the void, the corporate barons watch their ledgers, and the dragons watch the stars. It falls to the Corsairs to watch the doors, because if the doors give way, none of the rest of it matters at all."

Approaching the Forbidden Archives

The dossiers contained in this chapter represent the authorized public knowledge of the Sphere. Turn the page with caution: in the dark of space, the truth has its own gravitational pull.
