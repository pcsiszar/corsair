Downtime & Day-to-Day Operations

Not every hour of a Corsair's career is defined by pressurized breach-charges, howling decompression sirens, or high-stakes firefights through zero-gravity bulkheads. Corsairs are flesh-and-blood operatives who must decompress, treat physical trauma, overhaul battered starships, shop for high-grade ordnance, and scrounge up freelance income to pay dockyard berthing fees. Between operational contracts, play shifts into Day-to-Day pacing. This macro-pacing scope gives players the creative freedom to pursue personal projects, train new competencies, and manage their ship's logistics, while providing Game Masters with a structured engine to track operational runway, simulate a living station economy, and pace campaign arcs.

1. The 24-Hour Downtime Round & Shift Structure

Day-to-Day pacing operates on a unified, macro-scale round structure. A single round represents one full 24-hour day in the game world. At the start of each Day-to-Day round, every player character receives 3 Day-to-Day Action Points (AP). Each Action Point represents an 8-hour operational shift:

Shift Timeframe Operational Focus Example Downtime Actions
Shift 1: Morning 08:00 – 16:00 Primary Operations Medbay recovery, hull repairs, market run
Shift 2: Afternoon 16:00 – 24:00 Secondary Endeavors Freelance gigs, tactical training, study
Shift 3: Night 00:00 – 08:00 Rest, Drills, or Gigs Evening freelance, simulators, relaxation

Free Allocation & Sustenance

Characters may allocate their 3 Day-to-Day AP across any combination of downtime activities (such as training, taking freelance gigs, or shopping). Consuming 1 Supply at the End of the Round covers standard meals, personal hygiene, and healthy sleep patterns distributed naturally across the 24-hour cycle. Operatives do not need to spend an AP on sleep unless they are actively seeking medical recovery from Wounds or Stress through a Long Rest.

The Three Pacing Scopes

In Corsair, the GM dynamically adjusts pacing between three interconnected time scopes. Moment-to-Moment governs tactical combat, room breaches, and high-G dogfights where seconds matter and combatants spend 3 Moment-to-Moment AP. Place-to-Place shifts to hours, governing orbital transit, infiltration approaches, and tactical surveillance. Day-to-Day zooms out to 24-hour cycles, providing the structural canvas for repairs, medical convalescence, and long-range interplanetary burns.

2. Supplies & Operating Runway

A crew's remaining operational runway is measured in Supplies. Rather than burdening the table with separate bookkeeping for protein packs, hydroponic greens, reactor coolant cartridges, and life-support filter scrubbing chemicals, all vessel consumption is unified into a single daily burn rate.

The End-of-Round Heartbeat

At the End of the Round (every 24 hours of in-game time), deduct 1 Supply from the starship’s cargo hold. Paying this 1 Supply sustains environmental life support and refills every crew member's pool back to 3 Day-to-Day AP for the upcoming day.

The Consequences of Zero Supply

If the starship enters a new day with 0 Supplies remaining, the vessel's auxiliary reserves shut down, imposing severe immediate penalties starting that morning:

  • Auxiliary Systems Offline: All passive internal module benefits (medical bays, fabrication workshops, advanced sensors) power down.
  • Repairs Blocked: Starship hull damage cannot be repaired by crew or automation.
  • Crew Deprivation: Life-support scrubbers falter and food rations run dry. Every crew member immediately suffers 1 Light Wound and 1 Light Stress at the start of each unsupplied day.
  • Advancement Frozen: Physical exhaustion and hunger prevent effective practice; characters cannot spend Day-to-Day AP to gain XP or train attributes.
Doctrine // Macro-Pacing & Campaign Momentum

Day-to-Day pacing prevents downtime from devolving into vague hand-waving. By assigning concrete costs to shifts and supplies, every day berthed at a station creates meaningful tension: do the operatives spend their hours resting to clear debilitating wounds, drilling in combat simulators to gain precious XP, or working dockside odd jobs to afford drydock repair fees before their operating runway runs dry?

3. Recovery, Long Rest & Healthcare

Combat injuries and psychological strain are inevitable hazards of the trade. While field dressings and stim-injectors provide rapid triage during tactical engagements, deep healing requires quiet rest and clinical support.

The Long Rest Action

A character who spends 1 Day-to-Day AP resting quietly in their quarters or undergoing treatment in a medical facility executes a Long Rest. To resolve a Long Rest, the player makes a Success Test using Endurance + Willpower. Because attribute pairs always use the lower rating as the base dice pool, an operative with Endurance 4 and Willpower 2 rolls 2 base dice.

Every Success Point (SP) generated on the roll is freely allocated to restore Condition tracks:

  • Light Wounds & Light Stress: Restoring 1 Light segment costs 1 Success Point.
  • Aggravated Wounds & Stress: Clearing 1 Aggravated segment costs 2 Success Points.
  • Clearing Impaired & Crippled: The moment a filled Condition track is cleared of all damage segments, any Impaired (1 Downgrade) or Crippled (2 Downgrades) condition tied to that track is instantly removed.
Facility Tier Upgrades Standard Availability Healthcare Coverage Notes
Crew Bunk / Field +0 Ship quarters, surface tents Baseline self-care; roll base Endurance + Willpower.
Port Clinic +1 Station med-bays, berthing Covered by standard contract berthing & port fees.
Trauma Center +2 Specialized municipal hospitals Covered by sanctioned mission pay & medical riders.
Biosculpting Ward +3 Elite corporate hospital suites Covered by high-tier Corsair sponsor underwriting.

Shipboard MedBays: A starship equipped with an internal MedBay Module provides permanent, autonomous medical care wherever the vessel travels, granting +1, +2, or +3 Upgrades to all Long Rest tests performed aboard (see Starship Internal Modules).

Medical Doctrine // Impairment & Recovery

Entering combat while suffering from Impairment imposes a brutal 1 Downgrade penalty on all tests governed by that attribute. When an operative sustains critical bone fractures or psychic trauma, prioritizing an afternoon shift in a Port Clinic is essential to strip the impairment before the next contract deployment.

4. Training & Character Progression

When operatives have downtime to spare, they can hone their physiological reflexes, technical expertise, or academic mastery. Downtime training allows characters to bridge the gap between combat deployments, steadily earning Experience Points through disciplined study and physical conditioning.

  • Training AP Cost & Reward: Spending 1 Day-to-Day AP on focused physical conditioning, target shooting, flight simulations, or cyber-warfare theory awards 1 Experience Point (1 XP).
  • Strict Daily Limit: To reflect biological and cognitive limits, an operative can earn a maximum of 1 XP from Training per 24-hour round (spending at most 1 AP on training per day). The remaining 2 AP must be allocated to other activities (such as medical rest, ship maintenance, freelance odd jobs, shopping, or personal projects).
  • Closing the Gap: Downtime training serves primarily to close the small remaining XP gap (typically 1 to 2 XP) needed to learn a new Skill rank, purchase an appealing Trait, or increase an Attribute after completing an operational mission.
  • Spending XP: Accumulated XP can be spent at any time during downtime to increase Skills, acquire new Traits, or improve Attributes according to the standard progression ladder:
Progression Element XP Investment Advancement Mechanics & Restrictions
Skill Improvement 2 XP per rank Increases any Skill by +1 rank (up to maximum rank 5).
Acquire New Trait 2 to 8 XP Purchases a new Class Mod or general Trait based on rarity tier.
Attribute Increase 3 × Target Level Cost equals 3 times the new rank (e.g., Rank 2 to 3 costs 9 XP).
Training Ceiling 1 XP / 24 Hours Hard biological daily limit; maximum 1 AP allocated to training per day.

5. Starship Maintenance & The Ship Fund

Starships endure tremendous strain from orbital maneuvers, atmospheric reentry friction, and point-defense fire. Maintaining a space-worthy hull requires daily labor from the crew and access to industrial drydocks.

  • The Ship Fund ($250,000 Credits Baseline): Every Corsair cell begins play with a dedicated $250,000 Credits Ship Fund maintained in an escrow account, earmarked specifically for drydock repairs, hull reconstruction, docking fees, and shared vessel upgrades.
  • Field Self-Repair: Spending 1 Day-to-Day AP, consuming 1 Supply, and using a Mechanic's Kit allows an engineer to roll a Knowledge + Tinkering (or Finesse + Tinkering) Simple Test. Each Hit restores 1 Light Hull segment. Self-repairs cannot clear Aggravated Hull damage.
  • Professional Drydock Overhauls: Berthed at an orbital shipyard, a crew can contract a full overhaul taking 1 Day-to-Day round (24 hours) per Hull track, restoring 1 full Hull track completely (clearing Light and Aggravated Wounds) at a cost of $25,000 Credits per track paid from the Ship Fund.
  • Equipment Upgrades Note: Installing, calibrating, or swapping mastercrafted weapon and armor upgrade kits takes place naturally during downtime and costs 0 AP aboard ship.
Maintenance Tier Time / AP Cost Resource Cost Structural Restoration Rate
Field Patch Repair 1 AP (8 hours) 1 Supply + Tool Kit Restores 1 Light Hull segment per Hit on Tinkering test.
Drydock Overhaul 24 Hours / Track $25,000 / Track Completely restores 1 full Hull track (clears Aggravated).
Module Recalibration 1 AP (8 hours) Standard diagnostics Restores online status for disabled internal modules.
Station Berthing Slip Continuous Contract Escrow Covered by sanctioned missions; $500/day for unauthorized.
Engineering Doctrine // Field vs Drydock Overhauls

Self-repair in open vacuum is vital for patching superficial micro-meteorite breaches and keeping thruster manifolds firing. However, when structural keels buckle under heavy kinetic missile impact, the vessel requires heavy shipyard gantries to unseal hull plates and replace load-bearing titanium frames.

9. Freelance Work & Earning Income

Between major contracts, Corsairs frequently pick up local freelance work to pocket extra credits, learn station rumors, or close the small financial gap ($1,000 to $2,000 Credits) remaining to purchase a weapon upgrade kit. Accepting an odd job takes 1 Day-to-Day AP (one 8-hour shift). The player rolls a Simple Test using the specified Attribute Pair.

  • 1 or More Hits: Earns $500 Credits base pay, plus +$250 Credits for each additional Hit beyond the first.
  • 0 Hits: Earns $0 Credits. The shift is spent without mechanical injury.

  3. Advancing the World Clock: The station calendar advances by one day. New mission contracts arrive via Corsair commlink.

Operational Debrief // Downtime Invariants

1. The 3 AP Economy: Dividing 24 hours into three 8-hour shifts gives players tactical flexibility between medical rest, ship maintenance, training, and gigs.

2. Condition Recovery: Clearing a damaged track instantly strips the debilitating Impaired or Crippled condition from that attribute.

3. Advancement Pacing: The strict 1 XP/day training limit prevents artificial grinding, anchoring progression to real operational contracts.
