The Three Core Invariants

Every rule, hull specification, weapon parameter, and historical document in this book adheres to three foundational pillars:

1. Grounded Hard Sci-Fi Physics

No artificial gravity plating, no faster-than-light drives, and no psychic space magic. Ships generate gravity through continuous axial thrust or centrifugal spin drums. Vacuum is cold, propellant is finite, and heat radiation is life or death.

2. The Omni-Role Philosophy

No operative is locked into a single rigid duty. Every Corsair can fire an emergency sidearm, clear a jammed coolant line, patch an ally’s puncture wound, and negotiate salvage rights at an orbital airlock.

3. Consequences & Inertia

Actions carry irreversible tactical momentum. When armor buckles or kinetic slugs pierce a pressurized bulkhead, characters manage structural stress, decompression tracks, and tactical retreat rather than mindless attrition.

System Introduction

Welcome to the world of Corsair! This rulebook provides you with everything you need to embark on tactical operations, create memorable operatives, and tell high-stakes stories with your friends across the vast expanse of the Sphere.

What is a Roleplaying Game?

A roleplaying game (RPG) is a collaborative storytelling experience. You and your friends will create characters to portray, and together you will navigate a world full of tactical adventure, corporate espionage, harsh planetary exploration, and imminent danger.

One player takes on the role of the Game Master (GM)—often referred to as the Warden or Flight Controller. The GM is the primary storyteller, the referee of the physical rules, and the voice of the non-player characters (NPCs) that inhabit the world. The other players take on the roles of the Player Characters (PCs)—the protagonists of the narrative.

The game unfolds through a structured conversation. The GM describes the surrounding environment, a player states what their character attempts to accomplish, and the GM describes the resulting physical consequences. When the outcome of an action is uncertain or dangerous, the group turns to the dice to determine what happens, using the rules detailed in this book.

There are no winners or losers in a roleplaying game. The shared objective is to create a compelling, tense, and memorable story together.

The Principle of Narration

Dice never roll in a vacuum. A roll represents physical friction: boots catching against grated decking, capacitors whining under peak load, or thruster gimbal bearings fighting reaction torque. Describe what your character physically does before picking up the dice.

The Tone of the Setting

The setting of Corsair is a blend of The Expanse, Mass Effect, and Cowboy Bebop. It is a universe where humanity and fellow Newcomer species arrived 518 years ago into an abandoned solar system of 100+ engineered Earth-mass worlds known as the Sphere.

There is no faster-than-light communication; news travels at the speed of courier frigates. There are no energy shields; point-defense cannons shred incoming railgun slugs into lethal shrapnel clouds. In Corsair, technology is tangible, heavy, and maintained by sweat and solder.

Table Culture & Risk Calibration

Because Corsair operates in a grounded, high-lethality hard sci-fi environment, clear communication between players and the Game Master ensures engaging drama without table friction.

Player Agency & High-Stakes Operations

Operatives in Corsair are skilled professionals, daring scoundrels, or desperate survivors—not expendable cannon fodder. When characters take risks, the consequences should be transparent. Before players roll the dice on a lethal gambit, the GM should clearly state the potential costs: "If this test fails, your mag-clamp disconnects and you drift into Pelagia’s upper cloud layer."

The Fail-Forward Rule

A failed dice roll in Corsair never results in an uninteresting dead end where "nothing happens." Instead, failure introduces new complications: alarms blare, bulkhead seals crack, or ammunition exhausts, forcing the crew to adapt.

Lines, Veils & Calibration

The void is unforgiving, and missions often touch on claustrophobia, vacuum decompression, corporate exploitation, and deep-space isolation. Groups are encouraged to establish safety tools (such as X-Cards or Lines & Veils) during Session Zero to calibrate the intensity of psychological and physical trauma.

How to Use This Rulebook

You do not need to memorize all 320 pages of this book before rolling your first die. We recommend the following reading progression:

  • Step 1: Learn the Resolution System. Read Chapter 01: Core Mechanics to understand how Simple Tests, Gambits, and Teamwork function.
  • Step 2: Build Your Operatives. Head to Chapter 02 and Chapter 03 to choose your MANTLE chassis (Conduit, Hazard, Pilot, Vanguard, or Weaver) and pick starting Traits.
  • Step 3: Gear Up. Equip your operative with firearms, armor, and utility tech in Chapter 04.
  • Step 4: Launch into the Sphere. If you are the Game Master, familiarize yourself with Chapter 06A for Downtime cycles and Chapter 07 for faction dynamics.

Operative Briefing & Lexicon

Every Corsair operating across the Sovereign Colonies, Megacorporate corridors, and the Flotilla picket lines communicates through a standardized vocabulary of survival.

Core Operational Terms

AA (After Arrival): The universal calendar dating system, established 518 years ago when the Newcomer colony arks first entered the Sphere.

Corsair: A chartered independent operative, contractor, or ship-borne freelancer operating under official letters of marque or a freelance license.

MANTLE: Modular Adaptive Neural Transmission Linked Equipment. The proprietary class cybernetic chassis permanently bound to an operative via their Neural Control Chip (NCC).

Reaction Mass (Remass): High-density propellant heated by fusion reactors and expelled through magnetic nozzles. Without Remass, you drift until Pelagia’s gravity well claims you.

The Sphere: The vast engineered star system containing over a hundred Earth-mass worlds arranged in concentric orbital belts by the silent Precursors.

The Warden's Axiom

The vacuum does not negotiate, corporations do not forgive, and inertia never sleeps. Keep your seals lubricated and your heatsinks cleared.

The Three Factions in Brief

The Sovereign Colonies: Proud planetary worlds built upon soil and sweat, fiercely independent and wary of corporate encroachment.

The Megacorporations: Vander & Thorne, Hyperion Agronomics, and Halcyon Bio-Synthetics—transplanetary cartels wielding private fleets and orbital refineries.

The Flotilla: The nomadic armada of the original colony ships that never landed, patrolling the void lanes as the guardians of the Newcomer accords.
