# The Fume Cluster: Orbital Dynamics and Planetary Topography

The Fume cluster is a hyper-dense, engineered orbital node within The Sphere. Centered on the Gas Giant "Fume," this
cluster hosts three distinct binary pairs—a total of six terrestrial worlds—within a single radiation-shielded orbital
track at 800,000 kilometers. Each pair is locked in a 30-hour cycle, resulting in a system defined by extreme tidal
stress, consistent eclipses, and profound atmospheric diversity.

---

## I. The Central Anchor: Fume

Fume is a massive, ringed Gas Giant characterized by a modified metallic hydrogen core that minimizes high-energy
radiation output. Its atmosphere is a turbulent expanse of deep amber and violet clouds, illuminated by the internal
heat of its formation and the reflected light from its twelve sibling Gas Giants. Fume’s magnetosphere is the vital
life-support mechanism for the six worlds in its local track, acting as a buffer against stellar wind while producing
spectacular, permanent aurorae that illuminate the night skies of its orbiting moons.

---

## II. The Binary Pairs

### 1. The Primary Pair (Barycenter): Landfall and Nimbus

Situated at the base of the orbital track, these two worlds provide the system’s baseline terrestrial conditions.

* **Landfall :** Landfall is the most Earth-like of the cluster. It features a balanced distribution of
  continents and oceans, with moderate plate tectonics driven by the tidal pull of its twin. The geography consists of
  temperate forests, mountain ranges, and large, stable basins. Its climate is the most temperate in the Fume cluster,
  with the eclipse serving as a mild seasonal shift.
* **Nimbus :** This world’s geography is dominated by high-altitude plateaus separated by deep,
  vertical ravines. Because of the planet’s unique atmospheric density, heavy, inert gases collect in these low-lying
  ravines, creating "atmospheric lakes" that possess a liquid-like viscosity. The high-altitude plateaus are relatively
  thin-aired and arid, while the ravines are sheltered, pressurized micro-environments.

### 2. The Leading Pair (L4 Trojans): Pelagia and Suncatcher

These worlds occupy the leading Lagrangian point, forming the most climatically polarized binary in the cluster.

* **Pelagia :** Pelagia is a world composed almost entirely of water. Shallow, interconnected island
  chains span the equator, but the poles are marked by massive, deep-sea trenches. The planet possesses high thermal
  inertia, remaining largely indifferent to the hourly solar eclipse. Its atmosphere is thick, humid, and rich in
  oxygen, supporting expansive, semi-aquatic flora.
* **Suncatcher :** Pelagia’s twin is a brutal contrast: a planet of low albedo, dominated by vast,
  heat-absorbing desert basins and savannah-like equatorial plateaus. Its surface features very low thermal inertia; it
  experiences rapid thermal spikes during solar exposure and steep cooling during the eclipse. Its landscape is defined
  by vast, wind-scoured plains and permanent dust storms.

### 3. The Trailing Pair (L5 Trojans): Karst and Rime

This pair showcases the extreme effects of gravitational tidal friction on planetary crusts.

* **Karst :** Karst’s surface is a landscape of collapse. Centuries of tidal flexing have
  shattered the crust into a network of massive cenotes and sinkholes. The surface is essentially a barren, razor-sharp
  plateau. However, the true geography of Karst lies beneath; the planet’s interior is hollowed into massive, cavernous
  networks filled with geothermally heated inland seas and bioluminescent forests.
* **Rime :** Rime is defined by its extreme albedo. The Outer Face is locked under a permanent,
  kilometers-thick ice sheet that reflects the majority of incoming stellar radiation. The planet’s geography is a
  gradient of temperature: the outer hemisphere is a frozen wasteland, while the Inner Face features a single,
  geothermally maintained circular ocean. This coastal ring is the only habitable region, pinned between the encroaching
  glacial walls and the tidal-warmed sea.
