# Faction Dossier: The Underworld & Fringe Syndicates

```
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| JURISDICTION: UNPOLICED RAVINES, CRASH EXCLUSION ZONES, BADLANDS, & THE DEEP SHADOW|
| AUTHORITY: RAW PHYSICAL FORCE, SYNDICATE LEDGERS, & THE BLACK-MARKET EXCHANGE     |
| PRIMARY FACTIONS: THE JAILERS OF EVERGAOL, THE CLOUDSURFERS, THE IRON FANG,       |
|                   THE DESERT WRAITHS, THE CHILDREN OF THE LEVIATHAN               |
+-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

Wherever formal law draws a boundary, human—and non-human—ingenuity immediately invents a way to crawl beneath it. In the Sphere, the "Underworld" is not a single, tidy criminal syndicate with a dark-suited godfather sitting behind a mahogany desk. It is a sprawling, pragmatic, and remarkably stubborn ecosystem of people who looked at the high price of legal air, legal food, and legal docking permits, decided the mathematics were unacceptable, and resolved to make their own arrangements.

Crime in the Sphere is rarely ideological. It is driven by the brutal thermodynamics of the frontier: if the corporate monopoly on Nimbus charges twenty credits for an atmospheric filter that costs two credits to stamp from scrap sheet, someone in the foggy lower ravines will inevitably figure out how to steal the stamping die. And if the Flotilla outlaws the trafficking of un-sanitized Precursor tech under naval quarantine and colonial safety edicts, someone on Landfall will happily risk catastrophic cellular radiation to haul an alien focusing coil out of a smoldering impact crater, because that single humming cylinder of superconducting alien circuitry can buy a family fresh groceries and a decommissioned hopper for ten years.

The underworld exists in the cracks between the great powers: in the three kilometers of toxic, unpoliced fog beneath Cloudspire's gleaming skywalks; in the trackless, red-dust canyons of Suncatcher's Badlands where corporate crawlers fear to tread; in the creaking shantytown reef-barges of Pelagia; and in the radioactive exclusion zones of Landfall where automated alien kill-nodes still hunt the unwary.

Below are the five major fringe syndicates, outlaw networks, and illicit factions that operate in the shadow of the Sphere.

---

## 1. The Jailers of Evergaol

### Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos
If you ask an honest citizen in Crossroads about the **Evergaol Crash Site**, they will cross their fingers, murmur a quiet prayer of thanks to Goldheart, and change the subject. The crash site is a continent-scale scar on Landfall’s crust where the ancient automated dreadnought was brought down in 508 AA. It is a restricted exclusion zone of jagged black metal, active alien defense drones, and bizarre energy leaks.

The **Jailers** are the only people in the Sphere insane enough to call it home.

Composed of eccentric salvagers, renegade scientists, and mutated survivors who refused to abandon the crash perimeter, the Jailers dwell inside the hollowed-out titanium ribbing and cavernous cooling decks of the fallen dreadnought. Their cultural philosophy is a strange blend of scavenger pragmatism and pseudo-religious reverence for alien technology. They view the *Evergaol* not as an apocalyptic war grave, but as an infinite, miraculous junkyard sent by the gods to provide for anyone brave enough to pry open a bulkhead. 

They are notoriously weird, foul-mouthed, and deeply superstitious. They paint crude warning runes on airlocks with engine grease, wear patchwork hazard suits adorned with salvaged Precursor fiber-optics that pulse with faint amber light, and speak a rapid, clipped dialect filled with garbled machine-code terms.

### Leadership & The "Bloodmages"
The Jailers have no central council; they operate as autonomous salvage clans that convene around shared barter-hearths deep inside the wreck. However, authority within the clans belongs unconditionally to the **Jailer Bloodmages**. 

Despite their terrifying, occult-sounding title, Bloodmages possess no mystical magic. They are veteran cyberneticists and scavengers who have performed reckless, illegal home-brew surgery upon their own bodies: grafting active Precursor neural shunts and salvaged thermal conduits directly into their nervous systems and circulatory tracks. By siphoning electrical energy from their own biological metabolic heat and directing it through these subcutaneous alien filaments, a Bloodmage can project short-range kinetic displacement barriers (the "Crimson Shield") or vent concentrated, high-velocity thermal arcs from their hands (the "Piercing Blood"). 

This biological interface takes a horrific physical toll: their veins bulge with dark, iridescent fluid, their skin turns ash-pale, and they suffer from violent auditory hallucinations caused by the humming coils. To outsiders, they look like terrifying warlocks; to their clans, they are living gods who can walk past the dreadnought’s automated point-defense nodes without being vaporized.

### Territory, Illicit Goods & Signature Assets
The Jailers control the primary interior pathways of the Evergaol Exclusion Zone, fiercely guarding their private tunnels against corporate reclamation units and Flotilla research teams.

* **Illicit Precursor Relics:** The primary source of the Sphere’s black-market alien hardware: superconducting power conduits, exotic energy cells, and alien sensor arrays, sold to highest bidders for astronomical sums.
* **The Smuggler Trenches:** A maze of subterranean drainage tunnels bored beneath the crater's sensor perimeter, allowing couriers to smuggle artifacts into Crossroads.
* **Jailer Scrap-Rigs:** Heavy tracked vehicles reinforced with jagged alien hull plates and equipped with heavy cutting torches, used to drag massive machinery out of the hot zones.

### Relationship with Corsairs
The Jailers view Corsairs with intense wariness, seeing them as the armed lapdogs of the Crossroads Council or the corporate boards. However, the Jailers are above all merchants: they know Corsairs possess nimble ships, and Corsairs know Jailers possess the only keys to the alien vaults. 

When a salvage clan unearths a device that is too dangerous or too unstable to keep—such as an active alien beacon that begins heating up the surrounding bedrock—they will quietly contact an independent Corsair cell to buy it off them before corporate death squads arrive. Conversely, Corsairs are frequently hired by the Council or the SRC to venture into the exclusion zone to hunt rogue Jailers who have stolen critical quarantine hardware.

## 2. The Cloudsurfers

### Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos
Two hundred levels beneath the gleaming, rain-washed penthouses of Cloudspire on Nimbus, where the corporate directors sip imported wine, the city’s glittering architecture gives way to the "Non-Serviced Sub-Deck Concessions"—a polite bureaucratic term for the dark, damp, and neglected industrial ravines where the air is thick with industrial fog and the neon signs flicker with electrical faults.

This is the sovereign sky of the **Cloudsurfers**.

The Cloudsurfers are the iconic youth counter-culture, smuggler guild, and aerial gang of Nimbus. Mounted on custom-fabricated magnetic-repulsor hoverboards powered by modified drone turbine batteries, these daredevils soar through the vertical abyss of the city at breakneck speeds. They glide between titanium foundation pylons, ride the thermal updrafts of massive ventilation stacks, and dart through the narrow gaps between mid-deck skybridges, daring Directorate police craft to chase them into the treacherous gloom.

Their culture is loud, rebellious, and fiercely anti-authoritarian. Cloudsurfers dress in colorful, oversized windbreakers patched with thermal lining, wear high-altitude rebreathers sprayed with custom graffiti, and communicate through complex aerial hand-signals and encrypted short-range radio clicks. They despise the Arcology Directorate and the corporate cartels, viewing their monopoly on pressurized air and high-altitude real estate as an intolerable insult.

### Leadership & Internal Power Structure
The Cloudsurfers are not an army; they are divided into dozens of neighborhood "crews" (such as the *Vapor Hawks*, the *Pylon Rats*, and the *Neon Drifters*) that claim specific ventilation shafts, billboard catwalks, and drainage manifolds as their turf. Turf disputes are settled not with lethal firefights, but through high-stakes "High-Deck Runs"—perilous hoverboard races through obstacle-laden transit tunnels where the loser forfeits their board and their courier route.

Leadership within a crew belongs to the "Wing-Lead"—the fastest, most daring rider who has pulled off the most brazen stunts. While they engage in graffiti tagging, petty vandalism, and illegal street racing, the older, more experienced crews operate as the most efficient, decentralized courier service in the Fume cluster.

### Territory, Illicit Goods & Signature Assets
The Cloudsurfers dominate the Ravines of Nimbus, from Level 50 down to the suffocating "atmospheric lakes" at the bottom of the chasms.

* **High-Speed Black-Market Logistics:** When an executive wants to smuggle a stolen data chip past Directorate customs, or when a back-alley clinic needs un-metered pharmaceuticals delivered within twenty minutes, they hire a Cloudsurfer courier.
* **Custom Repulsor Boards:** Highly modified, lightweight hoverboards utilizing magnetic ground-effect coils and miniature solid-fuel rocket boosters, capable of agile vertical flight.
* **The Pylon Hangars:** Makeshift communal roosts welded onto the blind sides of massive suspension towers, inaccessible to anything that cannot fly.

### Relationship with Corsairs
To most Corsairs, Cloudsurfers are an absolute nightmare to catch: they are faster than a patrol cruiser in tight alleyways, they know every shortcut through the city’s ventilation shafts, and they have three thousand meters of vertical airspace to dive into if cornered. 

However, clever Corsair cells quickly learn that Cloudsurfers make invaluable allies. Because they spend eighteen hours a day soaring through the city’s underbelly, they see everything: which corporate lab had its lights on at midnight, which private shuttle landed on an unregistered pad, and where the syndicate hitmen are hiding. A Corsair who buys a round of hot noodles at a mid-deck kiosk and treats a crew with respect can purchase the finest reconnaissance network on Nimbus for a handful of credit chits.

## 3. The Iron Fang Syndicate

### Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos
Where the Jailers are strange hermits and the Cloudsurfers are rebellious daredevils, the **Iron Fang** represents organized, professional, and corporate-scale crime. Spanning the entire Fume cluster and maintaining branch operations in the frontier boomtowns of Wanabo, the Iron Fang is the undisputed heavyweight of interplanetary contraband.

The Iron Fang operates not like street thugs, but like a shadow megacorporation. They maintain audited accounts, enforce strict non-disclosure contracts, employ top-tier maritime lawyers, and conduct their business in private luxury suites aboard orbital transit stations. Their philosophy is pure, cold capitalism stripped of any legal pretense: *The law is simply a tariff imposed by governments that lack the efficiency to compete on an open market.*

The Iron Fang fills every profitable vacuum created by Flotilla regulations: they run black-market shipyards that scrape transponders off stolen corvettes; they traffic unregistered military coilgun munitions to private corporate militias; they smuggle refined helium-3 past naval customs pickets; and they fence stolen Precursor technology to foreign buyers across the border.

### Leadership & Internal Power Structure
The syndicate is steered by the **Fang Directorate**, a secret council of seven criminal bosses based in the commercial underbelly of **Port Zenith** and the high-end gaming clubs of Cloudspire. The current presiding figure is **Syndic Anton Drake**, a charming, middle-aged Naga gentleman who wears bespoke Chidori-Qwei suits, speaks five languages fluently, and has never physically held a firearm in his life. 

Enforcement is carried out by the **Iron Guard**—professional mercenary squads composed of dishonorably discharged Flotilla marines and corporate security defectors. Equipped with mil-spec kinetic rifles, smart-linked body armor, and heavy breaching tools, Iron Guard enforcers operate with surgical, ruthless discipline. When the Iron Fang decides to eliminate a debtor or silence an informant, they don't engage in messy shootouts; they stage a sudden, tragic "atmospheric seal failure" in the victim's apartment.

### Territory, Illicit Goods & Signature Assets
The Iron Fang operates across the entire system, utilizing unmarked cargo lighters, secret station bulkheads, and hidden asteroid waystations.

* **Heavy Black-Market Arms Dealing:** Military-grade railgun capacitors, un-serialized kinetic ammo, stealth hull baffles, and refurbished Draco drive pre-igniters.
* **The Zenith Laundromat:** A network of legitimate commercial front companies on Port Zenith—freight brokerage firms, recycling plants, and drydocks—used to wash billions in illicit credits.
* **Ghost Corvettes:** Fast, stripped-down blockade runners equipped with oversized fusion drives and sensor-absorbing paint, capable of outrunning Flotilla patrol cutters in open space.

### Relationship with Corsairs
The relationship between the Iron Fang and the Corsairs is one of intense, dangerous professionalism. On paper, the Iron Fang is the premier target for Flotilla and colonial Corsair warrants; capturing an Iron Fang syndic or seizing a weapons cache brings massive bounties and system-wide prestige.

In practice, the line is often uncomfortably blurry. The Iron Fang frequently acts as an anonymous information broker: if a psychotic pirate gang is raiding commercial lanes and disrupting business, the Fang will quietly leak the pirate’s flight vector to a trusted Corsair cell, letting the lawmen do the dirty work for free. Many a Corsair has found an encrypted data chit slipped under their cabin door, offering the coordinates of an outlaw base alongside a polite note: *"A mutual problem requires a surgical solution. Happy hunting."*

## 4. The Desert Wraiths

### Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos
Out beyond the protective walls of Dustwall on Suncatcher lies the **Badlands**—a vast, searing hellscape of red silica dust, sheer sandstone canyons, and blistering solar winds where the temperature swings eighty degrees in thirty hours. 

To corporate mining directors, the Badlands are a desolate wasteland to be exploited with heavy tracked crawlers. To the **Desert Wraiths**, the Badlands are holy ground.

The Wraiths are an insular, nomadic federation of survivalist tribes composed of Craggen outcasts, Naga desert-dwellers, and third-generation prospector families who abandoned corporate company towns to live free in the canyons. They view corporate mining cartels—particularly Caldera Foundry—as rapacious parasites who poison water aquifers, shatter sacred geological strata, and treat living beings like disposable tools.

The Wraiths are austere, silent, and deadly. They dress in layered, dust-caked sand-cloaks that conceal their environmental hazard suits, wear panoramic rebreather masks equipped with polarized polarized filters, and travel across the dunes on high-speed, solar-charged sand-skimmers equipped with wide balloon tires.

### Leadership & Guerrilla Tactics
The Wraiths are organized into family clans led by **Elder Sand-Speakers**—weathered patriarchs and matriarchs who navigate by celestial constellations and the acoustic reverberations of the wind through the canyons. 

In combat, the Wraiths are terrifying ambush predators. Rather than engaging heavy corporate mining crawlers in open kinetic slugfests, they utilize the environment. Their signature tactical tool is the **Veil of Sand**: deploying specialized aerosol canisters that release dense clouds of polarized, abrasive silica particulate. These clouds completely blind radar, scramble infrared sensors, and strip optical cameras to bare static, plunging enemy vehicles into sensory darkness. Emerging from the dust cloud on foot, Wraiths scale the crawler’s hull, disable the external radiator loops with thermal charges, and force the crew to surrender by threatening to vent their environmental seals.

### Territory, Illicit Goods & Signature Assets
The Wraiths claim the deep canyon networks of the Great Suncatcher Badlands, the Sunken Basins, and the hidden subterranean water aquifers.

* **Hijacked Mining Heavy Equipment:** Over the decades, the Wraiths have captured and modified dozens of massive corporate tracked crawlers, converting them into mobile, subterranean fortress-villages hidden beneath rock overhangs.
* **The Badland Aquifers:** Secret, subterranean geothermal springs that provide the only clean, un-metered drinking water on Suncatcher.
* **Pneumatic Harpoon Launchers:** Heavy, shoulder-fired magnetic harpoons designed to puncture pressurized vehicle cabins and tow disabled vehicles across sand flats.

### Relationship with Corsairs
The Desert Wraiths generally treat all off-worlders with hostile suspicion, assuming anyone carrying a firearm is a Caldera mercenary sent to claim their water. Corsairs who accept standard corporate "bandit extermination" bounties will find themselves lured into narrow slot canyons and ambushed with brutal efficiency.

However, the Wraiths are bound by ancient desert hospitality: if a Corsair cell rescues a stranded sand-scout, respects their water laws, or helps them defend a settlement from illegal claim-raiders, the Sand-Speakers will grant them the status of "Dune-Kin." A Corsair crew with Dune-Kin standing gains safe passage through the Badlands, access to hidden water caches, and guidance across treacherous terrain that no corporate satellite can map.

## 5. The Children of the Leviathan

### Criminal Profile & Outlaw Ethos
In the creaking, salt-rimed shantytown flotillas that cling to Pelagia's equatorial reefs, life is fragile, damp, and exhausting. You spend twelve hours a day scraping algae from nylon nets, your skin is permanently raw from salt spray, and the Land Barons on their opulent pontoon estates raise the price of fresh water every time a typhoon damages their tennis courts.

Under these conditions, it is easy to look down into the deep, emerald ocean and look for a higher power.

The **Children of the Leviathan** are a clandestine, fanatical maritime cult that operates across the lower decks of Pelagia. They worship the **Leviathan**—the colossal, ancient biological titan that slumbers in the Mariana Abyssal Trench four kilometers beneath the surface. To the cultists, the Leviathan is not an alien biological hazard; it is the living soul of the world, a slumbering god that drowned the Sandwyrm during the ancient Inter-Binary Famine and preserved the oceans for mortal life.

Their religious dogma teaches that the surface world is a corrupt, temporary illusion, and that one day the Leviathan will rise from the abyss to cleanse the ocean of the Land Barons’ floating palaces. While ordinary citizens dismiss them as mad reef-scum, the cult possesses a terrifying, blind devotion that makes them unpredictable and dangerous.

### Leadership & Abyssal Zealotry
The cult is guided by the **Trench Disciples**, charismatic, wild-eyed preachers who claim to hear the "Song of the Deep"—sub-audible acoustic reverberations that vibrate through the water column from the Mariana trenches. 

The cult operates in absolute secrecy within hollowed-out coral caverns and flooded ballast tanks of abandoned barges. Contrary to popular rumor, the cultists possess no magical ocean powers; what they have is an absolute, terrifying indifference to death. A Leviathan zealot will happily strap acoustic charges to their chest and dive into a luxury yacht's propeller intake, smiling as the hull buckles, convinced their soul will be embraced by the titan in the deep dark.

### Territory, Illicit Goods & Signature Assets
The cult operates out of the Equatorial Reef Slums, submerged grottoes, and derelict industrial barges outside the main shipping lanes.

* **Maritime Sabotage Hardware:** Heavy acoustic limpet mines, underwater cutting torches, and fast, stripped-down submersible sea-scooters used to attack offshore infrastructure.
* **Abyssal Bioluminescent Narcotics:** Crude chemical hallucinogens brewed from deep-sea vent fungi and bioluminescent algae, traded to off-world syndicates to finance weapons purchases.
* **The Submerged Grottos:** Secret, air-filled underwater caverns accessible only by free-diving through treacherous reef labyrinths, used as weapon caches and temples.

### Relationship with Corsairs
The Children of the Leviathan view Corsairs as armed mercenaries hired by the Land Barons to protect corporate greed, making them frequent targets for ambush. The Tidewarden Guild and the Pontoon Assembly regularly issue **Anti-Cult Suppression Writs**, tasking Corsairs with clearing out submerged saboteur nests, securing freshwater desalinization plants, and protecting offshore drilling platforms.

However, the cult can also be an unsettling source of intelligence. When deep-core corporate drilling accidentally breaches an ancient Precursor vault on the sea floor, the cultists are often the first to notice the energetic anomalies. A Corsair cell willing to wade through fanatical rhetoric and bizarre sea-chants can sometimes uncover vital clues about ancient undersea ruins that no official database possesses.
