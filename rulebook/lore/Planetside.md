# Orbital Mechanics and Surface Conditions

Habitation on a binary moon within the Sphere is defined by its proximity to the sister planet and the host gas giant.
These factors influence lighting, tides, and day-night cycles.

---

### 1. Tidal Locking: The Sister Planet

Earth-sized worlds in binary pairs are **tidally locked**, meaning the sister planet remains stationary in the sky
relative to a surface observer.

* **Visual Scale:** At 40,000 km, the sister planet appears approximately **40 times larger** than Earth's moon.
  Atmospheric weather systems and surface features are visible to the naked eye.
* **Phase Cycle:** The sister planet completes a full cycle of phases every 30 hours. During the "Full Sister" phase,
  reflected light is sufficient to illuminate the landscape and cast defined shadows.
* **Hemispheric Visibility:** The sister planet is only visible from the "Inner Face" of the world. On the "Outer Face,"
  the planet remains below the horizon.

---

### 2. The Host: The Gas Giant

The **Gas Giant** serves as the primary celestial backdrop. Its visibility and scale depend on the moon's specific
orbital position.

* **Scale:** The Gas Giant appears nearly **20 times larger** than a full moon from Earth. Observers can track the
  transits of other moons across its surface.
* **Planetshine:** During solar night, the Gas Giant reflects amber light from the central star. This "Planetshine"
  provides low-level ambient illumination across the surface.

---

### 3. The Daily Shadow

The primary rhythm of the 30-hour cycle is the **Daily Shadow**, a localized eclipse caused by the binary twin passing
between the surface and the sun.

* **Duration:** The eclipse lasts approximately one hour every 30-hour cycle.
* **Conditions:** During the shadow, surface temperatures decrease and atmospheric wind patterns shift. This period is
  used as a standard rest interval for colonial personnel.

---

### 4. Solar Conditions: The Orange Dwarf

The central **Orange Dwarf star** provides consistent solar radiation at the red end of the visible spectrum.

* **Illumination:** The light is amber-shifted, making reds and golds highly vibrant while greens appear darker.
* **Thermal Output:** The star's heat is less intense than a yellow sun's, allowing for extended surface activity
  without the high UV exposure typical of hotter stars.

---

### 5. Gravitational Influence: The Tidal Bulge

Gravity shapes the world's geography through a permanent tidal effect.

* **Oceanic Distribution:** The sister planet’s proximity creates a **Permanent Tidal Bulge** toward the center of
  gravity. Oceans do not ebb and flow but remain localized in this bulge.
* **Hemispheric Geography:** The "Inner Face" features higher sea levels and flooded valleys, while the "Outer Face"
  contains exposed coastlines and shallower basins.

---

### 6. Magnetic Shielding: Permanent Auroras

Moons orbiting within the Gas Giant's magnetic field experience constant atmospheric radiation deflection.

* **Atmospheric Effects:** Near the poles, auroras are a permanent feature. Due to the atmospheric composition and the
  amber solar light, these auroras typically appear in crimson and gold spectrums.
