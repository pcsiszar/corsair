# Life on a Binary Moon: A Colonist’s Perspective

Standing on the surface of a world within the Sphere is a sensory experience that defies traditional planetary logic.
Because of the massive scale of the celestial bodies involved, the sky is not a distant void, but a crowded, moving
landscape.

---

### 1. The Constant Neighbor: The Sister Planet

Because the two Earth-sized worlds are **tidally locked**, the Sister Planet does not rise or set. If you are on the
side of the world facing it, it is a permanent fixture of your life.

* **A World in the Sky:** At only 40,000 km away, the Sister Planet appears roughly **40 times larger** than the Moon
  looks from Earth. It is so close that you can watch its weather systems crawl across continents, see the glow of its
  evening lights, and distinguish its oceans from its forests with the naked eye.
* **The 30-Hour Phase Cycle:** The Sister Planet goes through a full cycle of phases—from a thin silver crescent to a "
  Full Sister"—every 30 hours. When it is "Full," it reflects so much light that it can cast shadows as sharp as the
  sun’s.
* **The Invisible Hemisphere:** On the opposite side of your world, the Sister Planet is never seen. To the people
  living there, it is a legend or a destination they must travel halfway around the globe to witness.

---

### 2. The Horizon Wall: The Gas Giant

While the Sister Planet is a constant companion, the **Gas Giant** is the backdrop of the entire sky. Depending on your
moon's orbital position, the Giant looms over the horizon like a colossal, curved wall of amber and violet clouds.

* **The Looming Scale:** The Gas Giant appears nearly **20 times larger** than a full moon. You can watch the transit of
  other moons as tiny black dots crossing its face, or track the rotation of storms larger than your entire world.
* **Planetshine:** During the "night" (when your side of the planet faces away from the sun), the Gas Giant reflects an
  immense amount of amber light. This "Planetshine" is bright enough to see your surroundings clearly, bathing the
  landscape in a soft, colored glow that makes true darkness a rarity.

---

### 3. The Daily Eclipse: The Sister's Shadow

The most distinct rhythm of life is the **Daily Shadow**. This is not a sunset, but a sudden, localized eclipse caused
by the two planets aligning with the star.

* **The Mid-Day Twilight:** Every 30 hours, the Sister Planet passes directly in front of the Orange Sun. For roughly an
  hour, the sun vanishes.
* **The Rest Hour:** During this eclipse, the temperature drops, the wind shifts, and the brightest stars become visible
  in a violet sky. This "False Night" is a universal time of rest for colonists, a quiet break in the heat of the day
  before the sun re-emerges.

---

### 4. The Golden Light: The Orange Sun

The **Orange Dwarf star** at the center of the Sphere provides a light that feels fundamentally "heavier" and warmer
than a typical yellow sun.

* **Eternal Afternoon:** Because the star is cooler and its light is shifted toward the red end of the spectrum, the
  world feels like it is caught in a permanent sunset. Greens appear deeper and more teal, while reds and golds are
  incredibly vibrant.
* **The Soft Hearth:** The heat from the sun is less "piercing" than a yellow star's. It feels like the radiating warmth
  of a fireplace, allowing for long periods of activity without the harsh glare or high UV burn typical of hotter stars.

---

### 5. The Permanent Tide: The Gravitational Bulge

Gravity isn't just an invisible force here; it shapes the very geography of the world.

* **The Ocean Mountain:** Because the Sister Planet's gravity always pulls from the exact same direction, the oceans do
  not ebb and flow. They are pulled into a **Permanent Tidal Bulge** on the side facing the twin.
* **High and Low Hemispheres:** The "Inner Face" of the world has significantly higher sea levels, often drowning
  low-lying valleys, while the "Outer Face" features vast, exposed coastlines and shallower seas.

---

### 6. The Eternal Auroras: The Magnetic Shield

Because the planets orbit within the massive magnetic field of the Gas Giant, the sky is constantly active with
radiation being deflected away from the surface.

* **The Sky Curtains:** Near the poles, auroras are not rare—they are a permanent feature. Because of the specific gases
  in the atmosphere and the orange light of the sun, these auroras often glow in deep crimsons and ambers, appearing
  like slow-moving fire in the upper atmosphere.
