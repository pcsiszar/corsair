# Locations: The Worlds and Corridors of the Sphere

If you ask an astrophysicist at the Sphere Research Center to describe where you live, they will lean back, clean their spectacles with a microfiber cloth, and sketch a cosmic masterpiece. They will draw you an amber dwarf star of exquisite stability, ringed by twenty-six colossal gas giants locked in a mathematical hymn called the 4:2:1 Laplace resonance. They will point to eighty-thousand-kilometer safe orbits where tidal forces are neatly balanced against magnetic sheaths, and show you binary Earth-mass twins that dance around shared barycenters every thirty hours without ever once crashing into each other. They will call it the greatest feat of gravitational engineering in the known universe, and they will be entirely correct.

If you ask an orbital shuttle pilot hauling fifty tons of fertilizer from Crossroads to Pelagia, however, they will tell you that the Sphere is a cosmic nuisance invented by someone who had clearly never smelled raw fish in microgravity. To the pilot, the Sphere is the thirty-minute panic window when your sister planet blotted out the sun and your solar arrays dropped seventy percent efficiency while you were trying to line up with a docking collar. It is the permanent, dull headache caused by switching between the 0.98g of Landfall's continental dirt and the zero-g spin-lock of an L1 cargo hub. And above all, it is the maddening knowledge that no matter which moon you land on, the port authority will still make you file four separate atmospheric quarantine manifests in triplicate before they let you step off the gantry.

Civilization in the Sphere does not cling to a single cradle. It is scattered across more than a hundred habitable worlds suspended in the magnetic hollows of twenty-six titans. To navigate this clockwork expanse—as a colonist, a merchant, a fleet officer, or a Corsair running on half a tank of reaction mass—one must understand the ground beneath their magnetic boots, the sister world hanging overhead, and the orbital corridors that link them.

## The Anatomy of the Clockwork System

The Sphere is not a solar system in the traditional, chaotic sense; it is a meticulously calibrated machine running on geological timescales. The entire architecture rests on three foundational structural invariants:

1. **The Amber Anchor and the Resonant Rings**: At the heart of the system sits an Orange Dwarf star (K-type). Cooler, steadier, and far less prone to violent coronal mass ejections than ancient Earth's yellow sun, its amber-tinted light bathes the entire system in warm, long-wavelength illumination. Around this gentle forge orbit twenty-six gas giants arranged into three concentric rings (six in the Inner Ring, eight in the Middle, twelve in the Outer). Because they are locked into an immutable 4:2:1 orbital resonance, their gravitational pulls continuously cancel out systemic drift, ensuring that the densest collection of terrestrial biospheres in the galaxy never wobbles out of alignment.
2. **The 800,000-Kilometer Sanctuary**: Naturally formed gas giants are cosmic meat grinders, surrounding themselves with lethal radiation belts generated by violent metallic hydrogen cores. The builders of the Sphere bypassed this by altering the giants' cores to dampen high-energy dynamos and installing faint inner ring systems that continuously sweep trapped particles from the void. The primary habitable track sits precisely 800,000 kilometers from each gas giant's center—far enough out to escape the inner radiation belts, yet comfortably shielded inside the giant's vast magnetospheric bubble, which effortlessly deflects the stellar wind.
3. **The Binary Dance and the Thirty-Hour Day**: Within each 800,000-kilometer track, habitable worlds come in pairs. Two Earth-mass moons orbit a shared center of gravity separated by exactly 61,000 kilometers. Tidally locked to one another, they present the exact same face to their twin across the void. The gravitational tug pulls the hydrosphere into a permanent tidal bulge: the "Inner Face" facing the sister world is characterized by high sea levels, flooded river deltas, and dense archipelagos, while the "Outer Face" directed toward the cold void features receding shorelines, vast exposed continental basins, and arid plateaus. Once every thirty hours, the sister world passes directly between the moon and the Orange Dwarf, producing a predictable, one-hour total solar eclipse that drops temperatures, shifts trade winds, and signals the traditional rest period for planetary biospheres and working shifts alike.

## The Spacer’s Horizon: Transit and Orbital Geography

Because the system was engineered rather than formed by cosmic accident, interplanetary logistics do not require centuries of cryogenic sleep or fictional faster-than-light engines. Instead, society operates on cold, predictable Newtonian physics and continuous-thrust Brachistochrone burns:

* **Hopping the Binary Gap (61,000 km)**: Jumping between sister worlds is a commuter run. A modest courier vessel burning at 1g reaches the midpoint in just over an hour, flips its main drive plume 180 degrees, and decelerates into the sister world's gravity well. Total transit time is roughly three hours, with a communication delay of less than a quarter of a second. The two worlds effectively share a single media ecosystem, financial exchange, and criminal docket.
* **Traversing the Trojan Expanse (800,000 km)**: In addition to the Primary Binary Pair, each gas giant's 800,000-kilometer track hosts two additional pairs at the stable L4 and L5 Lagrange points—one leading sixty degrees ahead, the other trailing sixty degrees behind. Traveling across this 800,000-kilometer straight line at a standard 1g burn takes approximately **3.5 hours**, with a light-speed communication lag of only **2.6 seconds**. The Primary and Trojan worlds form a tightly integrated interplanetary triangle where freight, agricultural produce, and naval patrols move with the ease of an oceanic shipping line.
* **Crossing the Deep Void (90,000,000 km)**: Journeys between neighboring gas giant clusters—such as the run from the Newcomer cradle of **Fume** to the Ancestral bastion of **X'ara**—cross deep interplanetary space. Under continuous 1g acceleration and deceleration, this run takes approximately **2.2 days**, with a communication lag peaking around five minutes. While requiring robust reaction-mass reserves, radiation shelters, and careful course calculations, the void between clusters is a highway, not a lifetime barrier.
* **The High Ground: L1 Anchorages and Bastion Yards**: Between every binary pair sits the L1 Lagrange point, a gravitational null zone suspended precisely 30,500 kilometers between the sister moons. Here, civilization anchors its heaviest void infrastructure: the sprawling transfer hubs like **Waypoint Station**, high-bandwidth comm relays, and orbital bastion docks like the **Port Zenith Bastion Yards** above Landfall, where capital hulls are overhauled and the first combat MANTLE frames were forged.

## The Theaters of Habitation

Across the vastness of the Sphere, civilization concentrates into distinct regional clusters, each defined by unique ecological adaptations, political borders, and historical scars:

### 1. The Fume Cluster (The Newcomer Heartland)
The home of Newcomer civilization for over five centuries. Centered on the amber-and-violet ringed giant **Fume**, this cluster contains six terrestrial moons arranged across three co-orbital positions:
* **The Primary Pair: Landfall & Nimbus**: **Landfall** is the temperate, Earth-like cradle where the four original seed arks touched down in -4 BA, anchored by the rustic stone-and-timber capital of **Crossroads**, the preserved **Landing Site**, and the scarred, lethal exclusion zone of the **Evergaol Crash Site**. Its twin, **Nimbus**, is a vertical world of high-altitude arid plateaus and dizzying chasms choked with dense, viscous atmospheric lakes, dominated by the gleaming multi-tiered arcologies of **Cloudspire** and the unpoliced, fog-shrouded criminal ravines below.
* **The L4 Leading Pair: Pelagia & Suncatcher**: A stark ecological study in contrasts. **Pelagia** is a warm, oxygen-rich ocean world where aristocratic Land Barons command colossal floating pontoon cities while disenfranchised laborers harvest chemical kelp from equatorial reefs beneath the watchful eye of the deep-sea Leviathan. Its binary partner, **Suncatcher**, is an arid, low-albedo dust world rich in heavy minerals, defined by the fortified crater-mining hub of **Dustwall**, ruthless corporate scrip, and lawless canyon badlands.
* **The L5 Trailing Pair: Karst & Rime**: Extreme environments born of tidal friction. **Karst** is a porous, shattered world of gaping cenotes whose surface is uninhabitable, hiding vast bioluminescent subterranean caverns and inland seas populated by the primitive **Lithari**—strictly guarded under the Flotilla’s **Non-Interference Protocol**. Across the binary gap sits **Rime**, an icy moon whose outer hemisphere is locked beneath kilometers-thick glacial sheets, confining civilization to a narrow geothermal coastal ring along its inner circular ocean.

### 2. The X’ara Cluster (The Ancestral Bastion)
Located across a 2.2-day transit corridor from Fume, **X'ara** is the ancient, sovereign territory of the **Ancestrals**—the ascetic, biologically uniform humanoid civilization that lived in unchanging stillness for millennia under the theological dogma of the Sacred Vigil and their immortal Archon. Unlike Fume's wild ecological diversity, X'ara's six worlds are remarkably uniform Earth-mass environments:
* **The Primary Pair: Irul’lia & Irul’oa (Irul 1 & 2)**: The administrative and military core. Irul 1 features towering spires rising from dark purple taiga forests and houses the only Flotilla diplomatic consulate on the surface; Irul 2 is a heavily fortified redoubt concealing subterranean manufacturing bays and cloning vaults dating back millions of years.
* **The L4 Leading Pair: Avon’ria & Avon’oa (Avon 1 & 2)**: An orbital debris graveyard and cultural convergence zone. Avon 1 is an Ancestral relic world of silicon-based flora where militant hawks and solemn traditionalists hold ritual vigils; Avon 2 is an open, copper-soiled moon home to vibrant Newcomer preservationist communities whose chaotic art festivals have become a rare bridge of friendship between the two species.
* **The L5 Trailing Pair: Oran’ria & Oran’oa (Oran 1 & 2)**: A sacred, strictly protected natural sanctuary. Oran 1 is renowned for monumental Precursor art installations—such as wind-singing geometric stone fields and mirror-polished peaks; Oran 2 consists of cobalt-rich turquoise wetlands inhabited by colossal, unclassified Great Beasts that stand perpetual guard over a slumbering ancient entity.

*(See full cluster dossier: [The X’ara Cluster](X'ara.md))*

### 3. The Wanabo Buffer & The Outer Frontiers
To the west of the civilized corridors lies **Wanabo**, a battered, asteroid-strewn system serving as the demilitarized buffer zone between the Flotilla and the **Orkish Empire**. Ravaged during the grueling decades of the Frontier War (485–508 AA), Wanabo is a frontier of contested mining claims, scarred orbital outposts, and tense military pickets where the fragile **Truce of Iron and Ash** is tested daily by rogue raiders, desperate scavengers, and deep-space prospectors.

## Why the Corridors Belong to Corsairs

If interplanetary transit in the Sphere is relatively swift, interplanetary law is an agonizing labyrinth. Under the historic **Skyline Accords of 335 AA**, the legal fabric of the Sphere was severed cleanly at the clouds:

* **Planetary Administrations** possess absolute sovereignty within their atmospheric gravity wells. Their sheriffs, magistrates, and local militias can dictate taxes, seize contraband, and arrest criminals on the ground—but their authority terminates sixty kilometers straight up. The moment a smuggler's sub-orbital skimmer clears the upper mesosphere, the planetary police must cut their throttles.
* **The Flotilla** rules the void. The Admiralty Board exercises undisputed jurisdiction over orbital transfer lanes, Lagrange customs hubs, and deep-space transit corridors. Yet the Flotilla's warships are massive, capital-scale hulls—heavy railgun frigates and fleet cruisers displacing thousands of tons. Bound to rigid orbital patrol tracks and stretched catastrophically thin following the losses of the Frontier War, a Flotilla warship cannot simply dive into a planetary atmosphere to kick down a warehouse door without triggering a constitutional civil war.

Between the clouds and the high void lies the **seam**—the jurisdictional grey zone where corporate syndicates smuggle illegal tech, fugitive cartels vanish into planetary blind spots, and orbital bounties walk freely on colony cobblestones. 

That seam is where the **Corsairs** operate. Armed with cross-jurisdictional charters, backed by multi-faction sponsors, and flying agile corvettes capable of both high-vacuum burns and atmospheric re-entry, Corsair cells are the only operatives authorized to hunt a target from an orbital station's magnetic docks down into the rain-slicked alleys of Crossroads or the foggy ravines of Cloudspire. To survive out here, a crew must know the orbital tables as well as their weapon profiles.
