# Social Interactions & Negotiations

In *Corsair*, social encounters are never mere speed bumps or passive dice checks. A tense negotiation with an unyielding port authority, an interrogation in the dim hold of a salvage skiff, or an impassioned appeal before a station council should feel every bit as dynamic, tactical, and consequential as a close-quarters firefight. Rather than relying on a single binary roll where a character with high Presence instantly solves a complex dilemma, social encounters utilize the game's core mechanical engines—**Action Points**, **Blocker Effects**, **Pacing**, and **Mental Condition**—to turn words into a high-stakes race against the clock.

This guide provides Game Masters with a structured framework to run social encounters that reward strategic thinking, sharp roleplay, and cooperative teamwork.

---

## The Core Engine: Determination as a Blocker Effect

When player characters attempt to convince, deceive, or intimidate a non-player character, the difficulty of that endeavor depends on how impactful the ask is to the narrative and how sharply it contradicts the NPC's core values, loyalties, and self-preservation. In minor or routine requests, a standard **Simple Test** using an appropriate Attribute Pair (such as **Presence + Presence** or **Presence + Knowledge**) is sufficient: score a single Hit, and the NPC complies.

However, when the stakes matter—when the request asks an NPC to risk their career, turn a blind eye to contraband, betray an employer, or surrender vital codes—the Game Master assigns the NPC **Determination**. Mechanically, Determination functions as a specialized **Blocker Effect** anchored to the specific objective or concession the players are pursuing.

Instead of convincing the NPC with a single test, the players must accumulate a total number of successes equal to the **NPC's Determination + 1**.

```
Total Successes Required = NPC Determination + 1
```

### Setting Determination Ratings

Game Masters should calibrate an NPC's Determination based on how alien or dangerous the request is to the character's mindset and immediate circumstances:

* **Determination 0 (Routine / Aligned):** The request carries minimal risk, aligns with the NPC's duties, or flatters their ego. Resolved with a standard Simple Test (requiring 1 Hit).
* **Determination 1 (Reluctant / Guarded):** The NPC has mild reservations, minor professional liability, or an innate suspicion of outsiders. Requires **2 total Hits** to convince.
* **Determination 2 (Stubborn / Risky):** The request violates protocol, risks financial loss, or involves significant personal discomfort. This is the baseline for most dramatic negotiations. Requires **3 total Hits** to convince.
* **Determination 3 (Hostile / High-Stakes):** The ask asks the NPC to commit a serious crime, compromise their direct superiors, or endanger their livelihood. Requires **4 total Hits** to convince.
* **Determination 4+ (Uncompromising / Fanatical):** The request strikes at the core of the NPC's sworn oaths, personal survival, or bedrock ideology. Breaking through requires overwhelming leverage, exceptional teamwork, and **5 or more total Hits**.

### Chipping Away at Determination

Each Hit (a die showing 8 or higher) rolled on a successful persuasion or coercion test chips away at the NPC's Determination on a 1-for-1 basis, precisely following the core rules for **Removing Effects**. Once the NPC's Determination pool has been reduced to zero, the very next Hit scored—whether rolled as an overflow Hit on the same test or achieved on a subsequent test—breaks their resolve and accomplishes the social objective.

For instance, if an evasive dockmaster possesses Determination 2, the players must score a total of 3 Hits across the scene. If an initial persuasion roll yields 2 Hits, both points of Determination are stripped away, leaving the dockmaster visibly wavering and vulnerable to a decisive closing argument that scores 1 final Hit.

### Hidden vs. Gauged Determination

Game Masters do not need to reveal an NPC's numeric Determination value outright. Instead, describe the character's demeanor, rigid body language, or defensive excuses to convey their stubbornness narratively. Players who wish to deduce exactly how entrenched an adversary is can spend an Action Point to make an analytical test (see *Player Maneuvers* below) to read their tells, gauge their remaining Determination, and uncover the motivations underpinning their resistance.

## The Ticking Clock: Patience and Rounds

Social interactions in *Corsair* do not take place in an infinite vacuum. High-pressure conversations occur under the strict scrutiny of ticking clocks: guards are on patrol, docking clamps are scheduled to release, security cameras are recording, or the NPC simply has better things to do than listen to armed mercenaries banter.

To reflect this urgency, social encounters are structured in **Moment to Moment** pacing using **Rounds** and **Action Points**. The Game Master establishes a telegraphed **Patience** limit—typically **1 to 2 Rounds**.

At the conclusion of each Round, the NPC burns 1 point of Patience. If the players fail to clear the NPC's Determination and land the final convincing Hit before Patience reaches zero, the window of opportunity snaps shut. The NPC terminates the conversation, raises an alarm, orders their guards to draw weapons, or walks away entirely.

### Dynamic Shifts in Patience

While the round clock represents the natural passage of time, the GM should modulate Patience dynamically based on the characters' conduct:

* **Burning Extra Patience:** Severe social blunders—such as an overtly offensive remark, an awkward or clumsy lie caught in real time, or pulling a weapon inappropriately—instantly burns an additional Round of Patience, escalating the scene immediately.
* **Buying Time:** Offering a tangible sweetener, demonstrating mutual professional respect, or presenting an immediate, non-threatening reason for the NPC to delay their decision can extend Patience by 1 Round, granting the cell precious breathing room to close the deal.

## Player Maneuvers: Beyond Direct Persuasion

During their turn in a social round, players possess 3 Action Points each. Rather than blindly hammering the NPC with direct persuasion rolls that risk burning the clock on failures, clever crews mix direct advocacy with tactical maneuvers to tilt the odds in their favor.

### 1. Direct Persuasion (1 AP)

A character steps forward to deliver an argument, appeal to emotion, or issue an ultimatum. The player narrates their case, and the Game Master calls for an appropriate Simple Test (such as **Presence + Presence** for raw magnetism, **Presence + Instinct** for a calculated emotional appeal, or **Presence + Knowledge** for legalistic logic). Each Hit scored removes 1 point of Determination from the NPC (or seals the agreement if Determination is already 0).

### 2. Probing and Observation (1 AP)

Instead of speaking, a character hangs back to scrutinize the NPC's behavior, cross-reference their credentials against station databases, or read micro-expressions. The GM calls for a Simple Test using **Instinct + Presence** (sensing lies and motives) or **Knowledge + Instinct** (analyzing corporate protocols or background files).

A success grants crucial tactical advantages:
* **Revealing Determination:** The GM openly discloses the NPC's current Determination and total remaining Patience.
* **Uncovering Levers and Motives:** The character identifies what the NPC truly desires or fears (e.g., crippling debt, fear of a superior officer, pride in their station).
* **Creating Upgrades:** Extra Hits rolled on the probing test can be spent to activate an **Upgrade Effect**, stepping up the dice of an ally's upcoming persuasion roll as they feed them the exact angle to exploit.

### 3. Clever Roleplay and Leverage: Conserving Actions

Dialogue in *Corsair* is mostly free—speaking, issuing ultimatums, or presenting evidence does not cost Action Points unless it triggers a formal mechanical test. While a GM might often rule that a compelling argument or piece of leverage simply grants an **Upgrade** to a subsequent persuasion roll, when players present truly irrefutable proof (such as surveillance footage of an escape ship fleeing, a signed confession, or a direct threat to immediate survival), the GM can make the executive call to **clear 1 or more points of Determination directly without requiring a roll at all**.

Bypassing the roll through sound reasoning or tangible leverage preserves precious **Action Points** (spending 0 AP) and completely eliminates the risk of rolling failures while the Patience clock ticks down. This reinforces that astute preparation and attentive roleplay are the sharpest weapons in a Corsair's arsenal.

## NPC Counter-Tactics

Non-player characters are active participants in a negotiation, not static progress bars. On their own turns—or reactively when challenged—an NPC can leverage three primary tactics to disrupt the players' efforts, modulate the encounter's difficulty, and protect their interests.

```
+--------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                              NPC COUNTER-TACTICS                               |
+-------------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
| 1. Contest        | Reactively spend 1 AP to roll Willpower as a Contest Roll, |
|                   | eliminating player Hits before they reduce Determination.  |
+-------------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
| 2. Insult/Threaten| Make an active Complex Test (Presence + Willpower) dealing |
|                   | Light Wounds to a PC's Mental Condition Track.             |
+-------------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
| 3. Argue/Disrupt  | Spend Hits on Action Effects to place Downgrades or        |
|                   | Blocker Effects onto the players' future talking points.   |
+-------------------+------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Contesting (Reactive)

When a player character delivers a compelling point and rolls Hits to reduce Determination, a stubborn or disciplined NPC can spend 1 Action Point to execute a **Contest Roll** using their **Willpower** attribute (or **Presence + Instinct**).

As outlined in the core contest rules, each die rolled by the NPC showing 8 or higher eliminates the highest die from the player's persuasion pool. If the NPC eliminates all of the player's Hits, the argument is rebuffed or ignored with sheer stubbornness, and no Determination is lost.

### 2. Insulting and Threatening (Active)

Hostile, arrogant, or predatory NPCs do not simply defend; they strike at the psychological fortitude of the crew. On their turn, an NPC can spend 1 Action Point to launch a targeted verbal assault, harsh mockery, or chilling threat against a specific player character.

The GM resolves this as a **Complex Test** using the NPC's **Presence + Willpower** against the target character's mental defenses:
* **The Action Roll:** The NPC rolls dice equal to their higher attribute. If at least one die lands on 8+, the verbal blow strikes home.
* **The Success Roll:** The NPC rolls dice equal to their lower attribute. The highest die represents the **Success Points** of psychological trauma dealt.
* **Inflicting Strain:** Success Points are marked as **Light Wounds** on the target PC's **Mental Condition Track I**. If the track fills, the character suffers an **Impaired** mental attribute (suffering 1 Downgrade on all subsequent tests involving that attribute), rattling their composure for the remainder of the negotiation.

### 3. Arguing and Disrupting (Active)

An NPC skilled in rhetoric, corporate doublespeak, or bureaucratic filibustering can spend their turn attempting to derail the conversation. The NPC makes a Simple Test using **Knowledge + Presence** or **Presence + Presence**.

By sacrificing Hits rolled on this test, the NPC activates Action Effects:
* **Downgrade Effects:** The NPC muddies the waters with confusing jargon or sharp counter-arguments, placing 1 **Downgrade** on the players' next persuasion test per Hit spent.
* **Blocker Effects:** The NPC lays down a hard conversational boundary (e.g., *"I will not discuss the manifest logs without my attorney present"*), establishing a Blocker Effect that forces players to eliminate set-aside Hits before they can re-introduce that specific topic.

### 4. Bargains and Counter-Offers

An NPC does not have to fight the players to a standstill. If an NPC realizes the crew has leverage or cannot easily be intimidated, they may proactively offer a **Bargain**. 

A bargain offers a compromise: the NPC agrees to immediately lower their Determination by 1 or 2 points, provided the players accept an unwanted condition, pay a bribe, or promise an auxiliary favor. Players can also propose bargains on their own turns to achieve the exact same reduction, trading credits, favors, or safe passage for immediate compliance.

## Group Social Encounters: Councils, Crowds, and Committees

Often, a Corsair Cell is not negotiating with a solitary gatekeeper, but pleading their case before a multi-member tribunal, calming an angry mob of refinery workers, or swaying a board of corporate directors. Running multiple independent Determination pools would quickly bog down the table; instead, groups operate under a unified framework.

### 1. Pooled Determination

The entire group shares a single, aggregated **Determination Pool** reflecting the collective skepticism of the body. A 3-member judicial tribunal might hold a pooled Determination of 4, while an agitated crowd of dockers could hold a Determination of 6. The group also shares a collective **Patience** clock.

### 2. Individual Exploits and Fissures

While the pool is shared, the individuals comprising the group are rarely of one mind. Players can use **Probing** actions to identify individual personalities within the collective:
* The sympathetic junior administrator who feels guilty about station policy.
* The arrogant lead magistrate whose pride can be provoked.
* The corrupt security chief who is susceptible to financial incentives.

When players direct their arguments, leverage, or roleplay toward a specific vulnerable member, a successful maneuver strips off a **disproportionate chunk of the pool** (for example, reducing Determination by 2 or 3 in a single strike) as that member breaks ranks, causes a public disagreement among the panel, or forces the rest of the committee to reconsider their stance.

## Comprehensive Example: Standoff in Cargo Bay 4

> ### Grounded Narrative Context
> Jax and Tessa have cornered a squad of hostile scrappers led by a stubborn salvage boss named **Brant** in the pressurized hold of a derelict freighter. Brant and his **five armed hirelings** have carbines leveled at the crew across a barricade of cargo crates. Both sides have weapons drawn, but a firefight in a pressurized compartment risks puncturing the hull and venting everyone into the vacuum.
>
> Jax and Tessa want Brant and his crew to drop their weapons and surrender peacefully. The GM assigns Brant a baseline Determination of 2, with each of his 5 hirelings contributing +1 Determination to their collective **Pooled Determination**, establishing a formidable **Determination 7** (requiring **8 total Hits** to break their resolve and compel a surrender). 
>
> With twitchy hirelings ready to panic, the GM sets a razor-thin **Patience of 1 Round**—the crew has a single round to break their nerve before gunfire erupts and the compartment decompresses.

### The Standoff: A Single Round at Patience 1

Both Jax and Tessa begin the round with **3 Action Points (AP)** each. The standoff unfolds through *Corsair's* dynamic turn pacing, with control alternating between the players and the GM whenever a side spends 2 or more AP.

#### Player Turn 1 (2 AP Spent)
* **Jax (1 AP):** Jax opens the standoff by assessing their adversaries rather than shouting blind threats. Spending 1 Action Point, Jax makes an **Instinct (4) + Presence (3)** Simple Test to read the scrappers' body language and defensive layout, rolling 4d10: `[3, 5, 8, 9]`. Jax scores two Hits (`8` and `9`). The GM reveals the gang's collective Determination (7) and the extreme urgency of the clock (Patience 1). Critically, Jax spots that the scrappers' heavy weapon is jammed and Brant keeps glancing toward the docking airlock—they are stalling, praying for their extraction shuttle. Jax spends his second Hit on an **Upgrade Effect** for Tessa. *(Jax: 2 AP remaining)*
* **Tessa (0 AP — Free Roleplay):** Tessa immediately exploits this opening using **Clever Roleplay**. Because talking is free, delivering this revelation requires no Action Points. She taps her data slate into the overhead monitor grid, displaying station sensor telemetry that shows the scrappers' extraction cutter already burning hard into deep space. Tessa calls across the bay: *"Your pilot panicked when the alarms tripped, Brant. Look up. Your ride just jumped out of the sector. Nobody is coming to pull you out."*
* **The GM's Call:** Showing irrefutable visual proof of abandonment strikes directly at their core survival. Rather than merely granting an Upgrade on a persuasion roll, the GM makes the call that this point is so devastating that **no roll is required at all**. The GM clears **1 point of Determination from Brant and 1 point from each of his 5 hirelings**, swatting away 6 points of Determination without spending an Action Point! The group's pooled Determination plummets from **7 down to 1**. *(Tessa: 3 AP remaining)*
* **Tessa (1 AP):** Capitalizing instantly on the chaos, Tessa spends 1 Action Point to deliver a pressing ultimatum, rolling a **Presence (4) + Willpower (3)** Simple Test with her upgraded pool (3d10 and 1d12 from Jax's Upgrade Effect). Tessa rolls: `[4, 7, 7]` on the d10s and `10` on the d12. That `10` is a Hit! This Hit strips away the **final point of Determination** (1 - 1 = 0). The scrappers' collective resolve collapses; with Determination at 0, they now need only 1 final Hit to turn their panic into an actual surrender. *(Tessa: 2 AP remaining)*

*Having spent 2 Action Points collectively (Jax 1, Tessa 1), control swaps to the GM.*

#### GM Turn 1 (1 AP Spent)
* **Brant (1 AP):** Cornered, humiliated, and desperate to keep his hirelings from dropping their guns, Brant spends 1 AP on an active Counter-Tactic: **Threaten/Intimidate** aimed at Jax. Leveling his sidearm, Brant snarls: *"Shut your mouth! We're not dying in a cage! I'll put a slug through your visor and vent this whole bay before I give up!"*
* The GM rolls a Complex Test for Brant's **Presence (3) + Willpower (3)** against Jax:
  1. **Action Roll:** Brant rolls 3d10, getting `[2, 7, 9]`. The `9` is a Hit—the threat strikes home.
  2. **Success Roll:** Brant rolls 3d10, getting `[3, 6, 7]`. The highest die is `7`.
* Jax takes **7 Light Wounds** to his **Mental Condition Track I**. With Endurance/Willpower of 3, Jax has 7 segments per track (4 + 3 = 7). Track I fills completely, inflicting the **Impaired** condition on Jax's **Presence** (imposing 1 Downgrade) for the rest of the scene.

*Control returns to the players.*

#### Player Turn 2 (2 AP Spent — Clinching the Surrender)
* **Jax (1 AP):** Shaken and Impaired by Brant's venomous threat, Jax spends 1 Action Point setting up a **Delayed Action**: bracing his carbine over the cargo crate and locking his crosshairs squarely onto Brant's chest, declaring that if hostilities erupt, he will fire *before* Brant can raise his weapon to shoot. *(Jax: 1 AP remaining)*
* **Tessa (1 AP):** Tessa has 2 AP remaining and only needs **1 final Hit** to close the encounter before Patience 1 expires at the end of the round. She steps forward to deliver the finishing blow, spending 1 AP on a **Presence (4) + Presence (4)** Simple Test: *"Drop the rifles and kick them across the deck, Brant. Do it now, and you leave here in zip-ties instead of body bags."*
* Tessa rolls 4d10: `[2, 5, 6, 8]`.
* The `8` is a Hit! Because Determination was already reduced to 0, this final Hit secures their unconditional compliance. Brant's defiance completely shatters. His jaw tightens, he unclips his sidearm, and tosses it onto the metal grating with a hollow clatter. Behind him, all five hirelings drop their rifles and raise their hands in the air. *(Tessa: 1 AP remaining)*

---

### Encounter Debrief: Pacing and Roleplay Economics

The standoff is resolved in a **single, breathless Round** before the 1-Round Patience clock expires, leaving both Jax and Tessa with 1 AP still in reserve. This scene illustrates three foundational tenets of *Corsair's* social system:

1. **Patience as a Strict Deadline:** At Patience 1, failure to break their resolve and land the final Hit within this round would have triggered immediate gunfire. The tight timer injected combat-level urgency into a dialogue scene.
2. **The Power of the GM's Call:** Dialogue is free. When Tessa presented irrefutable sensor footage, the GM made the deliberate ruling that the leverage was so absolute that no test was required, wiping 6 Determination for 0 AP rather than simply offering an Upgrade on a risky roll.
3. **Turn Pacing within a Round:** A round is not a single exchange. Control swapped between players and the GM as Action Points were spent (Player Turn 1 $\rightarrow$ GM Turn 1 $\rightarrow$ Player Turn 2), allowing an organic back-and-forth of probing, counter-threats, and closing arguments.
