# Investigation & Exploration

In *Corsair*, investigation and exploration are never reduced to passive pixel-hunting or a series of binary perception rolls that bring the story to a grinding halt on a single failed die. Whether operatives are searching the lavish, high-gravity penthouse of a corrupt shipping oligarch on Port Zenith, scouring the frost-rimmed bulkheads of a derelict frigate tumbling through Fume's rings, or picking their way through the radioactive rubble of a Precursor vault, the environment is a tactile, living puzzle designed to be prodded, interrogated, and dismantled.

Rather than waiting for the Game Master to call for rolls, players are encouraged to treat the physical space as real. They ask pointed questions, examine curious anomalies, and apply their tools with purpose. To govern this dynamic conversation without bogging down the session, *Corsair* organizes investigative scenes around three core concepts: **Keys**, **Veils**, and **Shrouds**, all bound to the ticking heartbeat of the **Action Point (AP)** economy and the **End of the Round**.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               THE ANATOMY OF AN INVESTIGATIVE SCENE                                |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  [ POINT OF INTEREST ]  --->  [ VEILS (Soft) ]  --->  [ SHROUDS (Hard) ]  --->  [ THE KEY ]        |
|  Described in the open        Concealment pierced      Mechanical barriers       The concrete clue  |
|  room overview (desk,         by Pointed Questions     cracked via Tests,        or path forward    |
|  vault, console, drapes)      (0 AP) or Tests (1 AP)   Tools, & AP (1 AP)        advancing the plot |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## The Core Triad: Keys, Veils, and Shrouds

Every investigative space—whether a compact corporate office or a sprawling subterranean facility—contains one or more focal points known as **Points of Interest (POIs)**. When the Game Master introduces a scene, they describe these POIs openly, painting an evocative sensory picture and giving the players immediate handles to grasp. 

Within these POIs lie the critical objectives of the scene, shielded by one or more layers of obstruction.

### 1. The Key
A **Key** is any discrete piece of information, physical object, biometric credential, or traversal vector that resolves a mystery, unlocks an obstacle, or propels the narrative forward. A Key might literally be an engraved brass cylinder that operates an antique safe, or it might metaphorically be an encrypted flight manifest, a telltale chemical residue on an executive's blotter, or an unmapped maintenance hatch leading to the lower reactor deck.

Keys are treated uniformly as concrete puzzle pieces. A single scene may contain an essential Key needed to track a quarry to their next hideout, as well as secondary Keys that offer tactical leverage, blackmail material, or financial windfalls. Keys are never hidden behind impenetrable dead-ends; they are always reachable if the cell is willing to spend the time, apply the right tools, or ask the right questions.

### 2. Veils (Soft Obstacles)
A **Veil** is a layer of perceptual, observational, or informational concealment. A document slipped behind a row of technical manuals, a false bottom built into a velvet-lined jewelry drawer, a spliced fiber-optic cable hidden behind decorative wall molding, or a hairline fracture in a pressurized airlock seal are all Veils. 

Veils do not require physical destruction, specialized tools, or mechanical bypassing to penetrate; they simply require someone to look in the right place or pay attention to the right detail. Veils can be parted in one of two distinct ways:

* **Pointed Inquiry (0 AP, No Test):** If a player asks a specific, inquisitive question about an established Point of Interest based on the GM's description (e.g., *"I lift the rug to see if the floorboards are scuffed,"* or *"I run my fingers along the underside of the desk to feel for a hidden switch"*), the Game Master **immediately reveals what is there**. No dice are rolled, and no Action Points are spent. This rewards observant, engaged roleplaying and keeps the conversation brisk and tactile.
* **Broad Search (1 AP, Simple Test):** If a player prefers to conduct a general, non-specific sweep of the area (e.g., *"I search the office for anything out of the ordinary"*), the operative must commit dedicated time. They spend **1 Place-to-Place Action Point** and execute a **Simple Test**, typically rolling **Instinct + Instinct** (for spatial awareness and noticing environmental oddities) or **Knowledge + Instinct** (for spotting out-of-place technical or procedural irregularities). If they score at least one Hit (`8+`), the Veil parts and the GM reveals the concealed element.

> **Failure on a Veil:** If an operative fails a Broad Search test, the Veil remains intact, and the **1 AP is consumed**. However, the scene does **not** lock up. Because time has passed, the world simply moves forward at the End of the Round. The players can immediately adjust their strategy—either by spending another AP to search again or by narrowing their focus and asking pointed, descriptive questions about specific objects in the room.

### 3. Shrouds (Hard Obstacles)
A **Shroud** is a tangible, mechanical, electronic, or structural impediment that actively bars access to a Key. A heavy steel lockbox, an encrypted datapad requiring a biometric passkey, a welded titanium blast door, or an automated firewall protecting an isolated security mainframe are all Shrouds. 

Unlike a Veil, a Shroud cannot be cleared simply by asking the right question or noticing where it is. Knowing that a heavy floor safe exists does not open it. Overcoming a Shroud demands physical intervention, technical prowess, specialized gear, and dedicated effort.

* **Action Point & Test Requirement:** Breaching a Shroud strictly requires spending **1 Place-to-Place Action Point** and executing a **Simple Test** using an Attribute Pair dictated by the character's approach and tools:
  * **Finesse + Finesse:** Picking a mechanical tumbler lock with tension wrenches or manipulating delicate internal relays.
  * **Knowledge + Finesse:** Slicing an encrypted terminal, bypassing a digital keypad, or rewriting an optical security relay using a cyberdeck.
  * **Strength + Strength:** Forcing an armored latch with a hydraulic pry bar, battering down a reinforced partition, or dragging heavy structural wreckage aside.
  * **Knowledge + Knowledge:** Deciphering an archaic cipher, analyzing complex chemical telemetry, or reverse-engineering an unfamiliar docking protocol.
* **Prerequisite Gear:** Having appropriate tools is a strict requirement for attempting tests against technical Shrouds. An operative cannot slice an optical keypad barehanded or pick a micro-tumbler lock without tension tools. Attempting a Shroud without the necessary gear is impossible, though improvising crude tools under pressure is governed by the GM's discretion.
* **Specialized Tools & Upgrades:** High-grade, professional equipment (such as an armorer's electronic bypass kit, an industrial thermal lance, or a dedicated military spectrum analyzer) provides a tangible mechanical advantage, granting **Upgrades** (stepping d10s up to d12s) on the test as specified in the item's profile.

## The Lockdown Engine: Fortified Shrouds

Standard Shrouds—such as a cheap desk lock or a civilian terminal—are bypassed with a single Hit (`8+`) on a Simple Test. However, when characters encounter high-security installations, military-grade data vaults, reinforced bulkheads, or reinforced panic rooms, the Shroud possesses **Lockdown**.

Mechanically, **Lockdown** functions as a persistent **Blocker Effect** anchored directly to the obstacle. When designing a fortified Shroud, the Game Master assigns it a Lockdown rating between **1 and 3**:

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                      LOCKDOWN RATINGS & EXAMPLES                                    |
+--------+--------------------------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------+
| Rating | Security Tier            | Narrative Example                    | Hits Needed to Clear    |
+--------+--------------------------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------+
| 1      | Commercial / Municipal   | Heavy deadbolt, encrypted datapad    | 1 Set-Aside Hit         |
| 2      | Corporate / Classified   | Multi-rotor safe, subnet firewall    | 2 Set-Aside Hits        |
| 3      | Military / Vault         | Armored blast hatch, black-site core | 3 Set-Aside Hits        |
+--------+--------------------------+--------------------------------------+-------------------------+
```

### Chipping Away at Lockdown
When an operative spends **1 AP** to execute a Simple Test against a Shroud with Lockdown, their Hits do not immediately open the mechanism. Instead, **each Hit (`8+`) scored on the roll removes 1 set-aside Hit from the Shroud's Lockdown pool**:

1. **Single-Attempt Breakthrough:** If an operative rolls Hits equal to or greater than the Shroud's Lockdown rating in a single test, all Lockdown dice are eliminated immediately. The lock pops, the firewall collapses, or the door swings open in that single 5-to-10-minute action!
2. **Incremental Progress:** If the operative scores fewer Hits than the Lockdown rating, the scored Hits still permanently reduce the Lockdown pool. For example, rolling 1 Hit against a Lockdown 2 safe removes 1 set-aside Hit, leaving the safe at Lockdown 1. The operative (or an ally) can spend another AP on a subsequent turn to roll again and clear the final Hit.
3. **Carried Momentum:** Progress made against a Shroud persists across rounds. If an operative spends 2 AP chipping away at a heavy blast door before pausing to check a security camera, the blast door remains partially dismantled; they do not lose their progress.

### Teamwork on Fortified Shrouds
Cracking high-security Shrouds under severe time pressure is an ideal moment for **Teamwork**. Multiple operatives in proximity can spend **1 AP each** to cooperate on the same Shroud during the same turn:

* Each participating operative rolls their respective Attribute Pair (for instance, one operative uses **Knowledge + Finesse** to slice the security bypass while another uses **Finesse + Finesse** to manually align the physical locking pins).
* The participants can **swap dice** between their pools to maximize the number of Hits (`8+`) produced.
* Their combined Hits are pooled together to strip away the Shroud's Lockdown rating in a single coordinated effort, allowing a synchronized cell to demolish heavy security barriers in a fraction of the time.

## Time Pressure: Failure as a Pure Time Sink

In *Corsair*, failing a test against a Shroud or during a broad search never results in arbitrary lock-jamming, broken picks that cannot be replaced, or narrative dead-ends—unless the obstacle is explicitly rigged with a tamper mechanism. Instead, failure operates as a **Pure Time Sink**.

When a player rolls zero Hits on a test to breach a Shroud:
* The obstacle remains sealed.
* The character's **1 Action Point is spent**.
* The player is free to spend another AP to try again immediately, or step aside to let a teammate try a different approach.

The true danger of failure in *Corsair* does not come from a jammed lock; it comes from the relentless ticking of the **Universal Heartbeat: The End of the Round**.

### The End-of-Round Heartbeat in Investigations
Exploration and forensic investigations take place at the **Place-to-Place** pacing tier. In this scope, a single round represents approximately **15 to 30 minutes** of real in-game time, and each character receives **3 Place-to-Place Action Points** per round (each AP representing roughly 5 to 10 minutes of concentrated effort).

When all characters in the scene have expended their AP, the Game Master executes the **End of the Round**:

1. **AP Refill:** All characters recharge their Place-to-Place AP back to 3.
2. **World Clock Advances:** 15 to 30 minutes elapse in the fiction. Patrol guards finish their break, automatic maintenance cycles initiate, or an orbital dock's scheduled departure window inches closer.
3. **Countdown Tracks Advance:** Any active Countdown Tracks (such as *"Facility Guard Shift Change,"* *"Orbital Decay,"* or *"Automated Security Sweep"*) advance by 1 box.
4. **GM Complications & Environmental Mutations:** The GM introduces new narrative wrinkles or escalating complications: a patrol drone buzzes down the corridor outside, an intercom crackles with a security check-in, or the office's smart environmental suite dims the lights for an automated cleaning cycle.

Because every failed test consumes 1 AP without producing progress, repeated failures rapidly burn through the cell's round budget, advancing countdown clocks and bringing hostile forces straight to their doorstep.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                    INVESTIGATION PACING LIFECYCLE                                  |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|  1. FREEFORM SCENE FRAMING                                                                         |
|     GM describes room & Points of Interest (POIs). Players ask Pointed Questions (0 AP, no roll).  |
|                                                                                                    |
|  2. CONCENTRATED ACTIONS (1 AP per Action)                                                         |
|     • Broad Search on a POI (Instinct + Instinct Simple Test) to part a Veil.                      |
|     • Breach a Shroud (Finesse/Knowledge/Strength Simple Test) to clear Lockdown.                 |
|     • Disarm a Countermeasure or Trap before it triggers.                                          |
|                                                                                                    |
|  3. THE END OF THE ROUND (Universal Pulse)                                                         |
|     • All characters refill to 3 Place-to-Place AP.                                                |
|     • Active Countdown Tracks tick +1 box.                                                         |
|     • GM introduces complications, patrol movements, or environmental shifts.                      |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

## Traps & Defensive Countermeasures

Not every Shroud or Veil sits passively waiting to be solved. Wealthy executives, paranoid syndicates, and military installations protect their most sensitive assets with **Traps and Countermeasures**. 

A countermeasure is an active hazard wired directly into a Veil or Shroud that triggers upon unauthorized tampering, an improper opening sequence, or a failed bypass attempt.

### Types of Countermeasures
* **Silent Network Alarms:** The most common corporate defense. Tampering with the lock without isolating the telemetry line sends an encrypted distress pulse to the station's security precinct, immediately filling 1 or 2 boxes on an active alert Countdown Track.
* **Tamper-Destruct Relays:** Common in intelligence and black-market operations. Opening the drawer without disengaging the secondary solenoid triggers a localized incendiary thermite strip or an electromagnetic pulse that destroys the datapad, vaporizing the Key unless salvaged through emergency repairs.
* **Physical & Chemical Deterrents:** Spring-loaded pneumatic micro-needles coated in neuro-toxins, high-voltage capacitor discharges across the safe handle, or localized aerosol knock-out gas canisters that flood the immediate space.
* **Environmental Lockouts:** Triggering a vault countermeasure causes reinforced blast doors to slam shut across all exits, transforming the investigation scene into an immediate containment hazard.

### Detecting & Disarming Traps
Traps follow the same fundamental logic as Veils and Shrouds:

1. **Spotting the Trap (Veil):** A trap is hidden behind a Veil. An operative can spot the trap without rolling by asking a pointed question (e.g., *"Before I pick the lock, I examine the seam around the faceplate with my inspection penlight"*). Alternatively, conducting a Broad Search or making a dedicated **Instinct + Instinct** Simple Test before tampering reveals the telltale signs: a hidden pressure wire, an extra circuit pathway, or a subtle chemical odor.
2. **Disarming the Trap (Shroud):** Once identified, neutralizing the trap is a Shroud. An operative must spend **1 AP** and make an appropriate test—typically **Finesse + Knowledge** (for electronic bypasses and cutting alarm leads) or **Finesse + Finesse** (for carefully wedging mechanical triggers). Failing the disarm test by rolling zero Hits does not automatically detonate the trap; it simply burns 1 AP as the operative carefully resets their grip. However, if the operative carelessly forces the primary lock without checking for or disarming the trap, the countermeasure detonates immediately!

## Exploration & Environmental Traversal

While the examples above focus on forensic detective work within rooms and offices, the **Keys, Veils, and Shrouds** framework scales seamlessly to macro-level exploration. Navigating a ruined research colony on a fungal moon, exploring an abandoned derelict warship drifting through deep vacuum, or traversing an active industrial mining refinery uses the exact same operational rules.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                               MACRO EXPLORATION ARCHITECTURE MAPPING                               |
+--------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Component          | Forensic / Investigative Context      | Macro / Environmental Context         |
+--------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
| Point of Interest  | Mahogany desk, terminal, wall art     | Shattered airlock, collapsed gantry   |
| Veil (Soft)        | False drawer bottom, hidden switch    | Hidden maintenance crawlway, rift     |
| Shroud (Hard)      | Tumbler lock, encrypted firewall      | Welded bulkhead, superheated plasma   |
| Lockdown           | Multi-stage safe tumblers (1–3 Hits)  | Heavy structural debris (1–3 Hits)    |
| The Key            | Incriminating ledger, biometric chit  | Safe transit route to the Reactor Core|
+--------------------+---------------------------------------+---------------------------------------+
```

### Environmental Hazards as Shrouds
In hostile exploration environments, natural or systemic hazards act as Shrouds blocking the party's path forward:
* **Welded or Jammed Blast Doors:** A heavy titanium bulkhead deformed by explosive decompression. Bypassing it is a Shroud requiring **Strength + Strength** with hydraulic jaws or **Knowledge + Finesse** with an industrial plasma cutting torch. High-grade bulkheads possess **Lockdown 2**, requiring multiple cuts to drop the plate.
* **Superheated Coolant Leaks:** A ruptured high-pressure conduit vents scalding chemical vapor across the only corridor leading to Engineering. The leak is a Shroud with **Lockdown 1**. Operatives can shut the regional valve using **Finesse + Agility** (timing the vents between bursts) or **Knowledge + Finesse** (remotely isolating the line from an auxiliary sub-station).
* **Decompressed Hull Breaches:** A micro-meteorite puncture has vented the atmosphere from a central transit corridor. Navigating the zero-g vacuum without safety tethers is a Shroud requiring **Agility + Finesse** (operating magnetic boots and thruster packs).

### Hidden Paths & Blueprints as Veils
Exploration sites are rarely single-corridor killboxes; they feature forgotten service ducts, unmapped ventilation shafts, and structural weaknesses that clever players can exploit to bypass deadly hazards:
* **Pointed Structural Inquiries:** When the GM describes a massive collapsed stairwell, a player asking, *"Do these high-voltage power conduits lead into an auxiliary maintenance tube behind the bulkhead?"* immediately parts the Veil. The GM confirms that a service crawlspace runs parallel to the collapsed stairs, granting safe passage for 0 AP and no roll.
* **Broad Area Scouting:** An operative spending **1 AP** to scan the area with a structural telemetry scanner rolls **Knowledge + Instinct**. Scoring a Hit (`8+`) reveals that the floor plates above them are mechanically fatigued, allowing the cell to breach upward into the officers' deck rather than pushing through the heavily defended main security checkpoint.

## Comprehensive Example: The Shipping Director's Office

To illustrate how Keys, Veils, Shrouds, Lockdown, Traps, and the End of the Round function together in play, consider the following scenario.

### The Setup
Two New Wave Corsairs—**Silas** (an agile infiltrator specializing in Finesse and covert bypasses) and **Tessa** (an engineer specializing in Knowledge and electronic slicing)—have infiltrated the luxury high-gravity penthouse of Director Vane, an oligarch on Port Zenith suspected of smuggling military coilgun munitions to frontier pirates. 

The cell has slipped into Vane's executive office while he attends a banquet on the lower promenade. The scene is running in **Place-to-Place pacing** (~15–30 minutes per round; 3 AP per character). The GM places a 3-box Countdown Track on the table labeled **"Dockside Security Patrol Sweep [ ][ ][ ]"**.

```
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| SCENE PROFILE: Director Vane's Executive Penthouse                                                |
| Active Pacing: Place to Place (15–30 min / round) | Each Operative: 3 AP                           |
| Active Countdown Track: Dockside Security Sweep [ ][ ][ ] (Advances +1 box at End of Round)       |
| Primary Key: The Smuggler's Manifest Ledger (Encrypted Datapad)                                    |
+----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Step 1: Scene Framing & Pointed Inquiry (0 AP)
The Game Master sets the scene:
> *"The office is opulent, smelling of real polished cedarwood and expensive imported brandy. In the center sits a massive, heavy executive desk of dark oak with a leather high-back chair. Behind it, floor-to-ceiling panoramic windows look out over the glittering orbital drydocks of Port Zenith. The left wall is lined with bookshelves holding antique nautical curios and crystal decanters, while the right wall features an ornate display cabinet holding antique ceramic masks. The room is quiet, save for the low hum of the station's atmospheric scrubber."*

* **Silas's Player asks a Pointed Question:** *"I walk straight to the executive desk. Does it have drawers, and are any of them open?"*
* **The GM responds (0 AP, No Test):** *"You step behind the desk. There are three drawers on each side. Five of them pull open smoothly—they contain high-grade stationery, expensive cigars, and personal correspondence. But the bottom-right drawer is firmly locked."*

Because Silas asked a pointed question about an established Point of Interest, the GM revealed the first **Veil** (the locked drawer) for **0 AP with no roll**.

### Step 2: Inspecting for Traps (Veil)
Knowing Vane is ruthless, Silas doesn't simply force the lock. 
* **Silas inspects the lock:** *"Before I stick a lockpick in there, I pull out my inspection penlight and magnifying visor. I look closely at the seam between the drawer and the desk frame. Is there any wiring or tamper switch?"*
* **The GM reveals the Trap (0 AP, No Test):** *"Because you took the time to check, you immediately spot a hair-thin copper wire tucked into the lower lip of the drawer. If the drawer pulls out more than two centimeters without being grounded, it will break the circuit and trigger a silent alarm to station security."*

Silas has cleanly parted the second **Veil** through smart, pointed inquiry.

### Step 3: Neutralizing the Alarm Wire (Shroud)
Silas must now neutralize the wire before cracking the lock.
* **Silas acts (1 AP):** Silas spends **1 AP** to disarm the alarm wire using his fine insulated tweezers and jumper clamps. This is a Shroud requiring a **Finesse (4) + Knowledge (2)** Simple Test.
* **The Roll:** Rolling 4d10 (his higher attribute), Silas scores `[3, 5, 8, 9]`. That's **two Hits**! The requirement was a standard Simple Test (at least one Hit). 
* **The Result:** Silas expertly clips the jumper leads across the circuit, grounding the line. The silent alarm is neutralized safely. Silas now has **2 AP remaining**.

### Step 4: Cracking the Drawer Lock (Shroud with Lockdown)
With the alarm bypassed, Silas tackles the lock itself.
* **The Obstacle:** The GM notes this is a reinforced biometric electronic deadbolt—a fortified Shroud with **Lockdown 2**.
* **Silas acts (1 AP):** Silas connects his electronic bypass pick to the deadbolt's maintenance pins. He spends **1 AP** and makes a **Finesse (4) + Finesse (4)** Simple Test. His specialized Locksmith Kit provides **1 Upgrade**, stepping one of his d10s up to a d12.
* **The Roll:** Silas rolls 3d10 and 1d12, scoring `[2, 6, 7]` on the d10s and `[10]` on the d12! That is **one Hit (`10`)**.
* **The Result:** One Hit removes 1 set-aside Hit from the Shroud's Lockdown pool. The heavy deadbolt clicks, internal gears spinning, but the secondary magnetic latch remains engaged. The Shroud is reduced to **Lockdown 1**. Silas has **1 AP remaining**.

### Step 5: Tessa Assists with Slicing (1 AP)
Hearing the secondary magnetic latch humming, Tessa steps in to assist.
* **Tessa acts (1 AP):** *"Let me splice into the auxiliary power bus from the desk terminal so we don't trip an emergency lock cycle."* Tessa spends **1 AP** to slice the desk terminal using her cyberdeck, rolling **Knowledge (5) + Finesse (3)**.
* **The Roll:** Rolling 5d10, Tessa rolls `[1, 4, 7, 8, 9]`—yielding **two Hits**!
* **The Result:** Scoring two Hits easily eliminates the final set-aside Hit from the Lockdown pool! The magnetic latch disengages with a soft pneumatic hiss, and the heavy drawer slides open. Tessa has **2 AP remaining**.

### Step 6: Finding the Hidden Compartment (Veil) & Securing the Key
Inside the drawer lies a velvet jewelry tray containing silver cufflinks and antique watches—valuable, but not what they came for.
* **Silas investigates:** *"I pull the drawer completely out and look at the depth. Does the inside measurement match the outside wooden frame?"*
* **The GM responds (0 AP, No Test):** *"It doesn't. The inside of the drawer is four centimeters shallower than the exterior casing. You press your thumb along the back velvet trim, and a spring-loaded false bottom pops upward."*
* **The Discovery:** Beneath the false bottom lies a military-grade, ruggedized datapad bearing the insignia of an Orkish arms foundry. Silas lifts it out—they have secured **The Key**!

### Step 7: The Clock Ticks (The End of the Round)
Tessa still has 2 AP remaining, while Silas has 1 AP. Tessa spends her remaining 2 AP copying the encrypted directories onto a clean data chit, while Silas spends his last AP searching the bookshelves for physical passcodes, rolling **Instinct + Instinct** and discovering an emergency backup cipher written inside the cover of an antique ship atlas.

With all AP expended, the **End of the Round** occurs:
1. **Time Advances:** 20 minutes have elapsed in the fiction.
2. **AP Refills:** Silas and Tessa refill their Place-to-Place AP pools back to 3.
3. **Countdown Ticks:** The GM marks 1 box on the security track: **"Dockside Security Patrol Sweep [X][ ][ ]"**.
4. **GM Complication:** The GM smiles: *"Outside in the corridor, you hear the heavy clatter of magnetic boots on the metal grating. A security guard's voice squawks over a shoulder comm: 'Control, this is Team 4. We’re heading up the executive lift to do the 22:00 check.' You've got the manifest, but you have roughly ten minutes before those lift doors slide open directly into this foyer."*

The scene seamlessly pivots from investigation to stealth or tactical extraction—all driven by the tangible expenditure of Action Points, intelligent player questioning, and the relentless heartbeat of the world clock.
