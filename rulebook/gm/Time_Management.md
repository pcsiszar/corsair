# Narrative Tools for Time Management

In *Corsair*, time is far more than a background element—it is a versatile narrative tool. By leveraging the structured
progression of **Rounds** and **Turns**, Game Masters can wield time to create tension, enforce consequences, and make
the world feel alive and reactive.

## Tool: Preparation Boundaries

This tool allows the GM to turn "preparation" from a vague narrative phase into a high-stakes tactical window. By
explicitly defining the boundaries of when a situation will escalate, you force players to make meaningful choices
about how they spend their most precious resource: time.

* **The Tactical Window:** Publicly stating "You have two rounds before the guard rotation arrives" creates immediate
  focus and prevents "analysis paralysis." It turns a quiet moment into a tense race against the clock where every
  Action Point spent on preparation is a gamble.
* **Variable Escalation:** The preparation boundary doesn't have to be fixed. A failed stealth check or a loud
  action might reduce the "safe" rounds from three to one, forcing players to abandon complex setups for
  immediate, messy action.
* **Installation and Fortification:** Encourage players to use their AP for "long-term" benefits during these
  rounds. Deploying automated turrets, hacking local security grids, or reinforcing cover are all narrative
  actions that provide mechanical advantages once the boundary is crossed.
* **Mechanical advantages:** Make sure that the players actually feel the impact of the prepwork. Use Upgrade/Downgrade
  effects, Triggered effects, Blocker effects, and other mechanics to make sure that they the characters are rewarded
  for their efforts.

## Tool: Objective Deadlines

Deadlines are used to force players to weigh risk against efficiency. This tool creates "do or die" moments that
heighten the drama of any encounter.

* **The Hard Count:** A visible, non-negotiable timer—like a bomb countdown or a ship entering atmospheric re-entry.
  This forces players to ignore secondary goals and personal safety to focus entirely on the primary objective
  before the clock hits zero.
* **Degrading Outcomes:** Not all failures are total. Use time to determine the *quality* of success. Rescuing a
  prisoner in two rounds might mean they are unharmed, while five rounds might mean they have already been moved
  or injured, leading to new narrative complications.
* **Micro-Deadlines (The Intercept):** Use the turn order to create immediate pressure. "You must stop the technician
  before my next turn, or the distress signal is sent." This forces a player to act now, potentially sacrificing
  their own tactical positioning to prevent a larger disaster.
* **Multi-Vector Pressure:** Running overlapping deadlines simultaneously—such as a hacking timer and an
  environmental hazard. This forces the crew to split their focus and decide which crisis is the most immediate
  threat to their survival.

## Tool: Dynamic Escalation (End-of-Round Events)

The structured nature of the Round provides a perfect "anchor point" for the GM to modulate the challenge dynamically.
Use the end of the round to introduce hurdles that respond to the players' success.

* **Reactive Reinforcements:** Instead of pre-set waves, use the end of the round to respond to the players'
  performance. If the crew is clearing enemies too efficiently, a specialized "Elite" unit might arrive at the
  start of the next round to maintain the challenge.
* **Battlefield Mutation:** Use the round's end to permanently alter the environment. Flooding rooms, venting
  atmosphere, or collapsing corridors not only creates new hurdles but also forces players to constantly rethink
  their positioning and movement.
* **Psychological Build-up:** Describe non-mechanical shifts that signal impending doom—the groaning of a hull
  under pressure, the smell of ozone, or the distant barking of hounds. These descriptions build tension and
  prepare players for more severe mechanical consequences in later rounds.
* **Atmospheric Decay:** In longer scenes, the environment itself becomes an enemy. Oxygen levels dropping,
  lights flickering out, or gravity becoming unstable are all tools to force players to wrap up their objectives
  quickly or face mounting penalties.

## Tool: Scheduled Narrative Events

To make the Sphere feel like a persistent world, use the "ticking clock" to move the plot independently of player
action.
This ensures the story doesn't wait for the players, making their interference feel more impactful.

* **The Unstoppable Plot:** Set specific rounds for major world events that happen regardless of player status.
  "At the end of Round 3, the transport ship launches." This makes the Sphere feel like a persistent world that
  doesn't wait for the protagonists to be ready.
* **Windows of Interference:** By telegraphing when an event will occur ("The shuttle launches at the end of next
  round"), you provide players with a clear, urgent goal and a defined window of opportunity to sway the outcome
  of the story.
* **Parallel Crisis Management:** Force the crew to choose between two conflicting events happening at once.
  "The reactor goes critical in 4 rounds, but the prisoner is being moved in 2." Managing these overlapping
  timers creates high-stakes drama and forces difficult moral or tactical choices.
* **Branching Success:** The round in which an objective is completed can change the narrative path. Finishing a
  data heist in Round 2 might go unnoticed, while finishing in Round 6 might mean the data was already flagged,
  leading to a pursuit in the next scene.

## Tool: Telegraphed Foreshadowing

This tool uses the GM's turn and the transitions between rounds to provide players with information that their
characters might not yet possess. By telegraphing upcoming threats, you build dread and allow players to make
"proactive" tactical decisions.

* **Sensory Cues:** Use sensory descriptions to hint at future changes. The rhythmic clanking of a heavy walker on
  the other side of a bulkhead, the smell of ozone before a power surge, or the flickering of lights as a ship's
  reactor begins to fail. These aren't just fluff; they are warnings of mechanical shifts to come.
* **The "Cutaway" Technique:** Briefly describe a scene happening elsewhere to heighten the stakes. "In the
  security hub, a guard reaches for the alarm." This gives the players meta-knowledge that creates immediate
  narrative tension, forcing them to decide how their characters—who may only hear a faint buzz—should react.
* **Mechanical Telegraphing:** Publicly track "Threat Clocks" or build a visible pool of dice for an upcoming
  environmental hazard. Seeing the "inevitable" approach through the game's mechanics is often more effective at
  building dread than a sudden surprise.
* **Actionable Intelligence:** Effective foreshadowing provides a window for interference. By telegraphing an
  impending arrival or event, you give players a round or two to reposition, set traps, or change their strategy
  before the threat manifests.


## Tool: Pressure through Action Point Scarcity

This tool uses the mechanical limitation of Action Points to create narrative tension. When time is tight, every
point counts.

* **The Action Tax:** Introduce environmental hazards or status effects that require players to spend AP just to
  ignore or overcome them. This effectively "shortens" their available time in a round, making every remaining
  point even more critical.
* **Collaborative Straining:** Create objectives that require more AP to complete in a single round than any one
  player possesses. This forces the team to pool their resources, leaving them vulnerable in other areas as they
  all focus on one task.
* **Opportunity Costs:** Present the players with multiple beneficial actions that all cost AP, but provide them
  with only enough points for a fraction of them. Forcing them to choose between "saving the data" and "securing
  the exit" creates immediate tension and long-term consequences.
* **The Attrition of Time:** In extended scenes, the passage of rounds serves as a slow drain on the crew's
  readiness. Even if AP refills, the depletion of Gambit dice, Health, and mental focus makes every passing
  round feel more dangerous than the last.
