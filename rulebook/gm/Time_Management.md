# Time Management & Pacing

In *Corsair*, time is far more than a passive background counter or a bookkeeping chore. It is an active, versatile narrative engine and the Game Master's sharpest tool for generating suspense, driving tactical decisions, and making the Sphere feel like a living, reactive environment. Whether counting down the seconds until an airlock cycles, coordinating a multi-hour precinct investigation across an orbital station, or scheduling long-haul interplanetary burns between binary moons, managing time effectively ensures that the story never stalls and that every player choice carries weight.

This guide provides Game Masters with a unified framework for governing time across all three of the game's core pacing tiers, using a tangible token economy, seamless zoom transitions, synchronized party clocks, and escalating countdown tracks.

---

## The Universal Heartbeat: The End of the Round

At every scale of play, the primary engine of time in *Corsair* is the **Round**, and its universal pulse is the **End of the Round**. While player characters spend their **Action Points (AP)** during their turns to influence their immediate surroundings, the End of the Round is when the broader world responds. 

Regardless of whether a round encompasses fifteen seconds of life-or-death firefight or a full twenty-four hours of shore leave, the End of the Round is the designated moment when the Game Master executes five essential functions:

1. **Refilling the Active Pacing's AP:** Every character present in the scene recharges their Action Points back to their maximum (3 AP) for that specific pacing tier.
2. **Advancing the World Clock:** In-game time advances by the duration of the completed round, updating astronomical positions, shift schedules, and operational windows.
3. **Advancing Countdown Tracks:** Active Countdown Tracks and ticking clocks increment, moving impending complications one step closer to fruition.
4. **Resolving Scheduled Narrative Events:** Pre-planned world actions—such as a scheduled shuttle launch, a security patrol shift, or an atmospheric scrub cycle—occur on their designated rounds regardless of player intervention.
5. **Introducing New Scene Elements:** The GM introduces reactive complications, calls in enemy reinforcements, or mutates the environment in response to the players' accomplishments or blunders.

By anchoring world progression strictly to the End of the Round, the GM maintains complete fairness. Players know that within their current round, they have a reliable window to coordinate, take risks, and execute combinations without the GM arbitrarily interrupting them mid-action.

---## The Three Pacing Tiers

To make time tangible and eliminate abstract mental math at the table, *Corsair* divides all gameplay into three distinct pacing tiers. At any given moment, the Game Master establishes which pacing tier is active, and characters spend their **Action Points (AP)** within that scope. 

> **Table Tip:** Many Game Masters and players find it helpful to track Action Points using physical tokens—such as poker chips, counters, or dice. Using three different colors of tokens (one for each pacing tier) makes it instantly obvious at a glance which tier is active and how many actions remain in that scope.

```
+-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                                           THE PACING TIERS MATRIX                                           |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Pacing Tier       | Round Duration     | What 1 AP Buys         | When the Round Ends                       |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Moment to Moment  | ~10–15 Seconds     | ~3–5 Seconds of high-  | ~15 seconds pass.                         |
|                   |                    | octane tactical action | Active Countdown Tracks advance.          |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Place to Place    | ~15–30 Minutes      | ~5–10 Minutes of        | ~15–30 minutes pass.                       |
|                   | (Minutes)           | focused exploration     | Patrol sweeps check in, sensors cycle.     |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
| Day to Day        | 1 Full Day         | ~8-Hour block (Morning,| 24 hours pass.                            |
|                   | (24 Hours)         | Afternoon, or Night)   | 1 Supply consumed, daily deadlines tick.  |
+-------------------+--------------------+------------------------+-------------------------------------------+
```

### 1. Moment to Moment
This is the most zoomed-in level of play, where every fraction of a second matters. It is reserved for high-stakes combat, close-quarters stealth infiltrations, emergency vehicular evasions, and tense, face-to-face negotiations. 

A single round lasts roughly **10 to 15 seconds**. Each **Moment-to-Moment Action Point** represents roughly **3 to 5 seconds** of intense effort—enough time to line up and fire a kinetic firearm, sprint four meters between bulkheads, dive into low cover, hack an electronic door bypass, or ready a defensive reaction.

### 2. Place to Place
This mid-level scope governs structured area exploration, municipal investigations, district-wide legwork, and facility infiltrations. Time is more fluid, focusing on an entire installation, spaceport terminal, or city district.

A single round represents roughly **15 to 30 minutes**. Each **Place-to-Place Action Point** represents roughly **5 to 10 minutes** of concentrated activity—such as picking an electronic security lock, searching an office for physical manifests, traversing an industrial catwalk, or slicing a local terminal directory.

### 3. Day to Day
This is the macro scope of the game, used when characters are engaged in long-term endeavors, interplanetary transit under constant fusion burn, or resting between contracts.

A single round represents **one full 24-hour day**. Each **Day-to-Day Action Point** represents an **8-hour block** of the day—typically corresponding to the Morning Shift, Afternoon Shift, and Night Shift (which includes required rest and downtime). Spending 1 Day-to-Day AP allows a character to undertake major personal projects, such as fabricating custom equipment, performing deep structural repairs on their starship, training skills to gain Experience Points, or meeting with faction sponsors. At the End of the Round, 24 hours elapse and the ship deducts 1 Supply from its cargo hold.

## Mastering Pacing Transitions & Time Synchronization

A dynamic session of *Corsair* will frequently move between pacing tiers as the narrative tension shifts. The crew might begin their day planning a contract (Day to Day), zoom into an afternoon reconnaissance shift to infiltrate a warehouse district (Place to Place), and suddenly find themselves in a firefight when an alarm trips (Moment to Moment). 

Transitioning between scopes is governed by two universal table rules: **Zooming In via Lowest AP Synchronization**, and **Zooming Out via Natural Continuity**.

```
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
|                       GENERALIZED PACING TRANSITIONS                            |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
| Zooming In (Stepping Down):                                                     |
| 1. All characters present in the immediate scene synchronize their broader      |
|    Action Points down to the lowest remaining AP among them.                    |
| 2. Characters refill their Action Points in the new, narrower pacing to their   |
|    maximum (3 AP) and begin a fresh round.                                      |
|                                                                                 |
| Zooming Out (Stepping Up):                                                      |
| 1. Characters set aside their narrower Action Points.                           |
| 2. Characters resume the broader pacing with whatever broader Action Points     |
|    they had remaining.                                                          |
| 3. If characters still have broader AP remaining, they continue their broader   |
|    round; when all broader AP are spent, that broader round concludes at the    |
|    End of the Round, advancing world clocks and refilling AP.                   |
+---------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
```

### Zooming In: The Lowest AP Synchronization Rule

In a shared physical location, time flows equally for everyone present. If one character spends an operational Action Point picking a complex magnetic lock while two companions stand watch, that time has passed for all three characters, regardless of whether the companions made a formal roll.

Therefore, whenever a situation escalates and the Game Master calls for **Zooming In** (whether from Day to Day into Place to Place, or from Place to Place into Moment to Moment), all characters present in that immediate scene must synchronize their broader Action Points down to the **lowest remaining AP among those present**. 

For example, if Jax spent 1 Place-to-Place AP slicing a security console while Silas and Tessa stood watch with 3 AP remaining, security suddenly breaching the room drops Silas and Tessa to **2 Place-to-Place AP** to match Jax. Everyone present has experienced those same ten minutes of real time. All present characters then receive their full **3 Moment-to-Moment AP** and roll initiative for Round 1 of the tactical confrontation.

### Zooming Out: Natural Continuity

When a focused scene concludes, the immediate crisis or operational task is complete. Transitioning back out to the broader pacing tier is completely frictionless:

1. **Discard Narrower AP:** Narrower Action Points are set aside.
2. **Resume Remaining Broader AP:** Characters resume the broader pacing with whatever broader Action Points they already had remaining. No extra broader AP are deducted—the operational action that led into or accompanied the focused scene has already accounted for the time spent.
3. **Reaching the Broader End of the Round:** If characters still have broader AP remaining in that round, they proceed with their next broader actions. If all broader AP have been spent, the broader round naturally concludes at the **End of the Round**, advancing the world clock by that tier's duration and refilling that broader tier back to 3 AP.

This applies symmetrically at every boundary:
* **From Moment to Moment back to Place to Place:** When a gunfight ends, characters set aside their tactical AP and resume their exploration with their remaining Place-to-Place AP.
* **From Place to Place back to Day to Day:** When an exploration scene or facility infiltration concludes, characters return to Day to Day, having completed that portion of their day, and proceed to their next Day-to-Day block (e.g., returning to the ship for the evening).

### Parallel Action & Split Parties

When the crew intentionally splits up across different locations—such as Jax and Tessa infiltrating a corporate warehouse while Silas monitors communications from their starship—characters outside the focused scene are **not** reduced to the lowest AP.

Instead, the Game Master runs the two groups in parallel:
1. Play out the focused scene with the active team in their narrower pacing.
2. When that scene concludes or reaches a natural pause, cut to the off-site character.
3. Grant the off-site character their turn to declare what they accomplished with their equivalent broader AP during that same timeframe.

By alternating between groups at the boundary of broader Action Points, the GM keeps every player engaged, prevents temporal desynchronization, and ensures that the entire crew reaches the End of the Round together.

## The GM's Time Management Toolkit

With the core pacing framework established, Game Masters can deploy several specialized operational tools to transform time into a dynamic, dramatic antagonist.

### Tool 1: Countdown Tracks & Ticking Clocks

In high-stakes scenes, the GM should rely on **Countdown Tracks** to make the passage of time visible and perilous. Unlike long-term campaign projects, immediate encounter countdowns should remain tight, urgent, and focused. As a standard rule of thumb, encounter countdowns made up on the fly should be calibrated to **1, 2, or at most 3 rounds**:

* **1 Round (Immediate Crisis / Flash Window):** An emergency that demands instant reaction before the window slams shut. Examples include diving through an emergency blast door before it locks, hacking an override terminal before security purge gas releases, or a 1-round preparation boundary before a target vehicle speeds past.
* **2 Rounds (The Tactical Standard):** The ideal baseline for high-tension tactical encounters. Two rounds provide enough breathing room for players to coordinate actions, set up Teamwork combos, or spend Action Effects, while keeping the pressure suffocatingly high. This is the standard duration for NPC **Patience** in social negotiations and standard response times for station security patrols.
* **3 Rounds (Extended Crisis):** Reserved for multi-stage disasters requiring divided focus, such as stabilizing a venting fusion core while fending off boarders, or racing to extract critical data before a facility initiates self-destruct.
* **Tracks of 8+ Segments:** Sizing tracks to 8, 10, or 12 segments is reserved strictly for macro campaign-level arcs (such as an orbital decay timer over multiple sessions or a syndicate manhunt across the Fume cluster), never for immediate encounter clocks.

#### Modulating Clocks Dynamically
While a Countdown Track automatically advances by 1 segment at the End of the Round, player choices should directly accelerate or delay the countdown:
* **Burning Time:** Reckless, loud, or blundering actions (such as firing an unsilenced kinetic rifle in an unsuppressed corridor or failing a stealth test near an acoustic sensor) immediately burn an extra segment on the countdown clock.
* **Buying Time:** Clever tactical maneuvers (such as deploying an electromagnetic jammer, sealing a secondary pressure bulkhead, or slicing a transit schedule) can erase a marked segment, granting the crew a vital extra round to accomplish their goals.
* **Milestone Triggers:** For 2- and 3-round tracks, the GM can anchor escalating complications to specific segments. For example, on segment 1 of a security track, local klaxons blare; on segment 2, automated corridor bulkheads drop; on segment 3, a heavily armored response team breaches the room.

### Tool 2: Preparation Boundaries & Fortification Windows

Game Masters can transform routine preparation into a high-stakes tactical minigame by establishing an explicit **Preparation Boundary**. Rather than allowing players to engage in endless hypothetical planning, the GM states clearly: *"You have exactly 1 round before the security convoy enters the courtyard. What do you do?"*

During a preparation round, players spend their 3 Action Points each to shape the upcoming battlefield using *Corsair's* mechanical engines:
* **Fortifying Cover:** Spending AP to physically haul heavy shipping containers or tip over industrial consoles, establishing **Superior Cover** (+2 Armor against ranged attacks) where none previously existed.
* **Deploying Blocker Effects:** Spending AP to weld side doors shut, lay magnetic spike strips, or rig acoustic jammers that impose **Blocker Effects** on enemy movement or communications.
* **Setting Triggered Effects:** Planting proximity charges, thermal tripwires, or automated defense sentries that carry **Triggered Effects**, detonating automatically when an enemy crosses a designated threshold.
* **Generating Upgrades:** Slicing local terminal cameras or analyzing sensor feeds to grant allies **Upgrade Effects** on their opening attack rolls once the encounter begins.
* **Setting Delayed Actions:** Spending 1 AP to prepare a **Delayed Action**, establishing a synchronized crossfire overwatch that interrupts enemies the instant they enter the killzone.

### Tool 3: Handling Failed Tests: Activating Actors

A common dilemma for Game Masters during time-sensitive preparation or stealth scenes is how to adjudicate failed dice rolls. If a player fails a test to slice a terminal or rig a trap, does the entire plan instantly collapse?

In *Corsair*, the primary and immediate penalty of any failed test is **the loss of the spent Action Point**. In a scene governed by a tight ticking clock, wasting 1 of your precious 3 Action Points is already a severe tactical setback.

When a failure carries an unavoidable secondary narrative complication—such as tripping an optical sensor wire, causing a loud mechanical clatter, or being spotted by a sentry drone—the GM should **not** interrupt the player's turn with instant, out-of-turn punishments. Instead, that failure **activates an actor for the GM's upcoming turn**. 

The GM announces: *"Your pry-bar slips with a loud metallic screech. The sentry drone at the end of the hall rotates toward your position."* That sentry is now an active participant. When the turn passes to the GM, the GM spends the drone's Action Points to sound an alarm, illuminate the characters with a spotlight, or open fire. This adheres strictly to the turn structure, gives the player characters an immediate opportunity to coordinate a response before the GM acts, and maintains total tactical fairness.

### Tool 4: Dynamic Escalation & End-of-Round Events

The End of the Round is the GM's designated stage to keep encounters fluid and prevent static standoffs. If player characters dig into impenetrable cover and refuse to move, use the End of the Round to alter the environment dynamically:
* **Battlefield Mutation:** Bulkheads buckle, toxic coolant vents across low ground, cargo gantries collapse, or zero-gravity stabilization thrusters fail, forcing characters to relocate or spend AP securing magnetic boot clamps.
* **Reactive Reinforcements:** Rather than placing all adversaries on the map at the beginning of an encounter, stagger their arrival across rounds. When the first wave falters, the GM announces at the End of the Round that an armored combat skiff has docked on the exterior landing pad.
* **Atmospheric Decay:** In damaged space vessels or vacuum environments, the GM can track environmental degradation. Each round that passes without repairing life support reduces ambient oxygen or increases ambient radiation, threatening cumulative wounds to characters without sealed environmental suits.

### Tool 5: Scheduled World Events & Telegraphed Foreshadowing

To make the Sphere feel persistent and indifferent to the protagonists, major world events should move along the timeline regardless of whether the cell is ready. If a corporate transport shuttle is scheduled to launch at the End of Round 3, it launches at the End of Round 3. If the crew reaches the landing pad in Round 2, they board cleanly; if they arrive in Round 4, they are left standing on a scorched pad staring at engine plumes.

To make scheduled events and lethal threats feel fair rather than punitive, the GM should always use **Telegraphed Foreshadowing** one round in advance:
* **Sensory Cues:** Describe sensory indicators that warn of impending shifts—the deep shudder of an orbital frame before a pressure collapse, the rising thermal hum of an enemy railgun charging its capacitors, or the rhythmic clanking of magnetic combat boots echoing through ventilation ducts.
* **Public Clocks:** Place physical Countdown dice or track sheets openly on the table. Watching a die count down from `[3]` to `[2]` to `[1]` at the End of each Round builds unbearable suspense and empowers players to make proactive tactical sacrifices before the hammer falls.

## Practical Examples in Action

The following narrative examples demonstrate how the GM wields time, action points, and pacing transitions across all three tiers of play.

---

### Example 1: The Badlands Ambush (Place to Place into Moment to Moment)

> Jax, Silas, and Tessa have tracked an illicit syndicate arms convoy transporting stolen military-grade munitions across the arid moon of Suncatcher. Navigating the windswept badlands in their rugged all-terrain rover, the crew arrives at a jagged sandstone canyon roughly twenty minutes ahead of the convoy’s projected arrival time. 
>
> The Game Master establishes the scene in **Place-to-Place pacing** for **1 Round (~20 minutes)**. Each character receives **3 Place-to-Place Action Points (AP)** to prepare the ambush zone before the convoy arrives.
>
> ```
> +-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> |                              CANYON AMBUSH PREPARATION (PtP)                            |
> +-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Character | AP Cost| Operational Preparation Action                                     |
> +-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Silas     | 1 AP   | Instinct + Instinct test to find ideal choke point (Upgrade Effect)|
> |           | 1 AP   | Instinct + Instinct test to spot Heavy Cover (+3 Protection)       |
> |           | 1 AP   | Readies Delayed Action: Sniper shot when mine detonates            |
> +-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Conceals the rover in a side fissure with heat-sink baffles        |
> |           | 1 AP   | Plants and arms the Landmine as a Delayed Action                   |
> |           | 1 AP   | Forfeited (holds in cover awaiting target arrival)                 |
> +-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Jax       | 1 AP   | Agility + Instinct test to dig forward melee blind (Upgraded pool) |
> |           | 1 AP   | Readies Delayed Action: Melee charge when lead vehicle stalls      |
> |           | 1 AP   | Forfeited (holds in blind awaiting target arrival)                 |
> +-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------+
> ```
>
> #### Round 1: Place-to-Place Preparation
>
> 1. **Silas (3 AP):**
>    * Silas begins by surveying the canyon floor through his electro-binoculars. He spends **1 AP** making an **Instinct (4) + Instinct (4)** Simple Test to identify where the convoy is bound to pass. Rolling 4d10, Silas scores `[3, 6, 8, 9]`—yielding two Hits (`8` and `9`). The first Hit locates a natural funnel where ancient flash-flood runoff narrowed the canyon road between sheer cliffs. Silas spends his second Hit on an **Upgrade Effect**, analyzing dust drifts and lighting angles to grant an advantage to further preparation work.
>    * Next, Silas scrambles up the western rim to secure a firing position. He spends **1 AP** making an **Instinct (4) + Instinct (4)** Simple Test to find optimal cover. Scoring a Hit, Silas discovers a weathered granite shelf that grants **Heavy Cover (+3 Protection)** with an unobstructed line of sight down into the killzone.
>    * With his final **1 AP**, Silas stabilizes his designated marksman rifle across the granite ridge and declares a **Delayed Action**: *"The instant the landmine detonates beneath the lead transport, I will fire an armor-piercing shot directly through the driver's cabin."*
>
> 2. **Tessa (3 AP):**
>    * While Silas scouts the canyon from above, Tessa takes the wheel of the rover. She spends **1 AP** reversing the vehicle into a deep crevice behind a sandstone bluff, cutting the main battery and deploying thermal-dissipation covers so the rover's engine heat will not register on approaching sensors.
>    * Tessa then hauls the military-grade Landmine the crew purchased for the job down to the exact bottleneck Silas flagged. Spending **1 AP**, Tessa digs a shallow trench across the road, primes the seismic pressure detonator, and conceals the charge beneath red dust. She sets the Landmine as a **Delayed Action**: *"Detonates when a vehicle of two or more tons passes directly over the plate."*
>    * With her prep work finished, Tessa slips behind a low boulder shelf (+1 Light Cover) with her sidearm ready. She decides not to take any additional actions, holding her remaining **1 AP** in reserve.
>
> 3. **Jax (3 AP):**
>    * Jax moves forward into the canyon gorge to position himself for close-quarters engagement. He spends **1 AP** setting up a concealed ambush blind among a tumble of boulders just meters from where the lead vehicle will stall. The GM calls for an **Agility (4) + Instinct (3)** Simple Test. Applying Silas’s **Upgrade Effect**, Jax rolls 3d10 and 1d12: `[4, 6, 7]` on the d10s and a `10` on the upgraded d12! Jax scores a clean Hit, sliding into the rock crevices without disturbing the scree or leaving visible tracks.
>    * Jax unslings his heavy Boarding Axe and spends **1 AP** to set a **Delayed Action**: *"The instant the mine detonates and Silas's rifle cracks, I will charge out of the dust to engage the lead vehicle’s escort guards in melee before they can deploy."*
>    * Like Tessa, Jax's preparations are complete. He holds in place, retaining **1 AP**.
>
> #### The Waiting Window & Forfeited AP
> Both Jax and Tessa have 1 Place-to-Place AP remaining. However, an ambush requires stillness. Because the crew has completed their preparation and is actively waiting for an external event to arrive on schedule, they cannot bank or roll over their unused Action Points. Time moves inexorably forward: the remaining minutes elapse, concluding the 20-minute Place-to-Place round. At the **End of the Round**, unspent Action Points are forfeited as the world clock strikes the convoy's arrival time.
>
> #### The Ambush Springs: Resolving the Opening Salvo
> At the End of the Round, a low mechanical rumble echoes between the canyon walls as two dust-caked six-wheeled transports rumble around the bend, escorted by syndicate outriders.
>
> 1. The lead transport rolls into the narrow funnel, passing directly over the buried Landmine. The Landmine’s **Delayed Action** triggers automatically, costing **0 additional AP**. The blast rips through the vehicle’s front undercarriage with a concussive shockwave, tearing away its steering axle and slamming the transport sideways into the rock wall, completely blocking the canyon pass.
> 2. The explosion instantly satisfies the trigger condition for Silas’s readied sniper shot. Silas executes his **Delayed Action** for **0 additional AP**, putting a high-velocity round through the cracked canopy to neutralize the vehicle's turret gunner.
> 3. Hearing the blast and sniper crack, Jax triggers his readied **Delayed Action** for **0 additional AP**, bursting from his boulder blind through the cloud of smoke to crash into the dismounted escort guards with his boarding axe before they can recover their footing.
>
> #### Zooming In to Moment to Moment
> With the opening salvo resolved and the convoy trapped in the chokepoint, the element of surprise gives way to an active firefight as surviving syndicate mercenaries bail out of the rear transport with rifles raised.
>
> The GM announces that the scene has **Zoomed In to Moment-to-Moment pacing**. 
>
> Having stepped down into tactical scale, all characters refill their pools to **3 Moment-to-Moment AP** for Round 1 of combat. Jax begins combat already in Close Quarters Combat (CQC) with the escort guards, Silas dominates the high ground from +3 Heavy Cover, and Tessa is positioned to provide supporting fire or suppressive fire, fully demonstrating how careful operational preparation in Place to Place seamlessly cascades into tactical dominance.

### Example 2: The Stolen Scientist (Place-to-Place Investigation with Countdown Track)

> In the neon-drenched, rain-swept aerostat metropolis of Cloudspire on Nimbus, Dr. Yune—a senior metallurgist at the Sphere Research Center (SRC) developing bleeding-edge recoil-compensation hardware for firearms—has vanished without a trace. The SRC contacts Jax, Silas, and Tessa with an urgent plea: local corporate competitors have deployed a shadow team to abduct and interrogate Dr. Yune, extract her encryption keys, and smuggle the schematics off-world before authorities intervene. Time is strictly of the essence—the sooner the crew tracks down and extracts Dr. Yune alive, the larger their mission bonus payout.
>
> The GM sets the scene in **Place-to-Place pacing**, where each round represents roughly 20 to 30 minutes of operational movement across Cloudspire's interconnected aerostat sectors. To reflect the urgency of the interrogation, the GM places a public **3-Round Countdown Track** on the table labeled *"Corporate Interrogation Complete."* If the track reaches `[0]` at the End of Round 3, the shadow team extracts the tech, administers a neuro-wipe, and escapes into the cloud deck.
>
> In Place-to-Place pacing, the three operatives have a combined pool of **9 Action Points (3 AP each)** for the round:
>
> ```
> +-----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> |                               THE CLOUDSPIRE TRAIL (9 AP)                               |
> +-------+-----------+--------+------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Round | Character | AP Cost| Operational Action                                         |
> +-------+-----------+--------+------------------------------------------------------------+
> | R1    | Jax       | 1 AP   | Bypasses electronic lock to enter Dr. Yune's apartment     |
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Instinct + Knowledge forensic test on personal terminal    |
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Piloting (Transports whole cell to "The Neon Drift" lounge)|
> |       | Jax       | 1 AP   | Canvasses colleague at the lounge to uncover terrace lead  |
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Knowledge + Hacking via Cyberdeck on cams (Upgrade Effect) |
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Piloting (Transports whole cell to the Control Tower)      |
> |       | Jax       | 1 AP   | Presence + Presence persuasion on Control Tower security   |
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Knowledge + Interfacing via Cyberdeck to trace VTOL logs   |
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Piloting (Transports whole cell to Foundry Platform 9)     |
> +-------+-----------+--------+------------------------------------------------------------+
> ```
>
> #### Round 1: Tracking the Shadow Team (9 AP Spent)
>
> Because Dr. Yune was abducted elsewhere, her high-rise apartment in the Upper Promenade is completely undisturbed. The crew's immediate task is to get inside and discover where she went last night:
> * **Entering the Apartment (Jax, 1 AP):** The front entrance is sealed by an electronic biometric lock. Jax spends **1 AP** using a bypass kit to pop the electronic deadbolt, letting the crew slip inside without alerting building security.
> * **Terminal Forensics (Silas, 1 AP):** Inside the quiet apartment, Silas spends **1 AP** conducting an **Instinct (4) + Knowledge (3)** Simple Test on her private workstation. He rolls a Hit (`8+`), recovering her personal itinerary and receipt records pointing to a midnight reservation at *"The Neon Drift"*, an upscale skybridge lounge hanging in the Lower Aerostat district.
> * **Vehicle Transit to the Lounge (Tessa, 1 AP):** Cloudspire's suspended platforms are separated by turbulent, multi-kilometer cloud channels. The GM highlights a vital vehicular rule: **by using a dedicated vehicle, a single driver can spend 1 Action Point to transport the entire cell**. Tessa takes the controls of their sleek VTOL rotor-craft, spending **1 AP** to pilot the crew across the cloud rift. Jax and Silas pay **0 AP** for the transit, arriving fresh.
> * **Canvassing the Lounge (Jax, 1 AP):** Inside the crowded lounge, Jax spends **1 AP** speaking with Dr. Yune's lunch companion, an anxious lab colleague. Jax uncovers that Dr. Yune was terrified of corporate surveillance, stepped out onto the rain-lashed skybridge terrace at midnight to take an urgent encrypted call, and never returned.
> * **Hacking the Terrace Cameras (Silas, 1 AP):** Silas connects his **"Mantis" Cyberdeck** directly into the promenade's optical sensor relay. Pairing his **Knowledge 4** with the deck's **Hacking 4**, Silas makes a **Knowledge + Hacking** Simple Test, rolling 4d10: `[3, 6, 8, 9]`. Scoring two Hits (`8` and `9`), Silas extracts footage of two masked operatives forcing Dr. Yune aboard an unmarked black stealth VTOL craft at 23:42. Silas spends his second Hit on an **Upgrade Effect**, storing the exact timestamp and flight vector to boost their upcoming sensor query.
> * **Transit to Control Tower (Tessa, 1 AP):** Tessa spends **1 AP** piloting their VTOL rotor-craft across the district to the local Air Traffic Control Tower.
> * **Persuading Control Tower Security (Jax, 1 AP):** Entering the district Air Traffic Control Tower, the crew encounters security guarding the flight operations desk. Jax steps forward, spending **1 AP** to make a **Presence (4) + Presence (4)** Simple Test, citing their emergency Corsair warrant and Flotilla search protocols. Scoring a Hit (`8+`), Jax persuades the security supervisor to bypass standard bureaucracy and unlock yesterday’s radar telemetry logs immediately.
> * **Interfacing with the Flight Logs (Silas, 1 AP):** Silas plugs his **"Mantis" Cyberdeck** directly into the air traffic terminal. Pairing his **Knowledge 4** with the deck’s **Interfacing 5**, he makes a **Knowledge + Interfacing** Simple Test to comb through the radar logs. Applying his banked **Upgrade Effect** from the terrace camera hack (rolling 4d10 and 1d12), Silas scores multiple Hits. He matches the exact 23:42 departure timestamp, isolates the stealth VTOL’s radar track as it slipped through low-altitude cloud channels, and pinpoints its landing site: **Foundry Platform 9**, a decommissioned aerostat casting facility suspended in the lower keel.
> * **Transit to the Keel (Tessa, 1 AP):** Tessa takes the controls, spending **1 AP** to pilot their VTOL rotor-craft down through thick vapor banks to the industrial keel, landing silently on a rusted exterior catwalk outside Foundry Platform 9.
>
> #### End of the Round & The Countdown Clock
> Roughly 25 minutes elapse across Cloudspire as the crew executes the entire operation with ruthless efficiency. All 9 Place-to-Place AP have been spent, concluding Round 1 at the **End of the Round**.
>
> The GM advances the world clock and ticks the Countdown Track down from `[3]` to `[2]`. Because the crew solved the trail in a single round without wasting operational actions, they have arrived well ahead of schedule while Dr. Yune is still alive and the interrogation is incomplete.
>
> #### Preparing for the Final Showdown
> Having reached Foundry Platform 9 with time to spare on the clock, the GM notes that the team faces an operational choice:
> * **Operational Recon & Preparation:** While they can spend Place-to-Place Action Points to scout the foundry, probe perimeter security, or splice surveillance feeds before stepping down to tactical scale, doing so burns operational time.
> * **Immediate Infiltration:** Alternatively, they can choose to infiltrate immediately. Since they tracked down the hideout so rapidly and are still in Round 1's operational window, an immediate breach allows them to catch the abductors off-guard and secure the highest possible bonus pay from the SRC.
>
> Opting for maximum speed and the top-tier payout, the operatives decide to breach without delay.
>
> #### Zooming In to Moment to Moment
> The GM calls for **Zooming In to Moment-to-Moment pacing**:
> * All characters synchronize their broader AP down to 0 and refill to **3 Moment-to-Moment AP** for Round 1 of combat.
> * The cell initiates their tactical breach on the holding facility with maximum operational advantage, having preserved Dr. Yune's life and secured the highest bonus pay through disciplined Place-to-Place time management.

### Example 3: Shore Leave, Repairs & Station Gigs (Day-to-Day Pacing)

> Following their high-stakes extraction on Cloudspire, Jax, Silas, and Tessa dock their starship at **Port Zenith**, a bustling commercial orbital station suspended in the upper atmosphere of Landfall. With their mission bounty banked, the crew schedules a **24-hour layover** to heal injuries, repair orbital micro-fractures on the ship's hull, restock supplies, procure new tactical hardware, and pick up local freelance work.
>
> The Game Master establishes the scene in **Day-to-Day pacing**, where a single round represents **one full 24-hour day**. Each character receives **3 Day-to-Day Action Points (AP)** corresponding to an **8-hour shift**:
> * **Shift 1 (Morning / 08:00–16:00):** 1 AP
> * **Shift 2 (Afternoon / 16:00–24:00):** 1 AP
> * **Shift 3 (Night / 00:00–08:00):** 1 AP
>
> In Day-to-Day pacing, the three operatives manage a combined pool of **9 Action Points (3 AP each)** across the 24-hour cycle:
>
> ```
> +-------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> |                                      PORT ZENITH LAYOVER (9 AP)                                       |
> +-------+-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | Shift | Character | AP Cost| Operational Downtime Action                                              |
> +-------+-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | S1    | Jax       | 1 AP   | Medbay Long Rest (Endurance + Willpower, clears Aggravated & Light Wounds)|
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Hull Repair via Mechanic's Kit (Knowledge + Tinkering Simple Test)       |
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Commercial Bazaar Shopping Spree (Supplies, Upgrade Kit, Shield, Grapple)|
> +-------+-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | S2    | Jax       | 1 AP   | Physical Conditioning & Gym Workout (Trains for 1 XP)                    |
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Freelance Search: Finding a station manager's lost pet (Tiered Pay)      |
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Freelance Bodyguard: Escorting mining rep through promenade (Tiered Pay) |
> +-------+-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> | S3    | Jax       | 1 AP   | Freelance Security: Catching pickpockets at night market (Tiered Pay)     |
> |       | Silas     | 1 AP   | Zero-AP Upgrade Install (Lethal Tuning) + Systems Study (Trains for 1 XP)|
> |       | Tessa     | 1 AP   | Flight Simulator Drills (Trains for 1 XP)                                |
> +-------+-----------+--------+--------------------------------------------------------------------------+
> ```
>
> #### Shift 1: Morning Operations (3 AP Spent)
>
> During the morning 8-hour block, the crew divides their efforts to address urgent medical and logistical priorities:
> * **Medbay Long Rest (Jax, 1 AP):** Jax took heavy kinetic fire during the foundry breach, completely filling Track I of his Physical Condition with 6 Light Wounds and 1 Aggravated Wound, which left his **Strength** Attribute **Impaired** (suffering 1 Downgrade on all Strength tests). Jax spends his morning shift undergoing intensive treatment in the station’s automated medical suite. A Long Rest requires an **Endurance + Willpower** Success Test. Jax has **Endurance 3** and **Willpower 2**; because tests pair attributes using the lower rating, his base pool is 2 dice. The medical facility provides **2 Upgrades**, elevating his pool to 2d12. Rolling `[9, 11]`, Jax generates **20 Success Points**! 
>   * He spends **2 Success Points** to clear the 1 Aggravated Wound (costing 2 SP per segment).
>   * He spends **6 Success Points** to heal all 6 Light Wounds (costing 1 SP per segment).
>   * With Track I completely cleared, Jax immediately removes the **Impaired** condition from Strength, restoring his physical capabilities to full potency.
> * **Ship Hull Repairs (Silas, 1 AP):** Micrometeorites and point-defense shrapnel pitted the starship’s exterior armor plating during orbital transit. Rather than paying exorbitant station dockyard labor fees, Silas rolls up his sleeves. He equips his **Mechanic's Kit** (**Tinkering 2**, **Locksmith 2**) and pairs the kit’s Tinkering attribute with his **Knowledge 4**. Spending **1 AP**, Silas makes a **Knowledge + Tinkering** Simple Test, rolling 4d10: `[4, 7, 8, 9]`—scoring two Hits (`8` and `9`). Silas seals the outer pressure seams and heat-treats the composite ablative plating, cleanly restoring the ship’s hull integrity to full operational readiness.
> * **Station Bazaar Shopping Spree (Tessa, 1 AP):** Tessa spends her 8-hour morning shift navigating Port Zenith’s multi-level commercial concourse. The GM highlights a fundamental economy rule: **spending 1 Day-to-Day AP on a shopping trip allows an operative to procure any standard equipment, supplies, and gear available in that market tier**. Drawing from their banked bounty earnings, Tessa buys:
>   * **Standard Crate of Supplies (5 Supply, 8,000 Credits):** Loaded directly into the starship's cargo hold to replenish life support reserves and reactor coolant.
>   * **Tier 1 Weapon Upgrade Kit (10,000 Credits):** Precision machining parts and gas regulators for weapon customization.
>   * **Standard Grapple Launcher (5,000 Credits):** A gas-tether winch rig (Mobility 3, Capacity 3, Load 2) for rapid vertical maneuvering.
>   * **Riot Shield (2,500 Credits):** A reinforced polycarbonate shield (+2 Protection, Min STR 4, 1H, Load 2) for Jax to pair with his boarding axe.
>   * *Total Capital Spent:* **25,500 Credits**.
>
> #### Shift 2: Afternoon Gigs & Training (3 AP Spent)
>
> With critical recovery and requisitions resolved, the operatives spend their afternoon on personal growth and short freelance contracts. The GM establishes the station's standard **Freelance Payout Formula**: completing an 8-hour gig awards **500 Credits base pay** upon scoring at least 1 Hit on a relevant test, plus **+250 Credits for each additional Hit scored**.
> * **Physical Conditioning (Jax, 1 AP):** Fully recovered from his injuries, Jax spends his afternoon shift in the station’s high-gravity centrifuge gym, running load-bearing conditioning drills. Spending 1 Day-to-Day AP on training grants Jax **1 Experience Point (1 XP)** (meeting his daily limit of 1 XP/day).
> * **Freelance Pet Recovery (Silas, 1 AP):** Silas accesses the port’s local job board and accepts a quirky, low-stakes afternoon freelance contract: an orbital station manager's prized exotic pet—a nimble, feathered glidewing—slipped out of its quarters and vanished into the labyrinthine ventilation ducts. Silas combines his keen tracking instincts with station blueprints, making an **Instinct (4) + Knowledge (4)** Simple Test. Rolling 4d10, Silas gets `[3, 5, 8, 10]`—scoring two Hits (`8` and `10`). Silas isolates the animal's acoustic scuffling inside an atmospheric bypass valve and safely lures it into a travel crate, returning the creature unharmed and collecting **750 Credits** ($500 base + $250 bonus).
> * **Freelance Bodyguard Detail (Tessa, 1 AP):** Tessa takes a straightforward 8-hour personal security assignment escorting an independent mining prospector through Port Zenith's crowded lower decks during a high-value rare-earth mineral transaction. The GM calls for an **Agility (4) + Presence (3)** Simple Test. Rolling 3d10, Tessa scores `[2, 8, 9]`—two Hits! Tessa deters pickpockets and syndication scouts without incident, securing her client and collecting **750 Credits** ($500 base + $250 bonus).
>
> #### Shift 3: Night Shift & Progression (3 AP Spent)
>
> * **Freelance Market Security (Jax, 1 AP):** Feeling completely refreshed after his afternoon rest and workout, Jax accepts an evening security contract from Port Zenith's merchant association: catching slippery pickpockets working the crowded night bazaar concourse. Jax pairs his reflexes and situational awareness into an **Agility (4) + Instinct (3)** Simple Test. Taking a dice pool equal to the higher attribute (4d10), Jax rolls `[6, 8, 8, 10]`—an outstanding three Hits! Jax intercepts and collars multiple opportunistic cutpurses, returning stolen credit-chits to grateful vendors and earning a top-tier payout of **1,000 Credits** ($500 base + 2×$250 bonus).
> * **Zero-AP Upgrade Install & System Study (Silas, 1 AP):** Back aboard the ship, Silas installs the Tier 1 Upgrade Kit purchased by Tessa onto Jax's Boarding Axe, tuning the blade's micro-vibration motor to add **Lethal Tuning (+1 Damage Bonus)**. The GM reinforces a core crafting principle: **installing or swapping standard upgrade kits during Day-to-Day downtime costs 0 AP**. Silas spends his actual 1 AP studying encrypted sensor telemetry and astronomical charts, earning **1 Experience Point (1 XP)** (meeting his daily limit of 1 XP/day).
> * **Flight Simulator Drills (Tessa, 1 AP):** Tessa books an 8-hour session in the port’s orbital flight simulation bay, rehearsing emergency atmospheric reentry maneuvers under thruster failure conditions. Spending 1 Day-to-Day AP on focused technical drills grants Tessa **1 Experience Point (1 XP)** (meeting her daily limit of 1 XP/day).
>
> #### End of the Round: 24-Hour Heartbeat & Supply Consumption
>
> Exactly 24 hours elapse aboard Port Zenith as all 9 Day-to-Day Action Points are spent across the three shifts. The GM announces the **End of the Round** and resolves the universal heartbeat:
>
> 1. **Consuming Supplies:** The starship consumes **1 Supply** from its cargo hold to maintain environmental life support, atmospheric scrubbers, and crew rations (reducing the stored supply count from 5 to 4).
> 2. **Refilling Day-to-Day AP:** Because the vessel remains fully provisioned and paid its 1 Supply operational cost, all three crew members refill their pools to **3 Day-to-Day Action Points** for the upcoming day.
> 3. **Advancing the World Clock:** The calendar advances by one full day. Station docking clearances renew, orbital transit windows to Landfall adjust, and new Tier 1–3 Corsair Oversight contracts populate on the crew's commlink terminals.
>
> By organizing downtime into clear 8-hour AP shifts, the GM allows players to balance critical medical recovery, necessary equipment maintenance, and commercial shopping with productive freelance income and character progression—keeping the narrative moving forward without losing a single day of operational momentum.
