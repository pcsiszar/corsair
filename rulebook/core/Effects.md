# Effects

While succeeding at a test achieves your immediate objective, game mechanics in Corsair invite you to shape the battleground, create tactical openings, and alter the narrative flow of a scene. This is accomplished through **Effects**. Rather than relying solely on standard outcomes, Effects allow you to convert exceptional rolls into dynamic opportunities—turning a single strike into a sweeping attack, driving an opponent backward, or setting up a defensive barrier for an ally.

## Activating Effects

To activate an Effect, you sacrifice **Hits**—dice displaying a result of 8 or higher—rolled during an Action Roll or a Success Roll, depending on the specific Effect's requirements.

Activating an Effect is an exercise in collaborative storytelling between you and the Game Master. The Effect you declare must align logically with your character's action, declared narration, and chosen Attribute Pair. For instance, you cannot use an attack roll with a rifle to push a heavy blast door shut across the room, but you could easily describe forcing an enemy backward into cover or shooting out a control panel. The GM retains final discretion on whether an Effect fits naturally into the unfolding fiction.

## Action Effects

Action Effects are activated during your **Action Roll**. By setting aside Hits before moving on to resolve your Success Roll, you can manipulate the immediate environment or extend the reach of your action.

### Chain Effect
When performing an action directed at a single target out of several eligible choices—most commonly during combat—you may spend Hits on a **Chain Effect**. For each Hit you sacrifice from your Action Roll, you select one additional eligible target to include in the action. 

When chaining a Complex Test, you perform a single Success Roll as normal, using the lower Attribute of your pair. The highest die result from that single Success Roll determines the Success Points applied individually to every target caught in the chain. You cannot chain actions that naturally affect multiple targets by default. Furthermore, if the base action requires expending a specific resource (such as ammunition, power cells, or consumable gear), you must pay that resource cost separately for each chained target.

When making a ranged attack, you can only chain targets that are in the same range band. Furthermore, the combined use of Chain and Crit effects cannot exceed your weapon's Rate of Fire.

### Upgrade and Downgrade Effect
When your action creates a clear situational advantage for yourself or an impending disadvantage for an enemy, you can activate an **Upgrade or Downgrade Effect**. Each Hit spent from your Action Roll allows you to step up or step down a die on subsequent tests related to that altered circumstance. Crucially, if you are resolving a Complex Test, you may apply this effect immediately to step up dice on your own upcoming Success Roll, and you may do so without specifying the situational advantage you created.

### Blocker Effect
If your narration aims to hinder, interrupt, or prevent an enemy from carrying out a specific future action, you can establish a **Blocker Effect**. For every **2 Hits** you sacrifice from your Action Roll, you set aside 1 Hit to create a pool of defensive dice. When an opponent attempts the blocked action, each set-aside Hit negates the single highest die from their test pool before they resolve the outcome. 

Once a set-aside Hit negates an opponent's die, that set-aside Hit is expended and removed from the pool. The Blocker Effect persists until all set-aside Hits are exhausted. If an opponent attempts to perform an action that normally does not require a test (such as opening a latch or standing up), the Blocker Effect forces them to execute a **Simple Test** first; failing to eliminate all set-aside Hits prevents them from completing the action.

### Persistent Blocker Effect
A **Persistent Blocker Effect** functions similarly to a standard Blocker Effect (costing 2 Hits per set-aside Hit), with one key distinction: the pool of set-aside Hits does not deplete after blocking a single action. Instead, the full number of set-aside Hits resets after each blocked attempt. 

This effect is typically reserved for specialized gear, physical restraints, or elaborate environmental hazards—such as magnetic restraints or a smoke screen. A Persistent Blocker Effect remains active until an opponent succeeds at their action despite the penalty, or until the physical conditions generating the effect are removed or negated.

## Success Effects

Success Effects are activated during your **Success Roll**, allowing you to amplify the effectiveness or intensity of an action that has already proven successful.

### Crit Effect
The **Crit Effect** is the primary Success Effect available across all Success Rolls. By sacrificing Hits from your Success Roll pool, you enhance the impact of your effort. For every Hit you spend on a Crit Effect, you add **+2 Success Points** to your total outcome. This bonus is added directly to the highest remaining die value selected for your Success Points.

### Push Effect
A **Push Effect** allows you to leverage raw momentum or physical force to reposition a target. For each Hit sacrificed from your Success Roll, you knock or force the target backward by up to 4 meters. If you wish to maintain pressure or stay in close proximity, you may move along with the target as they are displaced without spending additional movement. Push Effects can only be activated if **Strength** (or a similar physical power attribute) is included in the test's Attribute Pair; they cannot be activated with tests relying solely on finesse, mental, or social attributes.

---

## Removing Effects

Ongoing environmental hazards, physical restraints, and persistent blockers do not have to remain forever. If a character is in a position to eliminate or neutralize an active ongoing effect, they may spend **1 Action Point** on their turn to make a **Simple Test** determined by the GM. Each Hit scored on this Simple Test removes 1 set-aside Hit from the targeted effect, gradually breaking down the obstacle until it is completely eliminated.

## Combined Mechanics in Action

> Jax and his team are attempting to breach a secure corporate research vault defended by automated security drones. Engaging in close combat at the armored bulkhead, Jax declares, *"I step in and swing my axe in a sweeping melee strike across the lead drone to tear open its plating while driving it back into its squadmate to clear the entryway."* 
> 
> The GM assesses the melee action and calls for a **Strength (5) + Strength (5)** Complex Test. Jax rolls 5d10 for his Action Roll based on his higher Attribute (**Strength 5**), producing `[3, 5, 8, 8, 9]`. 
> 
> With three Hits (`8`, `8`, and `9`), Jax spends one Hit (`8`) to activate an Action Effect—an **Upgrade Effect**—stepping up one die on his upcoming Success Roll from a d10 to a **d12**. He leaves his remaining Hits (`8` and `9`) to ensure his Action Roll succeeds.
> 
> Moving to his Success Roll for his lower Attribute (**Strength 5**), Jax rolls 4d10 and 1d12 (modified by his Upgrade Effect). His roll yields `[5, 6, 7, 8]` on the d10s and `8` on the d12. 
> 
> Jax scores two Hits (both `8`s) on his Success Roll. He decides to activate two Success Effects:
> 1. He spends his first `8` to activate a **Push Effect** (valid since his test relies on Strength), shoving the lead drone back 4 meters directly into its squadmate.
> 2. He spends his second `8` to activate a **Crit Effect**, granting **+2 Success Points** to his highest remaining die.
> 
> The highest remaining die on his Success Roll is `7`. By sacrificing his second `8` for Crit instead of keeping it as his base die, Jax increases his total from 8 to **9 Success Points** (7 + 2 = 9), dealing heavy damage to the drone while driving it clear of the doorway.
