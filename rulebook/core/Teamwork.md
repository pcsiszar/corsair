# Teamwork

Sometimes, a single character isn't enough for the job. When multiple characters cooperate on the same task at the same time—such as forcing open a hydraulic blast door, infiltrating past a vigilant patrol, or executing a coordinated breach—they can use **Teamwork**.

## Joining a Teamwork Action

Any character's action can be turned into a **Teamwork Test** by additional eligible characters declaring their intent to join. A character can join a Teamwork action if:

* **Proximity:** The participants are in close proximity. In combat or moment-to-moment pacing, they must share the same space (2-meter area).
* **Appropriate Contribution:** Each participant rolls the Attribute Pair appropriate to their weapon, gear, or method of action. Participants do not need to roll the same Attribute Pair! For example, two characters forcing a heavy blast door might both roll **Strength + Strength**, but in a coordinated combat assault, one character might swing a warhammer (**Strength + Finesse**, Striking) while an ally thrusts with a combat knife (**Agility + Finesse**, Piercing). Melee attacks always combine two different physical attributes (Striking uses Strength + Finesse, Slashing uses Agility + Strength, and Piercing uses Agility + Finesse). Because all tests in Corsair evaluate Hits on 8 or higher, dice can be swapped freely between pools regardless of which attributes or weapons generated them.
* **Shared Target or Objective:** If the action has a target or destination, it must be identical for all participants (firing on or striking the same enemy, treating the same wounded ally, forcing the same bulkhead door).
* **Logical Feasibility:** The task must be something that multiple people can realistically cooperate on simultaneously.
* **Action Point Cost:** Every character who joins must spend **1 Action Point (AP)**. While spending AP on Teamwork counts toward the active side's 2 AP per turn limit, reaching or exceeding the limit does not prevent additional characters from joining—the turn only concludes once the entire Teamwork test is fully resolved.

---

## The Dice Swap

After all participants have declared their intent and spent their Action Points, everyone rolls their respective test pools simultaneously.

Once the dice hit the table, **any two participants can swap any amount of dice between their pools**. 

The strategic purpose of this exchange is mutual support: characters with surplus high-value dice (`8+`) pass their extra results to allies who rolled low, ensuring the entire team achieves its goal or shaves away vulnerabilities.

---

## Contesting a Teamwork Action: Defender's Advantage

When an adversary is the target of a Teamwork action—such as an agile boss targeted by a coordinated crossfire or an alert sentry being bypassed by a creeping squad—the defender has the opportunity to react and contest.

To represent the tactical edge of a skilled or desperate combatant fighting against superior numbers, *Corsair* awards the **Defender's Advantage**:

```
TEAMWORK CONTEST SEQUENCE:
1. Team Rolls      -> All attacking participants roll their dice pools.
2. Team Swaps      -> Attackers coordinate and commit their dice distribution.
3. Defender Rolls  -> The defender spends 1 AP and rolls their Contest pool.
4. Target Elim     -> Defender allocates each Contest Hit (8+) to eliminate 
                      the highest die from a pool of their choice!
```

### 1. Timing: Committing Before the Strike
The defending character makes their **Contest Roll** *after* the attacking team has finalized their dice swap. The attackers must commit to their strategy—deciding whether to spread their high dice thin so everyone hits, or consolidate them into an overwhelming spearhead—before they see how the defender reacts.

### 2. Targeted Die Elimination
For each Hit (`8` or higher) scored on the defender's Contest Roll, the defender eliminates the **highest die from an attacking participant's pool of the defender's choice**. 

The defender does not have to eliminate dice evenly; they can focus all their Contest Hits onto a single attacker's pool, or split their Hits across multiple participants to disrupt several attackers at once.

### 3. Knocking Attackers Out of the Action
If the defender's Contest Hits eliminate every `8+` die from a character's pool:
* **In a Simple Test (Individual Hurdle):** That specific character fails their individual check (e.g., they make noise and get spotted during group stealth, or lose their grip).
* **In a Simple Test (Shared Goal):** As long as at least one participant retains an `8+` after all eliminations, the shared action still succeeds (e.g., the door is forced open).
* **In a Complex Test (Combat & Damage):** Any attacker reduced to zero `8+` dice **fails their Action Roll entirely**. That attacker's strike is parried, dodged, or deflected; they generate zero damage and **cannot advance to the Success Roll**. Only attackers who retain an `8+` move forward to make their Success Roll!

---

## Complex Tests in Teamwork

Teamwork can be employed across both stages of a **Complex Test**:

1. **Action Roll:** All attackers roll their primary pools. Attackers swap dice. The defender rolls their Contest Roll and allocates their eliminations to strip dice. Any attacker retaining an `8+` hits and proceeds to the Success Roll.
2. **Success Roll:** All remaining successful attackers roll their secondary pools for damage/Success Points. Surviving attackers can **swap dice between their Success pools** as well, allowing the team to funnel high dice onto an attacker with specialized Crit effects or maximum lethality! 
3. **Contesting the Success Roll:** If the defender has a reaction, shield, or ability that actively contests a Success Roll, the defender may roll to eliminate the highest dice from the attackers' Success pools before final Success Points are tallied.

---

## Group vs. Group: Teamwork Contests

Defenders can also use Teamwork to resist incoming actions. When two or more defenders meet the standard Teamwork requirements (in proximity, spending 1 AP each, reacting against the same action or attackers), they declare a **Teamwork Contest**:

1. All defenders roll their Contest pools simultaneously.
2. Defenders swap dice between their Contest pools to maximize their total number of Hits (`8+`).
3. The defenders' combined total of Hits is then allocated against the attacking team's pools, eliminating the highest dice from the attackers as chosen by the defending players or GM.

*Example: Two station security guards shoulder-to-shoulder behind a blast door spend 1 AP each to make a Teamwork Contest against two Corsairs attempting to force the door open.*

## Delayed Teamwork Actions

Multiple characters can coordinate to set up a shared **Delayed Action** (such as establishing a crossfire overwatch on a corridor or executing a synchronized ambush).

* **Setup Requirements:** All participants must share the same space, spend **1 AP** each on their turn, and declare the exact same **Trigger**, **Target**, and **Timing** (Before or After). Each character prepares the action and Attribute Pair appropriate to their chosen weapon or method (such as an operative leveling a rifle using **Accuracy + Finesse** while their brawler squadmate readies a warhammer using **Strength + Finesse**).
* **Joining an Existing Delay:** If a character already set up a Delayed Action on an earlier turn in the round, an ally can move into their space on a subsequent turn, spend 1 AP, and declare they are synchronizing into that ally's readied action.
* **Simultaneous Resolution (0 AP):** When the declared trigger condition is met, all linked participants roll their tests simultaneously for **0 additional AP**. The participants then swap dice between their pools under standard Teamwork rules.
* **Defender's Advantage:** If the target of a Delayed Teamwork attack contests, Defender's Advantage applies normally: the defender rolls their Contest Roll *after* the attackers finalize their dice swap, eliminating the highest dice from whichever attacker's pool the defender chooses.
* **Disruption & Expiration:** If an individual participant is incapacitated, knocked out of the space, or forced to defend before the trigger occurs, their readied action is broken, but remaining allies can still execute their delayed action when the condition occurs. If the round ends without the trigger occurring, the readied action expires for all participants.

## Examples in Play

### Example 1: Individual Hurdle (Group Stealth)

Jax, Griz, and Silas are attempting to slip past an automated sensor perimeter and security cameras outside an orbital armory. The GM calls for a Simple **Finesse (4) + Agility (3)** Teamwork Test where every character must achieve an `8+` to move through without triggering an alert:

1. **Initial Rolls:**
   * **Jax (4 dice):** rolls `[10, 9, 4, 2]` — Two Hits (a `10` and a `9`).
   * **Griz (2 dice):** rolls `[5, 3]` — Zero Hits (a failure; Griz is about to stumble on loose grating).
   * **Silas (3 dice):** rolls `[8, 6, 2]` — One Hit (an `8`).
2. **The Problem:** Griz failed his individual check. Under normal circumstances, his misstep would trip the perimeter alarms and compromise the entire operation.
3. **Teamwork & The Swap:** Seeing Griz lose his footing, Jax's player narrates: *"I snag Griz's harness before his boot clips the metal decking, whispering the guard patrol pattern and matching his stride to my own."*
   * Jax passes his spare `9` to Griz's pool in exchange for Griz's `3`.
4. **Final Pools & Outcome:**
   * **Jax's pool:** `[10, 4, 3, 2]` (Highest die: `10` — **Success**)
   * **Griz's pool:** `[9, 5]` (Highest die: `9` — **Success**)
   * **Silas's pool:** `[8, 6, 2]` (Highest die: `8` — **Success**)

Because every character now holds at least one die showing 8 or higher, Jax's surplus success covers Griz's mistake, and the entire team slips past the checkpoint completely undetected.

### Example 2: Combat & Defender's Advantage (Complex Test)

Jax and Silas team up to take down an elite corporate Enforcer in close-quarters combat. Both spend 1 AP to execute a coordinated melee assault. Each attacks with their favored weapon and attribute pair (remembering that melee attacks always combine two different physical attributes):
* **Jax** swings a heavy trench club (Striking: **Strength 4 + Finesse 3**), rolling 4d10 for his higher attribute.
* **Silas** lunges with a combat knife (Piercing: **Agility 4 + Finesse 3**), rolling 4d10 for his higher attribute.

1. **Action Rolls:**
   * Jax rolls 4d10: `[10, 9, 5, 2]` (Two Hits: `10`, `9`)
   * Silas rolls 4d10: `[6, 4, 3, 1]` (Zero Hits)
2. **The Swap (The Greedy Gamble):** Jax and Silas want both of their strikes to land so they can both inflict damage. Jax passes his `9` to Silas in exchange for Silas's `3`. Even though Jax rolled with Strength and Silas rolled with Agility, dice are swapped freely between their pools. Both now hold a single Hit:
   * Jax: `[10, 5, 3, 2]` (One Hit: `10`)
   * Silas: `[9, 6, 4, 1]` (One Hit: `9`)
3. **The Enforcer Contests:** The Enforcer spends 1 AP to contest with an **Agility (3) Evasion** roll, rolling 3d10: `[2, 7, 8]` — **1 Hit!**
4. **Targeted Elimination:** With Defender's Advantage, the Enforcer chooses which pool to target. The Enforcer spots Silas overextended from his lunge, knocks the knife aside with a vambrace, and applies the Hit to Silas's pool, eliminating his `9`!
5. **Outcome:**
   * Silas's pool is reduced to `[6, 4, 1]`. Having no `8+` remaining, Silas fails his Action Roll completely; his strike is deflected and he **cannot advance to the Success Roll**.
   * Jax still retains his `10`. Jax's club blow connects solidly; Jax advances to the Success Roll to deal damage!

### Example 3: Synchronized Ambush (Delayed Teamwork)

Jax and Silas are stacked beside a security airlock, waiting for an enemy patrol scout to enter. During the Player Turn, both spend 1 AP to set up a **Delayed Teamwork Action** using **Accuracy (4) + Finesse (3)** with their sidearms:
* **The Trigger:** A hostile crossing the airlock threshold.
* **The Timing:** **Before** (firing to intercept the target before they can raise a weapon or take cover).

On the GM Turn, the patrol scout steps through the airlock. The trigger fires:
1. **Simultaneous Rolls (0 AP):** Both characters fire simultaneously:
   * Jax rolls 4d10: `[10, 8, 4, 3]` (Two Hits)
   * Silas rolls 4d10: `[7, 5, 2, 1]` (Zero Hits)
2. **The Swap:** Jax passes his `8` to Silas in exchange for Silas's `2`. Both now hold a Hit:
   * Jax: `[10, 4, 3, 2]`
   * Silas: `[8, 7, 5, 1]`
3. **The Scout Contests:** The scout spends 1 AP on an Evasion Contest (**Agility 3**), rolling 3d10: `[4, 6, 9]` — **1 Hit!**
4. **Defender Allocation:** The scout uses his Hit to eliminate Jax's `10`. However, Silas's `8` remains intact!
5. **Outcome:** Silas's shot hits the scout dead-on, advancing to the Success Roll and taking down the target before they can clear the doorway!
