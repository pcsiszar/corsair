Often, a test isn't just against a static obstacle; it's against an active opponent who is trying to interfere. When a
character is the target of another character's test (like being shot at, deceived, or tackled), they have the
opportunity to contest.

In order to contest an action taken by another creature, you must be in close physical proximity to its target—typically sharing the immediate space with them. For example, you can contest an attack coming at you or an ally right beside you, and you can contest an enemy's movement crossing your space. On the other hand, you cannot contest a hostile creature reviving an ally 40 meters away by shooting at them.

To perform a **Contest**, the defending creature rolls a **Simple Test** determined by the GM (called a **Contest Roll**). Each die landing **8 or higher (a Hit)** eliminates the highest die from the roll you contested.

You **resist** a test if your Contest Roll eliminates every 8+ die from the contested roll.

You can contest **Success Tests** as well. If, after contesting, no 8+ dice remain in the contested character's pool, the Success Test generates **0 Success Points**, even if there are dice that are yet to be eliminated.

You can also contest actions that would normally not require a test (such as shushing someone before they can speak, or preventing an enemy you knocked prone from standing up). When this happens, you declare your intent to contest, and the contested creature must decide if they are willing to perform a **Simple Test** to complete their action—which you can then contest—or forego it altogether.

#### Contest Example

> Returning to our gunslinger Jax: suppose Jax fires at a fleeing syndicate smuggler trying to reach an airlock, rolling `[2, 4, 5, 7, 8]`. The suspect has an **Evasion** of 3 and dives behind reinforced deck piping. The GM calls for an Evasion Contest Roll (**3d10**). The smuggler rolls `[3, 8, 9]`—scoring two Hits (8 and 9). These two Hits eliminate Jax's two highest dice (the 8 and the 7). Jax's remaining pool is `[2, 4, 5]`. With no die of 8 or higher remaining, the attack becomes a **Failure**; the round ricochets harmlessly off the reinforced piping as the suspect scrambles out of the line of fire.

### Contesting Teamwork Actions: Defender's Advantage

When an adversary is targeted by a **Teamwork Action**, the defender gains the **Defender's Advantage**:
1. **Team Rolls:** All attacking participants roll their dice pools simultaneously.
2. **Team Swaps:** Attackers coordinate and commit their dice distribution.
3. **Defender Rolls:** The defender rolls their Contest pool *after* the attackers commit their dice swap.
4. **Targeted Elimination:** Each Hit (8+) on the Contest Roll allows the defender to eliminate the highest die from **whichever participating attacker's pool the defender chooses**. The defender can focus all Hits onto a single attacker to knock them out of the test, or split Hits across multiple foes.
5. **Knocking Attackers Out:** In a Complex Test, if the defender eliminates all 8+ dice from an attacker's pool, that attacker fails their Action Roll completely and **cannot advance to the Success Roll**. Only attackers who retain an 8+ move forward to roll damage!

For full rules and multi-defender coordination, see [Teamwork](Teamwork.md).
