# Melee Weapons

*You can download and print the [Melee Weapons Printable Reference Sheet](Melee_Sheet.html) for play.*

In the tight corridors of starships, depressurized maintenance shafts, and claustrophobic orbital stations, discharging a firearm risks puncturing critical hull plating or ricocheting in zero-gravity. In these confined environments, a dependable melee weapon is an indispensable tool for survival. Whether utilizing precision combat knives, weighted riot clubs, or heavy two-handed mauls, close-quarters combat demands physical prowess, tactical positioning, and mastery over your weapon's mechanical profile.

---

## Weapon Statistics Explained

Every melee weapon is defined by its mechanical attributes, which dictate how it interacts with your character's physical statistics and tactical actions during combat:

* **Damage Type & Favored Attribute Pair:** Melee weapons inflict damage through one of three distinct physical methods: **Piercing**, **Slashing**, or **Striking**. Each damage type is inherently paired with a specific physical Attribute Pair that maximizes the weapon's leverage and momentum:
  * **Piercing (Agility + Finesse):** Swift, pinpoint thrusts directed at structural seams, visor slits, and armor joints.
  * **Slashing (Agility + Strength):** Wide, sweeping kinetic cuts that combine dynamic movement with heavy cutting force.
  * **Striking (Strength + Finesse):** Controlled, bone-shattering impacts delivered with precise leverage and disciplined body mechanics.

* **Favored Pair Damage Bonus:** When you make an attack test using the weapon's favored Attribute Pair, you gain a damage bonus equal to the **lower attribute** in that pair. While a character is free to narrate and perform a melee attack using any combination of two physical attributes, choosing the weapon's favored pair unlocks this substantial damage boost.

* **Minimum Strength (Min STR):** Physical combat tools require sufficient muscular leverage and mass to control effectively. Each weapon lists a Minimum Strength score:
  * **One-Handed Weapons (1H):** If your Strength is lower than the weapon's Min STR, you cannot wield the weapon effectively with a single hand; you must wield it with **two hands** to attack with it.
  * **Two-Handed Weapons (2H):** If your Strength is lower than the weapon's Min STR, the weapon is too cumbersome or heavy to wield, and you **cannot attack** with it under any circumstances.

* **Damage Bonus (Dmg Bonus):** A flat numerical value added directly to the total success points generated on a successful attack roll. Weapons with higher Minimum Strength requirements are heavier and more devastating, featuring significantly larger flat Damage Bonuses.

* **Hands:** Indicates whether the weapon is naturally wielded in one hand (**1H**) or requires both hands (**2H**).

* **Load:** The mass and physical bulk of the weapon as governed by the inventory Load system.

* **Cost:** The standard market value of the weapon in Credits.

## Dual Wielding

A combatant can choose to hold and fight with a weapon in each hand, creating a rapid, aggressive combat style that overwhelms opponents with combined offensive pressure.

* **One-Handed Only:** You can only dual wield weapons with the **1H** (one-handed) designation. You must meet the Minimum Strength requirement of each weapon individually to wield them simultaneously in separate hands.
* **Combined Damage Bonus:** When you make a melee attack while dual wielding two 1H weapons, you gain the flat **Damage Bonus of both weapons** added to your total outcome.
* **Attack Resolution:** When attacking, you designate which of your two weapons is leading the strike to determine the attack's **Damage Type** (Piercing, Slashing, or Striking) and its corresponding favored Attribute Pair. If your declared action utilizes that favored pair, you also add the lower attribute of the test to the combined damage.

## Melee Upgrade Kit

Even standard issue weapons can be customized into mastercrafted instruments through high-grade aerospace machining, monomolecular edge-honing, ergonomic carbon-fiber balancing, or high-frequency vibration emitters. Because each subsequent modification requires increasingly complex precision engineering, upgrade kits are steeply priced and escalate in cost with every installation.

* **Upgrade Limit:** A single melee weapon can benefit from a maximum of **3 Upgrade Kits** in total.
* **Tiered Cost Structure:**
  * **1st Upgrade:** **10,000 Credits**
  * **2nd Upgrade:** **25,000 Credits**
  * **3rd Upgrade:** **50,000 Credits**
* **Upgrade Choices:** Each time an Upgrade Kit is applied, choose one of the following permanent modifications:
  * **Lightweight Tuning:** Precision skeletal milling and composite counterbalancing reduce the weapon's **Load by 1** (to a minimum of 1/2 Load).
  * **Lethal Edge / Weighted Core:** High-frequency vibration motors or reinforced carbide weights increase the weapon's flat **Damage Bonus by +1**.

## Shields

Shields are rigid defensive barriers constructed from ballistic polymers, layered ceramics, or reinforced titanium alloys. Wielded in the off-hand, they provide vital cover during tactical breeches and close-quarters engagements.

* **One Free Hand Required:** Wielding a shield requires **one hand (1H)**. You cannot wield a two-handed weapon or perform two-handed tasks while actively holding a shield.
* **Physical Protection Bonus:** While held, a shield grants a flat bonus to your **Physical Protection** (e.g., +1, +2, +3). This bonus stacks directly with the Protection value provided by your worn armor, reducing incoming kinetic and physical damage before it affects your health.
* **No Dual Wielding Shields:** A character cannot wield two shields simultaneously, nor can they benefit from more than one shield's Protection bonus at a time.
* **Minimum Strength:** Like melee weapons, heavier shields require significant physical conditioning to brace effectively against high-velocity kinetic impacts. If your Strength is lower than a shield's Min STR, you cannot wield the shield.

## Example: Melee Combat in Action

> ### Tactical Example: The Boarding Action Strike
> Jax enters a contested airlock space occupied by a hostile pirate enforcer. Armed with a **Boarding Axe** (a Slashing weapon with Min STR 3, 1H, Load 2, and a **+2 Damage Bonus**), Jax initiates a close-quarters melee strike. Jax's character sheet shows **Strength 5**, **Agility 4**, and **Finesse 3**.
> 
> Because the Boarding Axe is a **Slashing** weapon, its favored Attribute Pair is **Agility + Strength**. Jax declares a sweeping overhand cleave, taking full advantage of the weapon's favored pair. With his Strength 5, Jax easily satisfies the weapon's Min STR 3 requirement, allowing him to wield it effortlessly in one hand.
> 
> 1. **Action Roll:** Jax rolls a dice pool equal to the higher attribute in the pair (**Strength 5**), rolling 5d10: `[3, 5, 7, 8, 9]`. Both `8` and `9` are Hits (8+), meaning the Action Roll succeeds. Jax spends one of his Hits (`8`) to activate an **Upgrade Effect**, stepping up one die in his upcoming Success Roll from a d10 to a **d12**.
> 2. **Success Roll:** Moving to the Success Roll for the lower attribute (**Agility 4**), Jax rolls 3d10 and 1d12: rolling `[4, 6, 7]` on the d10s and a `9` on the upgraded d12. The highest single die value is **9**, granting Jax a base of **9 Success Points**.
> 3. **Calculating Damage:** Because Jax attacked using the weapon's favored pair (**Agility + Strength**), he adds the value of the lower attribute (**Agility 4**) directly to his damage:
>    * Base Success Points: **9**
>    * Favored Pair Bonus (Lower Attribute = Agility): **+4**
>    * Weapon Flat Damage Bonus: **+2**
> 
> Jax deals a total of **15 points of damage** (9 + 4 + 2), smashing through the pirate enforcer's defenses.
