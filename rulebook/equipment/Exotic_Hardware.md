# Exotic Hardware

In the commercial bazaars and scrap-alleys of the Sphere, money can buy you a great many things. A modest stack of Flotilla Credits will purchase a dependable pump shotgun that smells faintly of machine oil, a suit of reinforced ballistic weave that might keep your ribs inside your chest when an Ork marine kicks you through a bulkhead, or a pair of magnetic boots that only fail three percent of the time. These are the workhorse tools of Newcomer civilization: mass-produced, thoroughly documented, and priced so that a greenhorn Corsair cell doesn't bankrupt their sponsor on week two.

And then there is the other stuff.

Exotic Hardware refers to the narrow, intoxicating, and thoroughly lethal category of equipment that exists far beyond standard military procurement. These are the crown jewels of black-budget corporate skunkworks, the un-cataloged prototypes liberated from clandestine research labs on Nimbus, and the terrifying, cold-glass relics dragged kicking and screaming from subterranean Precursor vaults. In the lexicon of ancient fantasy tabletop games, they are the "magic items" of *Corsair*—objects possessing properties that seem to bend the rules of engagement.

Yet in accordance with the fundamental laws of the Sphere, there is no magic here. Every prototype operates on bleeding-edge physics, extreme thermal shunting, and precision mechanical engineering. Every Precursor relic is a solid-state transducer tapping into the diffuse dark matter halo engineered by the Singularity. They are rare, exorbitantly expensive, and carried only by veterans who have both the coin to afford them and the physical constitution to carry their crushing weight.

## The Rules of Exotic Hardware

Exotic Hardware functions under distinct operational constraints designed to reward tactical mastery without trivializing the hard-scrabble realities of combat:

1. **Load Taxation Over Arbitrary Limits:** There is no mystical "attunement" ceiling in *Corsair*. An operative is free to carry as many pieces of Exotic Hardware as their spine can endure. However, exotic hardware is dense, power-hungry, and heavily shielded. Weapons, power units, and field rigs carry substantial **Load** (typically Load 2 to 5), while exotic carapaces represent a massive encumbrance investment. Because every operative is bound by the absolute carrying capacity of **$12 + (\text{Strength} \times 2)$**, packing multiple exotic systems forces agonizing compromises: you cannot wear a kinetic dissipation carapace, carry a dark-transducer carbine, and haul a seismic resonance rig without leaving your spare medical kits and sidearms back on the ship.
2. **Action Point Economy:** Activating an active exotic hardware function costs **1 Action Point (AP)**, or acts as an integrated enhancement to an existing 1-AP combat action. Passive defenses, sensory suites, and permanent material enhancements apply continuously, but active bursts always demand dedicated tactical focus.
3. **Pacing Cooldowns & Thermal Cycles:** Bleeding-edge prototypes generate severe thermal spikes and power drains. Instead of abstract cooldown counters, exotic cooling cycles are measured directly in Corsair's narrative pacing rounds:
   * **Moment-to-Moment Cooldowns:** Recover after **1 moment-to-moment round** (~5–10 seconds), resetting cleanly at the start of the operative's next combat turn.
   * **Place-to-Place Cooldowns:** Recover after **1 place-to-place round** (~1 hour). The operative must complete their short transit or tactical breach, flush cryo-coolant loops, or vent residual capacitor heat into a starship heat sink before the system can fire again.
   * **Day-to-Day Cooldowns:** Recover after **1 day-to-day round** (a full 24-hour downtime cycle), resetting after a proper shift of sleep, workshop calibration, and trickle-charging.
4. **Irreplaceable Maintenance:** When a piece of Exotic Hardware suffers structural damage and is reduced to 0 HP, it cannot be patched up with a generic soldering iron and a roll of thermal tape. Repairing a classified prototype requires an operative with a dedicated workshop and a successful **Tinkering + Knowledge** test against a Difficulty set by the item's Tier. Damaged Precursor relics cannot be repaired by mortal hands at all, unless the cell acquires rare molecular salvage or secures the favor of an Ancestral artisan or the scholar dragon Stormdrinker.
5. **Non-Customizable Architecture (No Upgrade Kits):** Exotic weapons, protective carapaces, and tactical hardware are precision-calibrated skunkworks trials or alien crystalline lattices whose tolerances operate at the atomic level. Because of this, **Exotic Hardware cannot be customized or modified with standard Upgrade Kits** (such as mastercrafted +1 Damage enhancements, Weight Reduction kits, or aftermarket weapon attachments). Attempting to drill, file, or bolt third-party components onto an exotic weapon short-circuits its induction coils, shatters non-baryonic barrel sleeves, or completely ruins its harmonic calibration. Their statistics, properties, and Load values are permanently fixed as written.

## The 4-Tier Rarity & Economic Ladder

To prevent market confusion and establish clear campaign progression, Exotic Hardware is organized into four distinct tiers of rarity, cost, and availability:

| Tier | Classification | Typical Cost (Credits) | Market Reality & Acquisition |
| :---: | :--- | :---: | :--- |
| **Tier 1** | **Mil-Spec Skunkworks** | $15,000 – 30,000 \text{ CR}$ | Limited-run military trials, skunkworks modifications, and high-spec aerospace machining from premier foundries (Caldera Foundry, Port Zenith Engineering, Panthera Arms). Available through high-end corporate fixers, retired veteran armories, or wealthy colonial sponsors. |
| **Tier 2** | **Classified Prototypes** | $40,000 – 80,000 \text{ CR}$ | Bleeding-edge corporate espionage targets, hybrid reverse-engineered alien polymers, and experimental cybernetic field rigs. Heavily guarded by corporate strike teams; acquired through corporate black-site heists, black-market auctions, or high-threat unsanctioned contracts. |
| **Tier 3** | **Precursor Relics** | $100,000 – 250,000 \text{ CR}$ | Intact alien artifacts, non-baryonic catalytic lattices, and self-repairing alloys recovered from deep Vault excavations or the Evergaol crash site. Strictly illegal contraband under the Covenant Accords; traded only in deep-underworld circles (the Jailers, Iron Fang, or Scav warlords). |
| **Tier 4** | **Singularities & Unique Wonders** | **Priceless** (Not for Sale) | Mythic, one-of-a-kind artifacts of campaign-defining significance (e.g., an intact dreadnought catalytic core, an uncorrupted Shard memory lattice, or an intact Precursor null-beam projector). Found exclusively as major campaign milestones or climactic salvage objectives. |
