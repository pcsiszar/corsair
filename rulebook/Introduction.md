# Welcome to Corsair

> ### The Elevator Pitch
> ***Corsair*** is a tabletop roleplaying game of high-stakes frontier adventure, tactical problem-solving, and found-family camaraderie, set against the vast backdrop of a macro-engineered cosmos. 
>
> You and your crew portray a **Corsair Cell**: licensed, independent operatives flying a versatile starship with a cross-jurisdictional charter. When local planetary law ends at the clouds and the naval peacekeepers of the Flotilla are light-hours away, your sponsors call on you. Whether you are investigating corporate sabotage in a spinning orbital arcology, braving derelict Precursor ruins for lost technology, negotiating tense standoffs across a fraying border, or scraping together enough fuel credits to keep your reaction thrusters burning, your crew stands as the thin line holding a young civilization together.

## Who Are the Corsairs?

### The Sphere at a Glance
The game takes place within the **Sphere**, a breathtaking engineered solar system featuring over a hundred terrestrial, habitable worlds and moons locked in tight binary orbits around colossal gas giants, all circling a quiet Orange Dwarf star. 

Circa five hundred years ago, automated seed arks brought the survivors of four dying worlds into this seemingly abandoned system. Arriving as the **Newcomers**, humanity and three alien lineages—the cold-blooded, crested **Naga**, the dense-skinned **Craggen**, and the chlorophyllic **Sylvan**—made their first historic landfall on a fertile moon appropriately named **Landfall**. Over five centuries of shared survival, exploration, and cultural blending across the moons, six natural hybrid lineages took root alongside them: **Drakelings**, **Elves**, **Gnomes**, **Dwarves**, **Kobolds**, and **Trolls**.

Today, civilization spans dozens of bustling worlds, gleaming orbital transfer stations, and remote mining habitats. Yet the laws of nature remain stubbornly grounded. There is no faster-than-light warp drive; ships burn continuous fusion engines along calculated orbital curves. There is no artificial gravity plating; you stay grounded either through the axial acceleration of your ship’s thrusters, the gentle centrifugal spin of a habitat drum, or the solid click of magnetic boots against steel decks. And in the vacuum of space, waste heat is as dangerous as an enemy kinetic slug.

### Your Role: The Corsair Cell
Civilization across the moons is divided by a historic treaty: planetary governments hold total jurisdiction within their atmospheric gravity wells, while the peacekeeping fleet known as the **Flotilla** polices the open void and interstellar shipping lanes. This separation creates a glaring legal seam. Smugglers, corporate raiders, rogue mercenaries, and political conspirators routinely commit crimes under planetary clouds and burn into orbit, daring local authorities to chase them past their legal boundaries.

To bridge this seam, authorities grant independent charters to **Corsairs**—small, autonomous crews given cross-jurisdictional warrants to cross from planetary mud to the cold vacuum of orbit without getting bogged down in red tape.

As players, you form a **Corsair Cell**—a tight-knit crew of 3 to 5 specialists sharing an agile starship. Every cell is distinct:
* **Conflicting Sponsors:** Legitimate Corsairs are typically backed by multiple, competing sponsors—such as a Flotilla naval commander, a planetary governor, a commercial trading guild, or an independent labor union. By balancing competing patrons, your crew maintains its operational independence.
* **The Omni-Role Ethos:** You are practical survivors, not rigid archetypes. While every operative has specialized training or hardware rigs, no Corsair is locked into a single duty. Every member of the crew knows how to draw a sidearm in a corridor firefight, monitor a sensor array, pilot a shuttle, patch a ruptured environmental suit, and haggle for spare parts at an orbital bazaar.
* **Freedom of Approach:** Whether your crew acts as principled investigators, gritty salvage hounds, diplomatic fixers, or razor-sharp troubleshooters, how you tackle your contracts is entirely up to you.

## What is a Roleplaying Game?

A roleplaying game (RPG) is a collaborative storytelling experience. You and your friends create characters to portray, and together you navigate a living world full of adventure, intrigue, and danger.

One player takes on the role of the **Game Master (GM)**—often referred to in Corsair as the **Warden**. The GM is the primary storyteller, the referee of the rules, and the voice of the non-player characters (NPCs) that inhabit the void. The other players portray the **Player Characters (PCs)**, the specialist operatives who make up the Corsair crew and drive the story forward.

The game unfolds through a natural conversation:
1. **The GM describes the situation:** The environment, the sensory details of the scene, and any imminent hazards or NPCs present.
2. **The players declare their intent:** You state what your characters do, say, or attempt—whether moving into cover, overriding an airlock seal, or negotiating terms with a tense frontier marshal.
3. **The mechanics determine uncertain outcomes:** When an action is routine, it simply succeeds. But when an outcome is uncertain, dangerous, or contested, the dice hit the table using the rules outlined in this book.
4. **The GM describes the consequences:** The fiction moves forward, the scene shifts, and the conversation continues.

There are no winners or losers in a roleplaying game. The goal is to collaborate, face tactical challenges, and tell a memorable, cinematic story together.

### What You'll Need to Play

To play Corsair, you will need a few essentials:
* **Dice:** The game uses three standard polyhedral die types: eight-sided (**d8**), ten-sided (**d10**), and twelve-sided (**d12**) dice. A handful of each die type (especially d10s, which form the primary resolution dice pool) is ideal for the table.
* **Character & Crew Sheets:** Printable sheets for recording your operative’s attributes, skills, equipment, and your starship’s modules.
* **Pencils & Scratch Paper:** For tracking notes, tactical sketches, and changing game states.
* **Tokens or Markers:** Simple counters (coins, poker chips, or glass beads) are useful for tracking Action Points and tactical positions during moment-to-moment encounters.
* **A Group of Friends:** Corsair is designed for 3 to 5 players, including the Game Master.
