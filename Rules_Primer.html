◈ THE CORSAIR ELEVATOR PITCH BRIEFING

Corsair is a game of high-stakes frontier adventure, blending the mythic wonder of Space Fantasy with the grounded weight of Hard Science Fiction. You play a Corsair Cell—licensed, independent specialists operating an agile starship with a cross-jurisdictional charter holding together a young, fragile civilization.

00.2 The Four Tenets of the Void

Before your crew steps through the airlock, keep these foundational realities at the core of every operation:

  1. Newton is Sovereign: Space stations and warships obey Newtonian mechanics. There are no artificial gravity plates. Weight exists only when an engine burns or when an orbital station's centrifuge rings spin. Magnetic boots and safety tethers keep spacers alive.
  2. The Vacuum Forgives Nothing: Kinetic railguns, hypervelocity slugthrowers, and thermal cutting beams generate lethal waste heat that must be radiated through glowing thermal arrays. A single puncture means immediate decompression.
  3. The Seam Between Mud and Orbit: The Skyline Accords dictate that planetary governments govern within atmospheric skies, while the Flotilla rules open space. Corsairs exist solely to hunt across that jurisdictional boundary.
  4. Impartiality in the Crossfire: Every cell is co-sponsored by competing powers (Flotilla, Colonies, Corporations, NGOs) to ensure neutrality under the watchful eye of the veteran Oversight Committee.

THE CLOCKWORK HEAVENS

THE SPHERE, BINARY MOONS, LIVING DRAGONS & THE TEN LINEAGES

01.1 The Macro-Engineered Sphere

Five centuries ago, automated seed arks brought the survivors of four dying worlds into the Sphere. Rather than a naturally accreted stellar system, the Sphere is a marvel of celestial macro-engineering: concentric rings of massive gas giants locked in Laplace gravitational resonance around a quiet, benevolent Orange Dwarf star.

Within the magnetic shelters of these gas giants, terrestrial worlds orbit in close-knit binary pairs. Separated by mere tens of thousands of kilometers, neighboring moons hang like colossal jewels in each other's skies. Standing on the docks of Pelagia, you can watch the amber sun strike the cratered ridges of twin moon Suncatcher without a telescope.

Hard Sci-Fi Brachistochrone Realities: Because moons orbit in close binary pairs, sub-light fusion torch ships burning at a continuous 1g Brachistochrone acceleration can transit between binary worlds in mere hours. Crossing between neighboring gas giants (such as Fume to X'ara) takes only 2.2 days. The Sphere is tightly connected, but demands strict orbital fuel budgeting.

01.2 The Ancient Living Dragons

The titans of the void are not myths, but colossal, ancient biological entities navigating open vacuum via internal bio-fusion dynamos. Sovereign beings of immense intellect, their alliances shape civilization:

  • Goldheart: The gentle scholar dragon watching over Landfall, whose early guidance shielded the first human, naga, craggen, and sylvan cohorts.
  • Stormdrinker: The thunderous titan circling the electrical storm-wells of Nimbus, who shared bio-fusion principles with Newcomer engineers in 35 AA, birthing interplanetary flight.

01.3 Ten Lineages, Two Philosophies

Newcomer civilization is composed of ten distinct biological lineages tracing back to the four original seed arks that touched down on Landfall in 0 AA:

  • The Four Founding Templates: The adaptable Humans, crest-bearing cold-blooded Naga, mineral-skinned and crystalline-eyed Craggen, and tall, chlorophyllic Sylvan.
  • The Six Hybrid Lineages: Over five centuries of shared survival, natural intermingling produced the draconic Drakelings, graceful Elves, ingenious Gnomes, sturdy Dwarves, gemstone-masked Kobolds, and towering Trolls.

Integrationism vs. Preservationism

Society is divided by two profound cultural philosophies:

Integrationism: Championed in metropolitan melting pots like Crossroads and Cloudspire. Integrationists believe that cultural blending, shared destiny, and inter-lineage solidarity represent the true future of the Sphere.

Preservationism: A traditionalist movement born from filial reverence for lost ancestral homeworlds. Preservationists maintain insular, single-lineage colonies, dedicated to keeping ancestral bloodlines, languages, and rituals pure to honor their forgotten ancestors.

◈ VACUUM INVARIANT // THE COLD FRONTIER ASTROGATION

Space has no atmosphere to carry sound, no magnetic fields outside the gas giant envelopes, and no mercy for neglected thermal radiators. A Corsair crew trusts their fusion torch, their sealed suit, and their comrades.

THE LAW OF THE VOID

THE SKYLINE ACCORDS, THE FLOTILLA & THE NEW WAVE CORSAIRS

02.1 The Skyline Accords (335 AA)

The fundamental constitutional treaty of Newcomer civilization divides authority into two sovereign realms:

  • The Sovereign Moons: Planetary governments hold absolute jurisdiction within atmospheric gravity wells, enforcing local laws, corporate charters, and property rights.
  • The Flotilla: The sovereign peacekeeping fleet that holds exclusive jurisdiction over open space, orbital lanes, customs hubs, and Lagrange stations.

While this treaty prevented planetary wars, it created a dangerous jurisdictional seam: criminals and corporate raiders routinely strike dirt-side settlements and burn into orbit, daring local authorities to chase them past atmospheric limits.

02.2 The Flotilla & The Tour

The Flotilla is commanded by the Admiralty Board (presiding from Redmane Station), which answers directly to the civilian High Council of elected colonial delegates.

The One-Year Tour: Every 18-year-old across the Sphere is drafted into the fleet for 12 months. Wealthy heirs scrape reaction-thruster carbon burns alongside rough street kids, forging a shared system-wide identity.

The Matrix: Every sailor belongs to one of 6 Wings (*Administrative, Transit, Patrol, Scout, Bastion, Sword*) and one of 7 Corps (*Marine, Pilot, Engineering, Medical, Officer, Sailor, Admin*).

02.3 The New Wave of Corsairs (518 AA)

Exactly ten years after the Fall of the ancient dreadnought Evergaol in 508 AA shattered naval battle fleets, the High Council authorized the New Wave of Corsairs to police the frayed frontier.

The Corsair Cell: Autonomous strike teams of 3 to 5 specialists based aboard a single, agile starship (such as an RF "Kestrel"). The system stands on three pillars:

  • Sponsors: Every Corsair is funded by a major power (Flotilla, Colonial administration, Megacorporation, Union, or Private patron). To enforce impartiality, cells feature mandatory mixed sponsorships so no single faction dominates.
  • The Oversight Committee: Composed of exemplary retired Corsairs and civilian delegates who review contract petitions for the public good and investigate operative misconduct.
  • Cross-Jurisdictional Mandate: Sweeping legal authority to carry military ordnance and pursue warrants from planetary mud to deep orbit.

Three Modes of Play

  • Freelancers (Aspirants): Uncommissioned ground-level crews with no sponsor or ship, taking commercial shuttles to earn notice.
  • Chartered Corsairs: Commissioned cells with sponsors, a dedicated starship, and official legal mandates.
  • Rogue Corsairs (Outlaws): Former Corsairs operating re-flagged ships in the black market, navigating backchannel favors.

CHARACTER CREATION & MANTLE RIGS

SPONSORS, TEN LINEAGES, CORE RATINGS & CHASSIS INTERFACES

03.1 Building an Operative

  1. Select Mode of Play: Decide with your table if you are starting as uncommissioned Freelancers, commissioned Corsairs (with starship), or hunted Rogue Corsairs.
  2. Choose Your Sponsor: If playing as Corsairs, each player selects a distinct sponsor (Flotilla, Colonial, Corporate, Civilian, Private) to guarantee cell impartiality.
  3. Select Biological Lineage (Race):
    • Human: Adaptable; gain +4 XP (10 total starting XP).
    • Craggen: Mineral-skinned; +2 points to Finesse/Knowledge.
    • Naga: Crested reptilian; +2 points to Agility/Instinct.
    • Sylvan: Chlorophyllic tusker; +2 points to Strength/Presence.
    • Hybrids: Drakeling, Elf, Gnome, Dwarf, Kobold, Troll grant tailored attribute splits and bonus XP.
  4. Assign Core Attributes & Condition Tracks: All six ratings start at 1. Distribute points across Strength, Agility, Finesse, Knowledge, Presence, Instinct. Calculate Endurance (2nd highest Physical) and Willpower (2nd highest Mental). Physical harm is tracked via Light Wounds () and Aggravated Wounds (); mental strain via Light Stress () and Aggravated Stress ().

03.2 The MANTLE Chassis

In Corsair, your class is a proprietary MANTLE (*Modular Adaptive Neural Transmission Linked Equipment*)—an aerospace cybernetic chassis interfacing directly with your spine via a Neural Control Chip (NCC). Due to synaptic bandwidth, you can equip only one MANTLE chassis at a time.

  • Conduit: Elemental shock trooper channeling capacitor charges ( Charge, max 3). Class: Flame, Frost, Lightning.
  • Hazard: Combat chemist wielding pressurized reactive catalysts ( Pressure, 0–3 gauge). Class: Venom, Hazard, Serum.
  • Pilot: Neurally integrated operator commanding an Exodrone avatar ( Battery, max 10). Class: Power, Speed, Precision.
  • Vanguard: High-mobility frontline operative with a morphing kinetic board ( Boost, banked d10). Class: Shield, Glider, Cannon.
  • Weaver: Nanite artisan manipulating a programmable fluid swarm ( Reserve, base 4L). Class: Create, Control, Modify.

Class Attribute Points: All three class attributes start at 1. Spend 7 Class Creation Points to customize your signature chassis ratings.

ATTRIBUTES & THE THREE TESTS

ATTRIBUTE PAIRS, D10 RESOLUTION & THE TWO-STAGE COMPLEX ROLL

04.1 Attribute Pairs & Dice Pools

When taking action under pressure, you never roll an isolated stat. The GM pairs the two most relevant attributes based on your narrative description into an Attribute Pair (e.g., Finesse + Agility for a zero-g shot, or Strength + Strength to force a hatch). Class attributes can pair with core attributes (e.g., Flame + Strength).

The Threshold for a Hit: All tests roll ten-sided dice (d10). Any die showing 8 or higher (8, 9, 10) scores a Hit ().

04.2 The Three Test Frameworks

  • Simple Test: Used for binary pass/fail situations (catching a tether, spotting an ambush, resisting toxins). Roll d10s equal to the higher Attribute in the pair. If at least one die rolls 8+, the action succeeds; if no die rolls 8+, it fails.
  • Complex Test: The standard framework for combat attacks and high-stakes operations where you need to answer both "Did it connect?" and "How hard?":
    1. Action Roll: Roll d10s equal to the higher Attribute. You must score at least one Hit (8+) to succeed and advance.
    2. Success Roll: Take a number of dice from the roll equal to the lower Attribute. The highest single die among them represents your Success Points (SP) / Damage.
  • Success Test: Used when success is guaranteed, but time or effort determines the degree of impact (slicing a data-terminal). Roll d10s equal to the higher Attribute; the highest single die becomes your Success Points.

DICE STEPPING & GAMBITS

UPGRADES, DOWNGRADES, PROBABILITY & THE BANKED GAMBIT TRACK

05.1 Dice Stepping (Modifiers)

Rather than flat math modifiers, situational advantages and hazards alter the physical die type:

  • Upgrade (): Step one d10 up to a d12 per net Upgrade (boosting Hit chance to 41.7%). If all dice are d12s, each extra Upgrade adds an extra d10.
  • Downgrade (): Step one d10 down to a d8 per net Downgrade (only an 8 hits; 12.5% chance). If all are d8s, extra Downgrades remove dice. If all dice are removed, roll 2d8 and take the lower result.
  • Cancellation & Cap: Upgrades and Downgrades cancel 1-to-1, capped at a maximum of 6 Upgrades or 6 Downgrades.

05.2 The Canonical Gambit Rules

Gambit () is a precious strategic pool of banked dice allowing operatives to turn defeat into victory:

◈ EARNING & SPENDING GAMBIT DICE RULEBOOK PROTOCOL

Earning Gambit: Whenever you roll your dice pool for any test, if any die shows its maximum possible value (a 10 on a d10, or a 12 on a d12), you have a choice: use that die in your test as normal, or "bank" it. Banking removes the die from your current roll and places it into your personal Gambit pool (stored on your character track, up to a maximum of 4 dice).

Spending Gambit: You can spend a banked Gambit die on any test your character makes. You can declare you are spending Gambit at any point—before rolling, or after seeing the results to turn a failure into a success! When spent, roll the Gambit die and add its result into the pool. It retains its original die type even if the pool has Upgrades or Downgrades.

The Golden Rule of Gambit: A player can either spend or gain Gambit during a test, but not both. If you spend a Gambit die on a roll, you cannot bank any dice from that test; if you bank a die, you cannot spend Gambit on that roll.

ACTION ECONOMY & EFFECTS

THE 3 AP ROUND, CONTESTS & SACRIFICING HITS FOR BATTLEFIELD CONTROL

06.1 Action Economy: 3 AP

  • 3 AP Standard: Characters start each round with 3 Action Points (). Refills to max at round start.
  • 1 AP per Test: Executing any test costs 1 AP.
  • 2 AP Pacing Threshold: Turn control immediately swaps to the opposing side once the active team spends 2 or more AP.
  • Contesting an Action: Spend 1 AP out of turn to roll a Contest Roll (). Each Hit (8+) eliminates the attacker's highest die!
  • Teamwork: Allies spend 1 AP each, roll pools, and can swap any number of dice between pools!

06.2 Action Effects (Action Roll)

  • Upgrade Effect (): Spend 1 Hit to step up a die on subsequent rolls or immediately on your upcoming Success Roll (d10 ➔ d12).
  • Downgrade Effect (): Spend 1 Hit to impose an impending penalty on an enemy’s subsequent test (kicking their legs out, dazzling sensors, suppression), stepping down their die to a d8!
  • Blocker Effect (): Spend 2 Hits to set aside 1 lockout Hit that cancels the enemy's highest die.
  • Chain Effect (): Spend 1 Hit per additional eligible target within range.

06.3 Success Effects (Success Roll)

  • Crit Effect (): Spend Hits on your Success Roll to add +2 Success Points per Hit spent directly to your highest remaining die.
  • Push Effect (): Spend 1 Hit (requires Strength) to displace a target 4 meters in any direction. You may move along with them.

BREACHING VAULT 7

UNIFIED DEMONSTRATION OF AP, GAMBITS, TESTS & COMBAT EFFECTS

07.1 The Engagement Setup

Chartered Corsair Marshal Jax (Vanguard breacher) and Specialist Lyra (Weaver infiltrator) board an illicit syndicate scow in the Fume rings. An armored syndicate enforcer aims a heavy flechette shotgun down the pressurized corridor.

Round 1: Jax Initiates Breach (1 AP)

Jax spends 1 (2 AP remaining). He locks his mag-boots and levels his assault shotgun. The GM calls for a Finesse (4) + Strength (5) Complex Test. Jax rolls 5d10 for his Action Roll (higher stat):

Action Roll: [3, 5, 8, 9, 10]

Jax scores three Hits (8, 9, 10)! The 10 is a maximum roll. Jax declares he is banking the 10 into his personal Gambit pool!

With his remaining Hits (8 and 9), the attack connects. Jax keeps the 9 to ensure success, and sacrifices the extra Hit (8) on an Upgrade Effect () on his upcoming Success Roll.

Step 2: Success Roll & Crit Damage

Moving to his Success Roll for his lower stat (Finesse 4), Jax's Upgrade Effect steps one die up to a d12! Jax rolls 3d10 and 1d12: [4, 6, 7 on d10s; 9 on d12].

The 9 is a Hit! Jax sacrifices it for a Crit Effect (), adding +2 Success Points. His highest remaining die is 7. Total: 7 + 2 = 9 SP! Adding shotgun bonus +2, Jax deals 11 kinetic damage, cracking the enforcer's chest plate.

Round 1 (Cont.): Lyra's Downgrade (1 AP)

With 1 AP spent by Jax, Lyra spends 1 (2 AP remaining) to fire an EMP dazzler through the corridor, testing Knowledge (4) + Finesse (3). She rolls 4d10: [2, 7, 8, 9].

Lyra spends one Hit (8) to impose a Downgrade Effect () on the enforcer's optical sensors, stepping down his next shot to a d8! Having spent 2 AP, turn control passes to the syndicate.

The Enemy Attack & Contest (1 AP)

The enforcer fires his flechette gun back at Jax. Due to Lyra's Downgrade Effect, his lead die steps down from a d10 to a d8. Jax spends 1 (1 AP remaining) out of turn to roll a Contest Roll () using Agility (4): rolling 4d10. Jax rolls [3, 5, 8, 9]—scoring two Contest Hits!

Jax's two Contest Hits eliminate the enforcer's two highest dice. With no remaining 8+ dice in the enforcer's pool, the shot glances off Jax's riot shield!

Spending Banked Gambit to Crack the Door

An emergency lockdown seals the vault. Lyra plugs in her cyberdeck to bypass the door (requiring 1 Hit). She spends 1 AP and rolls [3, 5, 7]—a failure!

Lyra decides to spend a banked Gambit die from her character pool. She rolls the extra d10: rolling an 8 (Hit)! Thanks to the Gambit die, the lockdown clears and Vault 7 swings open.
